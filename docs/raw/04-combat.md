## Engine by reference

Run combat with Draw Steel’s rules for:

- **Starting combat and the round:** surprise, turn order, and taking turns.
- **Your turn:** main actions, maneuvers, move actions, free and triggered actions.
- **Movement:** speed, shifting, difficult terrain, flying and climbing, and forced movement (push, pull, slide) against stability.
- **Attacking:** strikes, abilities, distance and area, line of effect, cover and concealment, flanking, free strikes, and opportunity attacks.
- **Damage:** damage types, immunity, and weakness.
- **Staying up:** Stamina, winded, dying and death, Recoveries, and catching your breath.
- **Conditions:** bleeding, dazed, frightened, grabbed, prone, restrained, slowed, taunted, and weakened.
- **Heroic resources, surges, and Victories.**
- **Malice and the Director’s side** (`25`).

## Heroic resources

Each class fuels its abilities with its own heroic resource. Unless its class chapter says otherwise, a resource follows Draw Steel’s heroic-resource rules.

| Class | Resource | Notable rule (see class chapter) |
|---|---|---|
| Operator | Adrenaline | — |
| Scout | Advantage | Represents reading the target; surges |
| Commander / Face | Influence | Gained from combat and social Marks |
| Medic | **Reagents** | **Persist between encounters** — they don’t reset when combat ends or starts, and they’re spent even outside combat |
| Wrench | Uptime | Earned and drained by the machines you field |
| Elementalist | Essence | Sustained workings cost Essence each turn |
| Street Priest | Conviction | Per-turn drip; Light or Dark pact |
| Hacker | Bandwidth | Spent on Programs |
| Technomancer | Resonance | Sprites; biofeedback when overreaching |

**Cost check:** in combat, a runner can’t use a heroic ability that costs more of their resource than they currently have.

## Keywords added by Ghostwire

Ghostwire abilities use Draw Steel’s keywords plus:

| Keyword | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tech** | Powered by machinery, not magic or psionics. Cyborgs can use it. |
| **Chrome** | Powered by an implant. |
| **Optics** | Powered by an emitter, holo, or optical system. |
| **Wired** | Acts in the Wired (connection state rules apply). |
| **Command** | Driven by command presence: orders, rallies, reads. |

## Ghostwire combat rules

### Kits need their gear

A Kit’s bonuses and signature ability work only with an owned qualifying weapon and armor. Without them, you fight with an **improvised weapon**: class weapon abilities still work, but no Kit bonuses apply (`08`).

### Armor is Stamina

Worn armor raises maximum Stamina instead of reducing damage. The only true damage reduction is **typed immunity** from sealed and hardened gear (`08`).

### Damage types

Draw Steel’s damage types are used unchanged. Ghostwire gear also tags damage as **kinetic** (untyped), **AP**, **electrical**, **fire**, or **toxin**.

### Connection states in combat

- A **Jacked In** runner’s body is inert and can’t take real-world actions; allies usually guard it.
- An **Overlaid** runner has a bane on real-world Power Rolls.
- Biofeedback from the Wired deals damage to the runner’s Stamina (`21`).

### Chrome under fire

An implant can be **Suppressed** (temporarily offline), **Damaged** (works at a penalty until repaired), or **Destroyed** (benefit lost until repaired or replaced) (`09`).

### Peoples at 0 Stamina

- **Cyborgs** enter **System Crisis** instead of dying normally, and can’t be healed by Magic (`05`).
- **Revenants** become **inert** at the negative of their winded value instead of dying; fire damage while inert destroys them (`05`).

### Machines in combat

Drones and vehicles fight on the same grid. Anyone can run **one** drone with limits; Wrenches field fleets and can Jump In. Vehicle chases use a range track (`23`).
