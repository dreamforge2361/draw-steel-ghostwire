# How to Play

**RAW status:** draft  
**Sources:** `docs/rulebook/00-STAGE1-skeleton.md` (Chapter 1 scope), `docs/rulebook/DS-ALIGNMENT.md`  
**Engine:** Draw Steel Heroes — use the official rules for playing the game and the Director’s role; Ghostwire remaps below.

---

## The table

One player is the **Director**. Everyone else plays a **runner** — a hero in a **crew** of professionals who take dangerous jobs for pay. The Director describes the world, plays everyone the crew meets, and runs the opposition. Players decide what their runners do.

## The shape of play

Ghostwire play moves between three modes. Each uses Draw Steel’s rules, with Ghostwire additions noted in later chapters.

| Mode | What happens | Ghostwire additions |
|---|---|---|
| **Legwork** | Getting the job, talking, investigating, sneaking, preparing | Negotiation with patrons and fixers; the Wired for information (`21`); Availability when buying gear (`08`) |
| **The run** | The operation: infiltration, combat, chases, Wired intrusion | Connection states and Trace Alert (`21`); machines and chases (`23`); chrome failures (`09`) |
| **Downtime** | Recovering, spending ¥, installing chrome and mods, training | ¥ spending and lifestyle (`08`); §Craft Projects (`10`); chrome surgery (`09`) |

## Dice

Ghostwire uses Draw Steel’s dice. Most rolls are **Power Rolls** on two ten-sided dice (`03`).

## Ghostwire’s additions at a glance

- **Money is real.** Runners track ¥ and buy the gear their Kits need (`08`).
- **Chrome costs flesh.** Living runners spend Body Integrity on implants (`09`).
- **The Wired is a second battlefield.** Every runner can connect; Hackers and Technomancers live there (`21`).
- **Magic is rare and costly.** Veil casters fight chrome and Cyborgs can’t cast (`22`).
- **Machines are allies.** Anyone can run one drone; Wrenches run fleets (`23`).
