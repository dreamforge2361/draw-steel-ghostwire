## What a perk is

A perk is a trick of the trade: something you picked up on the job that has nothing to do with your class. A perk isn’t chrome, gear, or doctrine. It costs no ¥ and no Body Integrity. It’s training and experience, the way a Kit is.

Most perks work outside combat: talking your way past a checkpoint, jury-rigging a dead lift, knowing which fixer has the answer. A few give you a small maneuver or triggered action.

## The six perk types

Every perk has one type. Five types line up with how Ghostwire skills get used; the sixth is the weird stuff.

| Perk type | What it covers | Skills it leans on |
|---|---|---|
| **Crafting** | Building, modding, repairing, projects | Technical skills (Repair, Electronics, Engineering, Cybertech, …) |
| **Exploration** | Moving through the Reach, physical feats, montage work | Action skills (Athletics, Acrobatics, Perception, Survival, …) |
| **Interpersonal** | Talking, reading, and moving people | Social skills (Persuasion, Deception, Insight, Performance, …) |
| **Intrigue** | Stealth, disguise, cuffs, countermeasures, the street network | Stealth, Deception, Security Systems, Perception, Demolitions |
| **Lore** | Knowing things and finding out | Knowledge skills (Streetwise, Corporate, History, Matrix Theory, …) |
| **Supernatural** | Veil, Signal, and Wired-adjacent street weird | No skill needed |

Supernatural perks aren’t spellcasting. They’re small, strange gifts that show up on the street: a Veil-spirit living in an alley rat, a one-way whisper through the Signal, a flash of the emotion stuck to a murder weapon. Any hero can take one, but some tables will want a reason. A Cyborg can’t use a perk ability with the Magic keyword (Veil Trick) under Arcane Severance.

## When you gain perks

Classes grant perks as they level. A grant either lists the perk types you can pick from or lets you pick any perk.

| Class | 2nd | 4th | 6th | 8th | 10th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Operator** | Crafting, exploration, or intrigue | Any | Crafting, exploration, or intrigue | Any | Crafting, exploration, or intrigue |
| **Scout** | Exploration, interpersonal, or intrigue | Any | Any | Any | Any |

Other classes list their perk grants in their own chapters. “Any” means any of the six types, lore and supernatural included.

## Test results in perk text

Perks use Ghostwire’s result wording: **low**, **middle**, and **high** result, the same bands as a power roll. “Improve the outcome by one result” means low becomes middle, or middle becomes high.

## Perk list

The 47 Ghostwire perks. A perk’s type decides which perk grants can pick it.

### Crafting perks

#### Crunch Shift

When you make a project roll using a Technical skill, you can spend a hero token to make another project roll for the same project as part of the same respite activity. You can’t use this perk more than once per respite.

#### Jury-Rig

Without needing to make a test — and even without tools — you can quickly jury-rig or patch a mundane item or piece of equipment related to a Technical skill you have. The fix lasts for 1 hour or works for one use or activation (whichever comes first, as the Director determines), then fails beyond repair. For example, with Repair you could bridge a dead lift’s power long enough for your crew to ride it up, and with Electronics you could splice a burner commlink out of scrap that works for 1 hour.

#### Master Tech

Whenever you make a test as part of a crafting or research project that uses a Technical skill you already have, you can make the power roll twice and use either roll.

#### Mobile Workshop

On any day when you don’t take a respite, you can spend 1 uninterrupted hour working on a crafting project using a Technical skill you have — in the back of the van, on a safehouse floor, wherever. If you do so, you gain 1d10 project points toward that project.

#### Spec Sheet

Choose one Technical skill you already have. Whenever you obtain a low result on an easy or medium test using this skill, you treat it as a middle result instead. Additionally, if you spend 1 minute inspecting a device, weapon, vehicle, or piece of chrome related to the chosen skill, you can estimate its street value in ¥ and learn of any flaws in its construction — knockoff parts, bad welds, a hidden backdoor.

#### Tape and Prayer

Whenever you make a test to build, mod, or repair something and don’t have a skill that applies to the test, you gain a +1 bonus to the power roll.

### Exploration perks

#### Boost Up

As a maneuver, you grab a willing adjacent ally or object of your size or smaller, then vertical push that target up to a number of squares equal to twice your Physique score — up a fire escape, onto a rooftop, over a fence. If a creature you push falls as a result of this movement, the effective distance of the fall is reduced by a number of squares equal to twice your Physique score. When you use this perk, you can’t use it again until you earn 1 or more Victories.

#### Concrete Wise

When you make a test using a skill from the Action skill group and at least one of the d10s rolled is a 1, you can reroll one d10. You can use this perk only once per test.

#### Crew Work

When you take your first turn during any montage test, you can both make a test and assist another hero’s test.

#### I’ve Got You

Whenever a willing ally falls and would land on you or adjacent to you, you can safely catch them as a free triggered action. Neither of you takes damage from the ally’s fall.

#### Point Runner

At the start of a group test or montage test, you can spend a hero token. If you do, all participants make tests as if they also had any skill you have from the Action skill group.

#### Push the Meat

Whenever you fail a Physique test, you can lose Stamina equal to 1d6 + your level to improve the outcome of the test by one result (low to middle, or middle to high). You can use this perk only once per test.

#### Put Your Back Into It!

During montage tests, whenever you make a test to assist a test and obtain a low result, the assisted test doesn’t take a bane. Additionally, once per montage test, you can turn an ally’s low result into a middle result.

#### Stray Whisperer

You can use the Survival skill to interact with nonsapient creatures who aren’t animals — autopilot drones, feral lab-grown vermin, Veil-touched beasts in the Sinks.

#### Street Sense

Whenever you are in the undercity or an unsecured stratum of the Reach (but not inside a secured arcology or enclave), you gain an edge on tests made using the Perception skill, and you can’t be surprised. Additionally, you feel it in your gut when a disaster is coming within the next 72 hours — a structural collapse, a grid blackout, a gang war boiling over — though you don’t know exactly what it will be.

#### Urban Camouflage

Whenever you are in the Reach’s abandoned or lawless strata — the Warrens, the Sinks, dead industrial levels, collapsed transit — once you are hidden from a creature, you don’t need cover or concealment to stay hidden from them.

### Interpersonal perks

#### Bullshit Detector

In response to another creature communicating information to you, you can spend a hero token to determine whether that information contained any knowing lies. If so, you know what the lies are, but not what the truth is.

#### Frequency Match

You can make a Persona test using the Performance skill to influence creatures who don’t have emotions or can’t understand you — a security AI, a guard drone, a burned-out augment. Additionally, once during a negotiation when an ally makes an argument, you can drop the right track or the right rhythm to give that ally an edge on their test.

#### Headliner

Whenever a creature watches you sing, dance, play a set, or perform a role (as an actor, not just in disguise) for 1 uninterrupted minute or more — live or on a stream — you gain an edge on any test made to influence that creature for 1 hour after the performance ends.

#### Muscle Talks

Whenever you make a test that uses the Performance, Persuasion, or Intimidation skill to posture, flirt, or threaten, you can use Physique instead of any other characteristic the test calls for.

#### Off the Record

Whenever you speak one-on-one with a creature, you can ask them one question about themself that might typically offend them or raise suspicion. If they choose not to answer honestly, they simply deflect or redirect the question, with no further complications. If they choose to answer honestly, the creature can immediately ask you a question about yourself in turn, which you must answer honestly.

#### Pardon My Friend

When an ally within 5 squares fails a Persona test, you can step in and make a Persona test that takes a bane, with your roll replacing the ally’s roll. This perk can be used only once per test, even if more than one character has it.

#### Silver Tongue

If you fail a test using the Deception skill, you don’t suffer any consequences associated with the failure. Additionally, during a negotiation, you can be caught in one lie without negative consequences. When you use either benefit of this perk, you can’t use this perk again until you earn 1 or more Victories.

#### So Tell Me …

Whenever you succeed on a Persona test to influence one or more creatures, you can ask one creature you influenced a follow-up question after the test resolves, which they must answer honestly. At the Director’s discretion, the creature doesn’t have to answer the question completely — or at all — if the response would put them or someone they love in danger.

#### Soapbox

Whenever you are not in combat, you can shout to get the attention of hearing creatures within 10 squares of you. Each such creature who is not hostile toward you listens to what you have to say for 1 uninterrupted minute or more, or until they sense danger or any form of imminent harm. While creatures are listening to you, each of your allies gains an edge on tests made to avoid being noticed by those creatures.

#### Spot the Tell

Whenever you make a test to read a person and obtain a high result, you notice several tells that give away their true feelings — a pupil flutter, a micro-twitch in a cyber-jaw, a heartbeat spike. Any test you make to read that person in the future gains an edge.

### Intrigue perks

#### Forgettable Face

If you spend 10 minutes or less interacting with a creature who hasn’t met you before, you can cause them to forget your face when you part. If asked to describe you, the creature gives only a vague, blank, and unhelpful description — and street cams somehow never get a clean frame.

Additionally, if you spend 1 hour or more assembling a disguise, you automatically obtain a middle result on any test to pass as someone else. If you have the Deception skill, you automatically obtain a high result on the test.

#### Gum Up the Works

Whenever a mundane trap or security countermeasure activates within 3 squares — a tripwire, a pressure plate, a sentry-turret trigger — you can use a triggered action to move up to 3 squares toward it. If this movement brings you adjacent to any of its mechanisms, you can jam it, preventing it from activating. As long as you stay adjacent to the mechanism, it can’t go off unless an attempt to disarm it fails.

#### Lucky Dog

Whenever you fail a test using an intrigue skill (Stealth, Deception, Security Systems, Perception, or Demolitions), you can lose Stamina equal to 1d6 + your level to improve the outcome of the test by one result. You can use this perk only once per test.

#### Quick Change

You can don or remove a disguise as part of any test you make using the Stealth skill, or while using the Hide maneuver.

#### Shadow Network

You have a network of fixers, fences, and street contacts. As a respite activity while you take a respite in a district or enclave, you can ask your contacts a question by making a Persona test. On a middle result, you learn one piece of information that would be common on the street — the back ways into a building, where a wanted runner is holed up, the name of the local gang boss, and so forth. On a high result, you can instead learn something uncommon on the street, as long as such information exists — the location of a hidden stash, the gun used in an executive’s assassination, the fixer secretly bankrolling a local hit crew, and so forth.

#### Slipped Cuffs

You gain an edge on tests made to escape restraints. Given 1 uninterrupted minute, you can escape any mundane restraints — zip-cuffs, mag-cuffs, duct tape — without making a test. Additionally, it’s not immediately obvious when you’ve escaped restraints until you do something that makes it clear you have done so (cast them off, use an ability that harms one or more creatures, and so forth).

### Lore perks

#### But I Know Who Does

Whenever you fail a test to recall lore using a skill from the Knowledge skill group, you instinctively recall the nearest place where the information you seek might be found — a data broker’s den, a corp archive, a sealed Wired node, a Veil-reader in the Sinks, or any other location of the Director’s determination. The Director can decide that certain lore can’t be revealed this way.

#### Data Miner

Whenever you make a test as part of a crafting or research project using a skill from the Knowledge skill group, you can make the power roll twice and use either roll.

#### Eidetic Memory

Your mind is a data vault, though not always a well-indexed one. When you finish a respite, choose one skill from the Knowledge skill group that you don’t have. You have that skill until you finish your next respite. Additionally, if you spend 1 uninterrupted minute or more reading any page of text or screen of data, you can memorize its contents, allowing you to memorize entire manuals with sufficient time.

#### I’ve Seen the Feeds

Each time you enter a district, arcology, or enclave you’ve never been to before, you can ask the Director one of the following questions:
- Who is the most influential public figure here?
- Who here would be the friendliest to us right now?
- What does this place need most from outsiders?If the Director doesn’t have an answer to the question you ask, or doesn’t want to answer, you can instead ask a different question.

#### Polyglot

You automatically learn two new languages, as long as you have regularly heard those languages spoken or seen them written before. Additionally, if you spend 7 days or more in a place where you regularly hear or read a language you don’t know — a street market, a corp satellite office, an enclave — you can pick up enough of it to hold a conversation or understand basic written information. Having picked up a language this way, you can subsequently learn it using the Learn New Language research project at half the usual project goal cost.

#### Remote Research

On any day when you don’t take a respite, you can spend 1 uninterrupted hour working on a research project using a skill you have from the Knowledge skill group — on a slate, in a data café, on a transit ride. If you do so, you gain 1d10 project points toward that project.

#### Specialist

You are a leading expert on a particular subject. Choose one skill you have from the Knowledge skill group. You always have a double edge on tests made to recall lore using this skill. Additionally, your expertise gives you a name in related circles. You treat your Renown as 1 higher when negotiating with an NPC who knows your reputation, or 2 higher if they have the same skill you chose for this perk.

#### Wiki Brain

Whenever you make a test to recall lore and don’t have a skill that applies to the test, you gain a +1 bonus to the power roll.

### Supernatural perks

#### Echo Reader

When you hold an object in your hand for 1 uninterrupted minute, you can sense whether it carries an emotional echo in the Signal. Objects with an echo could include treasured gifts, murder weapons, or personal keepsakes. If the Director determines that the object carries an echo, you learn the most dominant emotion attached to it, then receive a flash that answers one of the following questions:
- What was the name of the person whose emotion is imprinted on this object?
- Why does this emotion linger on the object?
- How long has it been since the object was held by the person whose emotion lingers on it?After asking one question, you can choose to dig deeper by asking one additional question from the list, but you are then overcome with emotions that aren’t yours. You take a bane on Instinct and Persona tests until you finish a respite, and you can’t use this perk again while you suffer this bane.

#### Ghost Touch

You nudge a tiny object through the Signal.

#### Signal Read

As a maneuver, choose a creature within 10 squares and read its Signal. If that creature is your level or lower, you learn the keywords in their stat block (Humanoid, Construct, Undead, and so forth).

#### Street Blessing

You can spend 1 uninterrupted minute to perform a Veil ritual of blessing — chalk sigils, a lit candle, a whispered name — targeting yourself or one willing creature you touch. The target has a double edge on the next test they make within the next minute. A target can’t use this benefit on an activity that takes longer than 1 minute.

#### Veil Familiar

A small Veil-spirit has taken up residence in a specific small animal or scrap object — an alley rat, a rooftop pigeon, a broken toy drone — and chosen to be your familiar, or adopted you as theirs.

The spirit uses Draw Steel’s **familiar** stat block.

The familiar can hold small objects in their mouth, claws, or grabber, but can’t perform activities that would typically require hands (opening a door, working a keypad, and so forth). They can’t harm other creatures or objects. They can flank in combat, but only with you.

If your familiar is destroyed, you can restore them as a respite activity, or by spending a Recovery as a main action to call them back into an unoccupied space adjacent to you.

#### Veil Trick

You pull a small, showy working through the Veil.

#### Wire Whisper

You send a one-way message through the Signal straight into a friend’s head.

## Perk abilities

Three supernatural perks grant an ability.

### Ghost Touch

*A Signal flex — you nudge something small without laying a hand on it.*

**Psionic, Ranged** · Maneuver · Ranged 10 · One object

You can grab or manipulate the target object through the Signal, moving it up to a number of squares equal to your Logic, Instinct, or Persona score (your choice). You can use this ability to turn a handle, flip a breaker, tap a keypad, and so forth. You can manipulate any small movable piece of a larger object as long as the piece is unattended and size 1T. You can’t use this ability to break a smaller piece off a larger object.

### Veil Trick

*You pull a small, showy working through the Veil — the kind of street magic that buys a round of drinks.*

**Magic** · Main action · Self · Self

Choose one of the following effects:
- You blink a size 1S or smaller object adjacent to you into an unoccupied space adjacent to you.
- Until the start of your next turn, a part of your body throws a shower of harmless, crackling Veil-sparks that light up each square adjacent to you.
- You kill or wake (your choice) every mundane light source of size 1L or smaller adjacent to you — a streetlamp, a neon tube, a lighter.
- You make up to 1 pound of food you touch taste delicious or disgusting. Vat-protein counts.
- Until the start of your next turn, your body gives off a particular smell you’ve smelled before. Each creature within 5 squares of you can sense it, but it can’t impose any condition or other drawback on them.
- You leave a small glowing Veil-tag on the surface of a mundane object you touch, or you erase a tag made by you or by another creature using Veil Trick.
- You touch a size 1T object and glamour it to look like a different object. Any creature who handles the object becomes aware of the glamour. It ends when you stop touching the object.

### Wire Whisper

*You drop a one-way message straight into a friend’s head — no commlink, no trace on the Wired.*

**Psionic, Ranged** · Maneuver · Ranged 10 · One ally

As long as the target understands one or more languages, you send them a message through the Signal that takes 10 seconds or less to speak. It arrives like a voice on a private channel. The target knows who the message is from and can decide to ignore it and subsequent messages.
