# Switchboard

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // Upper Underlevels*

## The Switchboard

*Cassavir's transit exchange — the on-ramp*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You come up the dead escalator into a cathedral of stopped machinery. The Switchboard was a rail junction once — a hall where a hundred lines crossed — and you can still read it in the bones: the great arrivals board frozen mid-flip, the signal-gantries overhead strung now with drying laundry and fairy-strings of salvaged lumen-wire. The trains are a thousand years gone. What moves through here now is people, and deals.

There is no sky. There is never a sky, this low — only the underside of the level above, close enough to feel, sweating that warm chemical rain down through the gantry-grates so the whole hall shines wet. But there is room here, and that is rare and precious in the Flats: the old train-hall vaults up four stories of open air, and every meter of it is trafficked. Courier-drones thread the upper dark in blinking files. A battered hovercab idles on its cushion by the old platform edge, driver asleep. Down on the deck, ground-rigs and cargo-crawlers nose through a crowd that never fully clears, and somewhere a rig-runner is revving an engine just to be heard. The noise is a single continuous thing — voices in six tongues, the sub-bass thrum of the foundation-pumps far below, the drone-whine, the hiss of the rain on hot metal — and under all of it, if you still your breath, the deep slow heartbeat of the pumps that have kept this tower alive for a thousand years.

At the center of it, where every sightline seems to bend, an old woman sits in a booth of dead switchgear like a spider at the center of a web she rewired herself. She has been watching you since before you saw her.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Cassavir** — the fixer the district is named for. She *appears* elderly and unarmed in a booth of dead switchgear; a good cosmetician and **soft chrome** sell the grandmother. Underneath she is a **datajacked cyborg Controller** — decked (Switchboard Deck), private overlay-cell on the hall, Seize Control and Lockout on nodes and people. Not street muscle and not a mage: backup is **implant weaponry**, not a gun on the table. She opens most sessions and can hand the crew any of the Twelve's hooks — except an AEQ retainer; the Mandate does not sell to a fixer. Severing her would cut a hundred quiet deals at once, so no one does.

**Tover "Two-Line" Vask** — Cassavir's floor-runner, a Corran guild-clan kid who carries messages she won't trust to the Wired. Reads every face in the hall.

**The Sleeper** — the hovercab driver who is never actually asleep; a Nyx set of eyes on Cassavir's ground, and everyone pretends not to know it.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — semi-lawless. No corp presence; Cassavir's own tolerated order.

Cassavir *appears* elderly and unarmed — no visible guns on the booth. She is a cyborg Controller: the hall is under a dozen quiet guarantees, and her people plus the overlay-cell are already in position. Draw a weapon here and you make a hundred enemies at once. Effective **Alert** response is social first, then Wire: the room turns cold, deals evaporate, doors close across the district, and she can Seize Control of the hall's nodes. If it comes to a body, backup is implant weaponry — not street muscle.

Two "porters" (Ironclad-trained, off the books) handle anyone who forgets the etiquette. They escalate slowly and never first.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Dense but edited. Cassavir runs a **private overlay-cell** from the Switchboard Deck: SIN-readers at the escalator log every face, but the public AR is scrubbed of corp advertising — a rare quiet in the visual noise, and a signal that you've entered her jurisdiction. A Controller's hall: she holds the nodes; Lockout punishes anyone who tries to take them back.

A hacker who pushes past the surface finds the hall's true architecture: hundreds of encrypted deal-threads knotting through Cassavir's booth. Reading them is a major score and a fast way to become a loose end.

Dead-drop tags float over three specific pillars — anonymous job-postings only visible to those given the key.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The On-Ramp. Cassavir has a job — she always has a job. The clean version came down from the Spires; the dirty version came up from Nyx's Churn. She'll let the crew choose which end they work, and the choice has teeth.

2. The Loose Thread. Someone has been reading Cassavir's deal-threads. She hires the crew off the books, from herself, to find the leak — not knowing (or not saying) it leads to the Sleeper, and through him to Nyx.

3. The Severance. A conglomerate finally decides the Switchboard is worth taking. If Cassavir falls, the crew loses their on-ramp — and the whole district's economy convulses. A campaign-turning event, seeded here.

## The Melt Market

*Grey-tech bazaar under the pink arterial*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The pink arterial is what everyone calls the old elevated freight-viaduct that shoulders its way across three blocks overhead — a rusted artery of girders and dead signal-lamps, lit now from beneath by a hundred stalls' worth of scavenged pink and magenta signage until the whole underside glows like something dissected under a surgical lamp. Rain finds every gap in the viaduct plating and comes down in threads and sheets, hissing where it lands on hot solder and cook-plates, and the vendors have long since given up fighting it — every stall runs a ragged tarp-roof stitched from a dozen different scavenged materials, and the runoff channels between the tables are ankle-deep by second watch.

Above the viaduct's far edge you can just make the traffic that never stops: a cargo hovercraft grinding along the freight-lane cut into the level above, courier-drones darting under it like fish beneath a whale, and lower still, weaving between stalls at street level, boosted three-wheel rigs whose riders never quite slow down for the crowd. The air is thick with ozone and burnt flux and someone's fry-oil, and every third stall is shouting its wares in a different tongue over music that doesn't match the stall beside it — a wall of sound that only breaks when a deal actually happens and, for one beat, everyone nearby goes quiet to listen.

Tables here are stacked with things that shouldn't work and mostly do: cracked cyberdecks re-flashed with grey firmware, chrome limbs missing their calibration docs, biomonitor rigs with the Caduceus serials filed off. Nothing here has papers. That's the entire point.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ilsa Corrigan "Solder-Ma"** — matriarch of the Market's oldest stall-row, a Corran guild-clan elder who vets every new vendor personally and remembers every debt owed her going back thirty years.

**Whit** — a jittery Technomancer barely out of his teens who trades flashed decks for favors and information; he hears things in the Wired that make him talk to himself, and half of it is true.

**Deacon Rael** — an Ironclad deserter turned enforcer-for-hire, works the Market's "insurance" on a rotating retainer; polite until he isn't.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — contested, loosely held by Corran stall-families with Nyx tolerance (a cut of every sale flows upstream to the Churn).

No single authority; disputes are settled stall-to-stall or escalate to Solder-Ma's informal court. Weapons drawn openly bring every nearby stall-owner's hired muscle in fast — the Market protects its commerce first, its people second.

Alert here means stalls physically collapse and vanish in minutes; grey-tech vendors are built for a fast strike-and-scatter when Sanctum audit-drones or Ferrum enforcers sweep the viaduct.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public overlay shows a defunct freight-market with a stale "condemned" tag Ferrum never bothered updating — deliberately, since it keeps casual traffic (and casual patrols) away.

Underneath, the Market runs on a barter-mesh: item listings broadcast peer-to-peer on unlicensed frequencies, invisible unless your deck knows the Market's rolling cipher, which changes weekly and is sold, itself, as grey-tech.

Whit maintains a shadow ledger in the Wired tracking which chrome sold here has "called home" to its original corp registry — a list worth killing over.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Bad Chrome. A batch of grafted limbs sold last week are phoning home to Caduceus on a hidden channel. The buyers are starting to disappear. Someone needs to find out who's harvesting the data — and why.

2. Solder-Ma's Favor. Ilsa calls in an old debt: escort a shipment of "clean" grey-tech through Ash Tenements before a Nyx quota deadline, no questions about what's really in the crates.

3. The Filed Serial. A piece of gear the crew is examining turns out to be stolen Ironclad combat-frame tech — meaning Ironclad's retrieval contractors are already on their way to the Market, and they don't shop politely.

## Grafthouse Walk

*Back-alley ripperdocs; Caduceus deniable*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The alley narrows as you go in, buildings leaning closer overhead until the gap between them is barely a slot, and what little open air remains is choked with sagging cable and old medical-cross signage flickering green through the rain. There is no sky here — there never is — just the underbelly of the level above, pipes sweating condensation that drips warm and faintly antiseptic-smelling onto the cracked pavement, mixing with the chemical rain until you can't tell which is which. Somewhere above the roofline a hovercraft ambulance eases along the service-lane cut into the next level up, running dark, no siren, because Grafthouse Walk doesn't advertise its business to anyone who isn't already bleeding.

Green light is the throughline: green surgical-glow spilling from shopfront windows, green neon crosses buzzing over a dozen doorways, green biohazard-strip warnings peeling off half the walls. Under it, the street sounds different than the rest of the Flats — quieter, punctuated by the whine of bone-saws and the hiss of anesthetic canisters, voices pitched low, the wet mechanical clatter of surgical drones being wheeled between clinics on hand-carts because the alley's too narrow for anything bigger. A cargo-crawler idles at the mouth of the walk, engine ticking, because the driver knows better than to bring it any further in.

Every door here promises the same thing in different words: we can fix that, no questions, cash only. Some of them mean it.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Dr. Yuen Marrick** — once licensed, now deniable; runs the Walk's best-regarded table, keeps a genuine Caduceus supply line open through a cousin still on the books upstairs. Charges fairly, refuses no one who's dying, remembers everyone who's lied to her.

**Coz** — a self-taught grafter with more confidence than training; cheap, fast, and the reason half the Walk's ghost stories exist. Some Edgerunners swear by him anyway.

**Sella Farrow** — Caduceus's deniable liaison, disguised as a courier; she quietly resupplies the legitimate clinics and quietly reports back on anything unusual — like SIN-less patients with corp-grade chrome already installed.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — a fragile neutral ground; no gang claims it outright because everyone eventually needs a table here.

Violence inside a clinic is nearly unheard of — even rival gang members observe an unspoken truce on the table, though the alley outside offers no such courtesy.

Alert response is medical-professional, not martial: bad behavior gets you blacklisted from every door on the Walk, a punishment more feared than a beating, since a blacklisted Edgerunner bleeds out looking for the next clinic over.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer shows a cluster of unlicensed medical services with generic warning tags — Sanctum's actuarial crawlers rate the whole street "uninsurable," which is the closest thing to a compliment the Walk gets.

Dig deeper and a hacker finds real supply-chain manifests moving through Sella's courier account — proof Caduceus is running deniable medicine into the Flats it officially disavows.

Several clinics keep patient chrome-registries entirely offline, on physical drives, specifically so the Wired can never subpoena them.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Ghost Patient. A body on Marrick's table has cyberware that doesn't match any known manufacturer — grey-tech, or something worse, wired into a person who insists they never installed it.

2. Supply Run. Caduceus's real shipment is late and patients are dying without anesthetic. Sella needs deniable muscle to escort the next batch through territory Nyx has started taxing.

3. Coz's Mistake. A botched graft has left someone with a chrome limb slaved to a remote kill-switch neither Coz nor his client understands — and someone on the Spires does.

## The Green Room

*Street-faith clinic & soup line*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You smell it before you see it — broth and scorched grain and wet wool — cutting through the ever-present ozone-and-rain reek of the Flats like a hand on the shoulder. The Green Room isn't green; it's the color of the old emergency-signage paint that still clings to its walls, faded from decades under a roofline that leaks steadily, that same chemical rain finding every seam in the level above and dripping into buckets set out with the patience of people who've done this every night for years. There's no sky above the queue that winds around the block — only girders, ductwork, and the underside of the level above, strung with sodium-gold lumen-strings someone hung up years ago to make the wait less grim.

The queue itself tells you where you are faster than any sign: SIN-less exiles, Cinderhold refugees with the burn-scars to prove it, tired Corran dock-crews coming off shift, a couple of kids nobody's claiming. Overhead, a lattice of cargo-drones on the level-above freight lane throws intermittent shadow-strobes down through the grating, and at street level a beat-up ground-rig idles at the curb, engine running for the heat as much as the fuel — someone's been sleeping in it for weeks. Voices here are hushed compared to the rest of the district, the six-tongue roar of the Flats muted to murmurs and the occasional crying child, the sub-bass foundation-pump thrum still there underneath everything like it always is, patient as a heartbeat.

Inside, warmth, low light, and volunteers moving fast — this is the only place in Switchboard where nobody asks what you did to get here.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Brother Amsel** — a street-faith priest in patched vestments, formerly a Caduceus field medic before he "found the Lamp"; runs triage himself when the line gets bad, and never once asks for payment.

**Nadia Okonkwo-Reyes** — a Corran guild-clan volunteer who coordinates supply runs and quietly recruits capable hands for jobs that keep the soup line funded — some of them legitimate.

**Whisper** — a Wereraven who roosts in the Green Room's rafters more nights than not, watching the queue for faces the Wired would like to know about, and warning Amsel first.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — sanctuary ground, tolerated by every faction as unclaimed and unclaimable; even Nyx enforcers stand down at the door.

The Green Room has no guards because it needs none — an unwritten rule of the Flats holds that violence here brings down consequences from every faith-adjacent network in the district, Corran clans included.

The one real threat is corp indifference, not gang violence: Sanctum periodically "assesses" the block for demolition, and Ferrum owns the paper on the building.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public overlay barely registers the Green Room at all — no ads, no ratings, just a faint community-tag left by grateful former patrons, easy to miss in the noise.

A closer look reveals Whisper's data-nest: an informal, encrypted bulletin board of who's missing, who's looking, who needs found — the closest thing the Flats has to a public registry of its own people.

Ferrum's property-management systems quietly flag the block as "pending reallocation" — a demolition order nobody's told Amsel about yet.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Pending Reallocation. Ferrum's paperwork says the Green Room's building is scheduled for clearance next quarter. Amsel needs the crew to find out who signed the order — and whether it can be stopped, bought off, or has to be fought.

2. Whisper's Warning. A face came through the soup line that Whisper recognizes from a Meridian surveillance bulletin — a fugitive, or a plant. Amsel wants the crew to find out which before it burns the sanctuary's neutrality.

3. The Missing Volunteer. Nadia hasn't come back from a supply run into Ash Tenements. The Green Room's people don't do violence — but they know people who do, and they're asking the crew, quietly, to go looking.

## Hollow Rail Stn.

*Dead platform; Nyx dead-drop nexus*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The platform drops away below street level, down a stairwell half-collapsed and never repaired, into a dark that the district's neon doesn't reach. This is the old rail line's other terminus — the Switchboard's dead twin — abandoned so long ago the tracks have gone to rust-lace and the destination boards still list stations that don't exist anymore. There is no sky, obviously; there's barely a ceiling, just the vaulted stone of the level above pressing down close and wet, chemical runoff sheeting down the tunnel-mouth in a curtain that catches what little light reaches this deep and turns it to smeared amber threads.

Even here, traffic finds a way — a single maintenance drone-lane threads the tunnel roof, running blacked-out courier-drones on schedules no public registry admits exist, and down on the platform itself a scavenged rail-cart, motor swapped for a scrap-shop repulsor unit, sits ready for a run nobody official sanctioned. Sound behaves strangely down here: the district's roar above is reduced to a distant hum, replaced by dripping water, the click of settling metal, and — if you know to listen for it — the soft electronic chirp of a dead-drop node waking up because something walked past its sensor.

Graffiti tags in a dozen competing hands cover every surface, half of them AR-only, invisible without the right lens — and every one of them means something to somebody, usually Nyx.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ket "Quiet Ket" Sorenna** — Nyx's dead-drop custodian for the station, spends most of her life sitting in the dark cataloguing packages she never opens; utterly unbothered by anything short of a direct threat to the drop network itself.

**Old Vann** — a scavenger who's lived in the station's back tunnels for a decade, knows every collapsed passage and half-buried service shaft; trades tunnel-knowledge for scrip or favors.

**The Courier Twins** — two Wereravens who use the tunnel roof as a flyway between districts, carrying data no one trusts to the open Wired.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Nyx-controlled, quietly and completely. No visible enforcement; the emptiness itself is the security.

Nyx doesn't patrol the station so much as own the silence around it — anyone lingering too long gets a visit from Quiet Ket or, if she's not satisfied, someone considerably less polite.

Alert here is nearly invisible: tripped sensors reroute the drop network overnight, and by the time anyone comes looking, the "evidence" is three districts away.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public overlay shows nothing — an abandoned rail asset with no active tags, deliberately dead space in a city that hates dead space.

Underneath, the tunnel is dense with Nyx dead-drop tags, AR-only graffiti-codes, and a rotating cipher that changes with every drop cycle — a hacker who cracks it gains a real-time map of Nyx's local logistics.

Meridian's surveillance mesh has a known gap here; whether that's incompetence or a bought blind spot is a live question among Signal's own auditors.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Gap in the Mesh. Someone at Meridian is selling the surveillance blind spot over the station to the highest bidder — and Nyx isn't the only party who wants it kept quiet.

2. Ket's Missing Package. A drop went uncollected for the first time in years, and Quiet Ket wants to know why before she reports it up the chain — because the answer might be that its intended recipient is already dead.

3. Tunnel Rats. Old Vann has found a passage that leads somewhere the Flats' maps don't show — deeper, older, and Nyx wants it explored before Ferrum's surveyors stumble onto it first.

## Lamp on Ninth

*Street-priest's block; sanctuary*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

A single lamp burns at the corner of Ninth, an old brass fixture nobody remembers installing, kept lit by hand because the block's power conduits died decades ago. It has become the district's unofficial compass — ask any Flats local for directions and they'll measure distance from the Lamp, not from anything the Wired maps. Overhead, as everywhere here, there is no sky, only the close wet underside of the level above, rain falling in that same warm chemical drizzle, but on this block it seems gentler somehow, or maybe it's just that the crowd moves slower, unhurried by the neon-hustle pressure of the rest of Switchboard.

Air traffic still crosses above — a cargo hovercraft grinds along its freight-corridor two levels up, drone-couriers blink past on their routes, indifferent to the sanctuary below — but at street level the ground-traffic thins to a crawl near the Lamp, ground-rigs idling respectfully rather than gunning through, because even hardened rig-runners know better than to make trouble on this block. The neon here leans warm — amber, gold, the color of the Lamp itself reflected back a hundred times in wet pavement — and the six-tongue murmur of the Flats softens into something closer to conversation, punctuated by the low chant of an evening vigil drifting from the chapel-front, and always, underneath, that patient sub-bass heartbeat of the foundation-pumps.

People kneel here who kneel nowhere else. That alone should tell you something about the ground you're standing on.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Yeva** — the street-priest who tends the Lamp itself, elderly, sharp-eyed, and the closest thing the Flats has to unimpeachable moral authority; Corran by blood, ordained by nobody but the people who trust her.

**Deacon Oskar Pell** — Yeva's second, younger and more willing to bend the faith's neutrality when he thinks the cause is just — a tension Yeva tolerates because she remembers being young too.

**The Vigil-Keepers** — a rotating cast of a dozen or so locals, Corran clan-kin and Cinderhold exiles alike, who keep watch through the night in shifts; more witnesses than guards, but that's often enough.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — sanctuary, recognized informally by every faction operating in Switchboard.

The Lamp's protection is reputational, not martial: harming anyone on this block, or breaking sanctuary during a vigil, marks you as an enemy of the street-faiths district-wide — a slow, grinding kind of consequence that outlasts any single beating.

Ironclad-trained muscle is conspicuously absent by design; the faith relies on the Vigil-Keepers' numbers and the district's collective memory instead.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer shows the Lamp on Ninth as a minor cultural landmark, oddly persistent given how little else in the Flats gets official recognition — some suspect a sympathetic soul inside Meridian keeps the tag alive on purpose.

Beneath it, Mother Yeva's network runs an old-fashioned, deliberately low-tech ledger of favors owed and given across the district — nothing stored on the Wired, everything remembered, which makes it both harder to hack and impossible to subpoena.

A quiet AR marker, visible only to the faith's own, tags the block as a no-fly zone for anything armed — a request, not an enforced law, but one almost everyone honors.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Sanctuary Broken. Someone violated the vigil last night — a body was left on the chapel steps as a message. Mother Yeva wants it solved quietly, before the district's faith in the sanctuary itself starts to crack.

2. Deacon Pell's Gambit. Oskar has been quietly running Edgerunners on "just causes" without Yeva's blessing, using the sanctuary's reputation as cover. If it comes out, it could burn the Lamp's neutrality to the ground.

3. The Ledger. A rival fixer wants Yeva's favor-ledger — not to publish it, but to know exactly who in the Flats owes whom, and use it to quietly reshape the district's balance of power.

## Fixer's Row

*Second-tier brokers & pawn dens*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Fixer's Row runs three blocks of storefronts crammed shoulder to shoulder under a low mezzanine overhang, every window crowded with hand-lettered signs advertising things half-legal and half-true. There's no sky above the Row — there's the mezzanine's underside, dripping that endless faint-chemical rain through gaps in the plating, catching pink and cyan and sodium-gold off two hundred competing signs until the wet street looks like a spilled paint tray lit from within. This is where the deals too small for Cassavir and too risky for the Melt Market's grey-tech crowd get done — pawned chrome, half-legit contracts, rumors sold by the word.

Overhead traffic never really stops: courier-drones weave between the Row's overhanging signs close enough to clip an unwary head, a battered aircar idles rooftop on a landing pad barely big enough for it, and at street level a line of hovercabs queues for fares while ground-rigs squeeze past them with inches to spare, horns blaring in three different registered languages. Every fixer on the Row has their own bark, their own patter, competing for attention in the wall of noise — voices, drone-whine, the hiss of rain on hot signage, and threaded through it all, faint but constant, the district's deep sub-bass heartbeat.

Everyone on the Row is selling something. The trick is figuring out what they're actually selling before you buy it.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Benno Task** — a second-tier fixer who's spent twenty years failing to become Cassavir and finally stopped trying; reliable for small jobs, resentful about it, good information despite himself.

**Marisol Quint** — runs the Row's busiest pawn den, buys chrome and contracts alike, and has a quiet side business laundering Nyx merchandise through legitimate-looking receipts.

**Pockets** — a teenage runner-for-hire who knows every fixer's schedule and grudge on the Row, and sells that knowledge to anyone who asks nicely, or pays.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — fractured, no single authority; each storefront enforces its own peace, and grudges between fixers occasionally spill into the street.

Violence here draws attention fast, but not protection — the Row's fixers are more likely to profit off a fight (taking bets, selling footage) than stop one, unless it's happening on their own doorstep.

Sanctum-linked "loss adjusters" occasionally sweep the Row auditing pawned goods for stolen corp property; their visits are the closest thing to organized law the block ever sees, and they're not popular.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer is saturated with competing ad-tags, fixer ratings, and pawn-den inventories, all fighting for the same sliver of attention — the densest advertising overlay in the district outside the Melt Market.

A hacker digging past the noise finds the Row's real currency: a gossip-mesh of encrypted reputation scores fixers keep on each other and on clients, quietly more accurate than any formal credit rating.

Sanctum's actuarial crawlers maintain a persistent, unwelcome presence here, scanning pawned goods against corp theft registries — most fixers keep at least one item in reserve specifically to "find" when a crawler comes calling.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Benno's Bad Bet. Task took a job he can't complete and needs the crew to finish it quietly before the client — a mid-level Ironclad contractor — decides an example needs making.

2. Hot Goods. Marisol unknowingly fenced a piece of chrome stolen from a Caduceus research contract. A "loss adjuster" is already asking questions, and whoever sold it to her wants it back before she can talk.

3. Pockets Knows Too Much. The kid has been selling information to one too many rival fixers, and now several of them want him silenced — permanently. He wants protection, and he's offering something valuable in trade: he knows where a body is buried, literally.

## The Dry Dock

*Chop-shop yards; boosted-rig fences*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Dry Dock got its name generations back, when this was a maintenance yard for the rail line's service craft, and the name stuck even after the last dock drained. Now it's a sprawl of corrugated chop-shop bays and stripped-down rig carcasses stacked three high behind a fence that's more suggestion than barrier. Above it, the level-above's underside sags lower here than anywhere else in Switchboard — old structural damage never repaired — so the chemical rain comes down harder and closer, drumming on scrap-metal roofing in a rhythm that never quite syncs with the sub-bass pump-thrum underneath.

The yard is loud with more than voices: cutting torches spitting blue-white sparks, hydraulic lifts groaning under stolen rig-chassis, and overhead, low and constant, boosted rigs and cargo-crawlers coming and going on a rotation the fences pretend is legitimate — while higher up, out past the sagging plating, a courier-drone lane and the occasional cautious hovercraft mark the boundary between "the Dock's business" and "everyone else's." Neon here is sparse and utilitarian, worklamps more than signage, sodium-gold pools swallowed fast by shadow between them, rain hissing where it hits hot metal and dying instantly.

Nobody asks where a rig came from at the Dry Dock. Everybody already knows, and everybody's fine with it.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Grissom "Torch" Ude** — runs the yard's biggest chop-bay, an ex-Ferrum dock-worker who took his trade underground after a labor dispute turned violent; fair dealer, terrifying when crossed.

**Lil Pryce** — the Dock's fastest fence, moves boosted rigs through three shell registrations before a real buyer ever sees them; connected loosely to Nyx's ground-logistics arm.

**Deke Marrow** — a rig-runner who tests every stolen vehicle personally before sale, and has therefore survived more crashes than anyone in the district has a right to.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Nyx-tolerant, Corran-staffed; an informal guild of chop-shop crews keeps order between themselves and splits protection duty.

The fence is patrolled loosely by hired watchers, more interested in preventing theft from the yard than policing what happens inside it; internal disputes are settled by the crew bosses, fast and physically.

Ferrum periodically sends "repossession" crews after specific stolen vehicles with traceable corp parts — these raids are the Dock's most dangerous recurring threat, and everyone drops what they're doing to help repel them.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public overlay tags the Dry Dock as a legitimate salvage-and-recycling operation — paperwork Lil Pryce maintains meticulously, because a clean-looking front is worth more than any lock.

Underneath, every rig in the yard carries a shadow VIN, swapped and re-swapped through a rotating registry that a skilled hacker can trace backward to figure out exactly which corp is missing which vehicle.

Ferrum's asset-tracking systems occasionally "ping" a chassis in the yard before the Dock's crews can scrub it — a race against the clock that's led to more than one shootout.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Hot VIN. A rig sold through Lil's chain turns out to belong to a Sanctum executive's personal fleet — meaning it has tracking hardware nobody found, and it's leading trouble straight back to the Dock.

2. Ferrum's Repo Squad. A high-value stolen cargo-crawler is due for a Ferrum repossession raid within the hour. Torch needs bodies to help move it out — or defend it — before the squad arrives.

3. Deke's Last Ride. Deke test-drove something he shouldn't have — a combat-frame rig with active Ironclad security countermeasures — and now it's driving itself back to whoever's tracking it, with him unconscious inside.

## Ash Tenements

*SIN-less warren housing; gang seams*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Tenements rise in ragged, uneven blocks, additions bolted onto additions for decades until no two floors of the same building quite line up, walkways strung between towers on cable and prayer. There's no sky above — the underside of the level above is lost entirely behind the tangle of jury-rigged awnings, cable runs, and drying laundry strung across every gap, so the chemical rain arrives already filtered through someone else's rooftop shelter before it reaches you, dripping brown-tinged and stale. Somewhere in the maze above, a hovercraft picks its way along a service corridor too narrow for anything larger, running lights dimmed to avoid drawing attention it doesn't want.

At street level the traffic is almost entirely on foot and rig — battered ground-vehicles idle at the mouths of alleys too tight to enter, engines running for warmth, while overburdened courier-drones dart low between the buildings because the wider lanes above are watched by factions that charge a toll for passage. Neon here is scavenged and inconsistent, half-dead signs flickering pink and cyan in colors their original owners never intended, and the six-tongue roar of the district fractures block by block into distinct dialects and distinct loyalties — this alley is Corran-kin, that one isn't, and the line between them is a lot more real than it looks.

The Tenements hold more people with no SIN at all than anywhere else in Switchboard — which means more people the Wired doesn't officially know exist, and more reasons for someone to come looking anyway.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Anwen "Stitch" Kade** — an unlicensed community organizer who's turned one tenement block into something close to a functioning cooperative; fiercely protective of her people, deeply suspicious of outsiders.

**Doskol Varn** — a minor gang boss carving out territory in the Tenements' eastern blocks, backed loosely by Cinderhold supply lines; ambitious, dangerous, not yet powerful enough to be a real problem — yet.

**Reeve** — a Corran guild-clan enforcer who keeps an uneasy truce between three rival blocks, more peacekeeper than gangster, though the distinction blurs under pressure.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — fractured warren; each block or cluster of blocks answers to a different informal authority, with no unified control.

Security is hyperlocal and personal — residents protect their own block fiercely and are indifferent or hostile to trouble a few streets over; outsiders are watched constantly and trusted slowly.

Alert here spreads by word of mouth and rooftop signal-lamps faster than any Wired notification — a stranger causing trouble is known on the far side of the warren within minutes, long before any official response could arrive.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public overlay is nearly blank — most residents are SIN-less by circumstance or choice, so the Wired's official map treats the Tenements as sparsely populated, wildly undercounting the people actually living there.

Beneath the surface, a resident-run mesh network (built and maintained quietly by a handful of self-taught Technomancers) provides informal identity, reputation, and safety information the official Wired refuses to track.

Meridian occasionally tries to map the warren more accurately for ad-targeting purposes; these survey attempts are deeply unwelcome and sometimes met with sabotage.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Doskol's Ambition. Varn is quietly buying weapons and muscle, positioning to take the whole eastern warren by force. Stitch wants him stopped before the fighting spills onto blocks that have stayed neutral for years.

2. The Census Drone. A Meridian survey drone got shot down over the Tenements last night, and now Signal wants answers — and access — that the residents are refusing to give. Someone needs to broker peace before Meridian sends something bigger.

3. No Name, No Number. A child has gone missing from Stitch's block — SIN-less, meaning no official registry will even acknowledge she exists. Stitch is calling in every favor she has to find her before whoever took her disappears into deeper districts.

## Signal Spike

*Meridian relay mast; watched ground*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Spike rises out of a fenced concrete plaza like something that landed rather than something that was built — a Meridian relay mast easily fifteen stories tall even hemmed in as it is by the level above, its transmission array vanishing up into the gap between levels where the "sky" ought to be, if there were one. There isn't, of course; there's only the underside of the next level up, and the mast's warning-strobes strike that ceiling of pipe and plating in slow red pulses that turn the endless chemical rain the color of old blood for half a second at a time before fading back to Meridian-blue.

Traffic worships this place without meaning to — courier-drones and Meridian relay-drones both use the Spike as a navigation beacon, circling it in lazy, overlapping patterns at three different altitudes, while a pair of Signal-liveried hovercraft hold a slow, permanent patrol loop around the plaza's perimeter. At ground level, ordinary traffic gives the fence a wide, unconscious berth — ground-rigs detour rather than pass close, and pedestrians walk a little faster here, a little quieter, like the mast itself is listening. Which it is.

The plaza's own sound is different from the rest of the Flats: less the six-tongue roar, more a low electronic murmur, relay-hum and cooling-fan whine layered under the ever-present sub-bass heartbeat, as if the noise itself has been filtered and catalogued before it's allowed to reach you.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Site Manager Corvin Ashe** — the Meridian technician officially responsible for the Spike's upkeep; competent, incurious by professional necessity, and quietly aware that asking too many questions about his own employer's data traffic is a fast way to lose the job — or worse.

**Pica** — a rogue Technomancer who's built a fragile, illicit foothold inside the Spike's peripheral systems, skimming data too minor for Meridian to notice — so far.

**Agent Rell Dovic** — a Meridian security officer who patrols the plaza on foot, memorizes faces obsessively, and has started noticing the same Edgerunners passing through more often than coincidence should allow.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — the one hard exception to the district's semi-lawlessness; this plaza is genuine, active Meridian-controlled ground.

Fenced, monitored, and patrolled around the clock — the closest thing to corp-grade security anywhere in Switchboard. Loitering draws attention within minutes; anything resembling sabotage draws an armed response fast.

Alert here escalates hard and immediately: Meridian treats any interference with the Spike as an attack on the district's entire data infrastructure, and responds with drones first, questions later.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Spike is, unsurprisingly, the single densest node of the Wired anywhere in Switchboard — a visible torrent of data-tags, relay traffic, and surveillance feeds converging on one physical point.

A hacker skilled enough to get past the Spike's defenses finds it isn't just relaying district traffic — it's quietly aggregating surveillance data from half of Switchboard's other POIs, including feeds Meridian was never authorized to pull.

Pica's foothold is small but real: a persistent backdoor that lets her skim minor data for resale, and a liability that could bring Meridian's full attention down on anyone associated with her if discovered.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Pica's Backdoor. Pica needs help expanding her foothold in the Spike's systems without tripping Meridian's internal audits — and what she finds while doing it turns out to be worth far more than she bargained for.

2. Unauthorized Aggregation. Evidence surfaces that the Spike is illegally pulling surveillance data from private clinics and sanctuaries across the district, the Green Room and Lamp on Ninth included. Someone wants it exposed — and someone else wants it buried.

3. Dovic's Suspicion. Agent Rell Dovic has started building a file on the crew from repeated plaza sightings alone. They need to either give him a reason to stop looking, or find leverage on him before his file reaches someone who acts on it.
