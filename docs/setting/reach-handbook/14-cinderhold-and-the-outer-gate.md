# Cinderhold & the Outer Gate

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // The Edge of the World*

## The Marrow Gate

*The one way out — and the wall that keeps the dark from coming in*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The gate is the biggest single made-thing you have ever stood beneath. After a lifetime under the hive's low ceilings, the Marrow Gate opens upward — a canyon punched clean through the Reach's forty-foot outer wall of black iron, poured concrete, and heavy industrial plate. This is no simple door: it's a **massive** **hydraulic blast-gate**, twin armored slabs driven by external pistons and rams the size of buildings, flanked by gatehouse control-towers bristling with watch-platforms, floodlights, and the long silhouettes of mounted guns pointed outward. And it runs on a **timer** — the higher-ups inside the hive decide the hours, and when the great pistons sigh and the slabs grind shut, they shut for everyone. This is the only place in the Reach where the wall's job is written plain: not to keep people in, but to keep something out.

There is sky here — the real, terrible open sky, the first most Flats-born ever see — and it does not comfort. It hangs vast and bruise-colored over the poisoned scrubland, and on the horizon the black wall of the jungle stands like a second, living wall, its huge trees lost in mist. The rain that falls here is cleaner than the hive's and somehow worse for it. Traffic through the gate is slow, checked, and watched: caravans queue in the checkpoint lanes, salvagers haul their wasteland finds in for inspection, and everyone — everyone — is logged by the hard-eyed militia in Ranger field-coats before the inner door will cycle.

Above the main blast-door, someone has welded three words in salvaged letters, and they are the first law of Cinderhold: **THE GATE CLOSES AT DARK.**

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ranger-Captain Cael Marrow** (human), "the Gate" — commands the gate and, through it, Cinderhold. Former Greenline Ranger, keeps a cold back-channel to Mama Cassavir inside the wall, and personally decides who passes. Fair, hard, and utterly unbribable on the one rule that matters: the gate closes at dark.

**Gate-Sergeant Odell Rusk** (Goliar) — Marrow's second, who runs the checkpoint lanes and the inspection crews; built like a blast-door himself and twice as immovable about paperwork.

**The Watch** — Marrow's ad-hoc militia of retired Rangers, Goliar kinship-crews, and hard cases with nowhere else to go; disciplined by frontier necessity, not corp pay.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — outside all corp authority; Marrow's law is the only law, enforced by the Watch and the plain fact that everyone here needs the gate to hold.

Response is military, not corporate: the guns point outward at the wilds, but they turn inward fast for anyone who threatens the gate mechanism itself. The gate is Cinderhold's heart, and Marrow will spend lives to protect it.

Alert here is the wall alarm — the horn that means the treeline has moved. When it sounds, every other concern stops.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired is thin and getting thinner the closer you stand to the outer door; past the threshold it fails entirely. Public AR shows Marrow's own hand-maintained gate-tag and a stark corruption-warning boundary.

Marrow's hardened comm-line to Cassavir terminates in a strongbox in the gatehouse — the single most secure node in Cinderhold.

A technomancer near the open door feels the Wired go strange, Veil-corruption bleeding in from the wilds; the deep net out here does not speak in the hive's voice.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Passage. Getting a crew, a cargo, or a person through the Marrow Gate — in or out — against Marrow's judgment is a run in itself. He cannot be bribed, but he can be convinced, and he owes Cassavir. Worse if they arrive as the timer runs down and the great pistons are already sighing shut.

2. The Door That Wouldn't Close. Someone sabotages the hydraulic mechanism at dusk, jamming the slabs half-open, and the crew is the only hands near enough to repair the pistons — or hold the breach — before full-dark and whatever's already moving in the treeline.

3. The Timer Is Wrong. The hive's higher-ups shorten the gate's daily hours without warning, stranding a caravan — and the crew — outside as the slabs close. Someone uptower wanted them locked out. Finding out who, from the wrong side of a forty-foot wall at nightfall, is the run.

## Cindermarket

*The first market for wasteland goods — and the last honest prices in the Reach*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Cindermarket is what a bazaar looks like when no corp takes a cut. It sprawls in the gate's shadow, a chaos of scrap-stalls and tarp-roofed pitches built from the bones of dead cargo-containers, and it sells the things the hive pretends not to want: wasteland salvage, unlicensed grey-tech, foraged jungle reagents, and food that grew in real dirt under real sky. The air is woodsmoke and rust and the strange green tang of things carried in from outside the wall.

There's sky here, filtered through a patchwork of stretched tarps and net, and the light is all firelight and salvaged lumen-strings, warm where the hive is cold. Nobody scans your SIN — half the traders don't have one — and the deals are done face to face, in cash or barter or the sharp-eyed trust of people who all live one wall-breach away from the same death. It is the freest commerce in the Reach and the most dangerous, because everything here came from somewhere it maybe shouldn't have, and someone uptower always wants to know where.

A trader missing two fingers waves you toward a stall of jungle-foraged reagents, grinning like a man who has seen worse than you and lived to mark it up.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**"Two-Finger" Pell** (human) — Cindermarket's unofficial mayor-of-commerce, a wasteland trader who knows the price and provenance of everything that passes through; missing fingers to a jungle reagent that "seemed safe."

**Sesh Marn** (Corran) — a guild-clan fence who moves grey-tech and salvage between Cinderhold and Cassavir's network inside the wall; the market's link to the Flats' economy.

**The Foragers** — a loose brotherhood of wasteland scavengers who go out for the reagents, relics, and salvage the market sells; every one of them has a story about what chased them back.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — self-policed by the traders and the Watch; theft is punished by the market itself, fast and without appeal, because the market only works on trust.

No formal security, but every trader is armed and the Watch patrols the edges. The real deterrent is exile: rob Cindermarket and you're put outside the wall at dusk.

Alert brings the Watch and, for a real threat, Marrow himself — the market is Cinderhold's economy and he protects it as such.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Thin and unreliable, like all of Cinderhold; most deals happen off the Wired entirely, in cash and hand-signal. Public AR shows a stale, corruption-flagged market tag.

Sesh Marn runs a small private mesh linking to Cassavir's network — the one reliable data-thread in the market, and a target for anyone wanting to map the Flats' salvage economy.

Foraged jungle reagents sometimes carry faint Veil-corruption that fritzes AR near the stalls — a hacker's early warning that a "relic" is more cursed than it looks.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Provenance Problem. Something in Cindermarket came from a place it shouldn't have — a corp lab, a sealed vault, a Veil-site — and a very serious buyer (or its very serious owner) is coming to collect. Pell wants the problem gone before it burns down his market.

2. The Forager's Map. A scavenger died in the market of jungle-fever, and left a map to where the good salvage is. Everyone wants it. It leads somewhere no one should go.

3. Kestrel's Clean Money. Kestrel Dynamics starts quietly buying up Cindermarket stalls through fronts, paying uptower prices — the soft first move of the Clean Route. Pell smells it and wants the crew to find out who's really behind the generosity.

## Marrow's Lantern

*Cinderhold's one true tavern — the last warm room before the wild dark*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Lantern is the heart of Cinderhold — a great low hall of salvaged plate and wasteland timber built right against the gate's shadow, and it is the last place with a roof, a fire, and a door that locks before the wilds begin. Step out its rear door and the hive's ceiling is gone, replaced by the real open dark and, on the horizon, the black wall of primeval jungle where the huge trees stand like the pillars of a drowned cathedral and things move that the Flats has no names for.

Inside, though, it is warm — genuinely, gloriously warm, a real wood-and-scrap fire in a central pit, the smell of roasting meat that walked on its own legs this morning, and light from a hundred hanging lanterns because out here the Wired is thin and a lantern is a thing you can trust. The crowd is unlike any inside the wall: Greenline Rangers off the frontier, wasteland caravaneers, Cinderhold salvagers, hive-exiles who can never go back, and edgerunner crews staging for a job in the green. They drink hard and early, because everyone here knows the odds of the ones who go out the back door.

Behind the bar, a weathered man in a Ranger's old field-coat pours a measure, sets it down, and looks at you the way a man looks at weather coming in.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ranger-Captain Cael Marrow** (human) — owns the Lantern and pours himself, because it's how he takes the measure of everyone passing his gate. (Full profile at the Marrow Gate.)

**Sister Ona** (Corran) — a Lamp-on-Ninth street-priest who runs a soup-line and vigil-lamp from a corner; Cinderhold's conscience and field-medic.

**"Longtooth" Garrik** (Goliar) — caravan-master and wasteland guide who knows the safe game-trails better than anyone still breathing; hires out to take crews into the near-wilds, on his terms.

**The Ones Who Came Back Wrong** (mixed species, Veil-touched) — survivors of the deep wilds, changed and haunted, whom Marrow shelters and who serve as the settlement's grimmest early warning of what the jungle is doing.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — the settlement's safest room and its command post; held by Marrow's militia.

Marrow's law under his roof: no fighting, and the gate closes at full-dark for everyone. Break either and you're put outside to reconsider.

Alert is the wall alarm; when it sounds every drink hits the table and every hand finds a weapon.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Thin and flickering; Cinderhold runs on lanterns and Marrow's word because the Wired can't be trusted this far out.

Marrow's hardened back-channel to Cassavir lives in a strongbox behind the bar — the most valuable node in town.

A technomancer feels the Wired go strange out here, Veil-corruption whispering in the deep net in a voice that isn't the hive's.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Back Door. Every expedition into the wilds begins here; Longtooth guides, and the question is whether the crew returns through the front door, gets carried in, or not at all.

2. The Wall Alarm. The horn sounds mid-drink, something huge has hit the Outer Wall, Cinderhold is undermanned, and Marrow is looking straight at the crew.

3. The Clean Route. Kestrel arrives at the Lantern with a contract that ends Cinderhold's poverty and its freedom in one signature; Marrow is the fulcrum and the crew's choices decide whether the last unowned ground stays unowned.

## The Greenline Post

*Verdant's forward station — where the Rangers watch the jungle back*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Bolted to the outer face of the wall, higher than anything else in Cinderhold, the Greenline Post is the only structure here that looks built rather than scavenged — a hardened Verdant field-station of green-painted plate and long observation slits, its watch-mast crowned with sensor-dishes and a heavy spotting-scope that never stops sweeping the treeline. This is the Greenline Rangers' forward eye on the wilds, and from its platforms you can see what the gate is really up against: kilometers of poisoned scrub, then the jungle wall, then nothing anyone has mapped and lived to sell.

The sky is fully open here and the wind carries the green rot-and-blossom smell of the wilds straight up into your face. Rangers move with the unhurried economy of people who have learned that panic gets you killed out here; they log every movement in the treeline, every corruption-bloom, every big shape that surfaces and submerges again in the canopy. A Ranger at the scope tracks something you can't see, murmurs a bearing to a logger, and does not look away.

On the wall below the Post, a long tally-board records the things that have come out of the green this season. The numbers are not going down.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ranger-Lieutenant Vesna Corr** (Elvani) — commands the Post, a Verdant career officer whose corporate poise sits oddly on the frontier; genuinely dedicated to the Rangers' mission even as she navigates Verdant's colder corporate interest in the wilds.

**Scout "Bramble" Teo** (wereraven Changer) — the Post's best long-range eyes, who reads the treeline through a scope from the watch-mast and has named half the apex-things on the tally-board.

**Old Hessing** (human) — the Post's naturalist-of-record, a half-mad former corp xenobiologist who catalogs the wilds' critters and will talk a crew's ear off about the Reach Behemoth if they let him.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — Verdant-held ground, the one corp presence Cinderhold tolerates because the Rangers point their guns at the jungle, not the town.

Hardened and disciplined; the Post is built to survive a wall-breach and coordinate the Watch's defense. It answers to Verdant, but in practice to Marrow when the alarm sounds.

Alert here means a confirmed wilds incursion — the Post is where the wall alarm originates.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Post runs the only robust Wired presence in Cinderhold — a hardened Verdant sensor-net feeding treeline data back up the wall. Public AR shows Verdant's official corruption-boundary and hazard tags.

The Post's real dataset — mapped incursion patterns, corruption-spread models, and everything Hessing has cataloged about the wilds — is the single best intelligence on what lies beyond the gate, and Verdant guards it.

A technomancer patched into the Post's net can see the corruption bleeding into the Wired from the jungle in real time — beautiful, and a warning.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Tally Grows. Incursions are up this season and Corr doesn't know why; she'll hire a deniable crew to escort a Ranger patrol into the near-wilds to find the cause before Verdant sends a "solution" that ends Cinderhold's freedom.

2. Hessing's Specimen. The old naturalist wants a live sample of something from the treeline, and offers the crew a fortune and a map. Bringing it back alive is worse than it sounds.

3. Verdant's Real Orders. Bramble intercepts a coded Verdant directive suggesting the corp's interest in the wilds is not conservation but resource — and that the Rangers, and Cinderhold, are expendable to it. Corr doesn't know. Should she?

## The Caravan Yard

*Where the wasteland runs meet — engines, guns, and one-way odds*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Caravan Yard is Cinderhold's motor-heart: a wide scrap-paved lot just inside the Outer Wall, hard against the two great gates where the muddy expedition roads punch through and drive off into the jungle. This is where the wasteland haulers muster, refit, and pray before they take those churned-mud arteries out into the green. These are not hive-vehicles — they're armored, up-gunned, jury-welded beasts built to survive a run across the poisoned scrub and back, their hulls scarred by things with claws, their roof-mounts swiveling at every sound. The air is diesel-analog, hot metal, and the ozone bite of overworked shielding.

Under the open sky, crews load cargo and check weapons with the grim focus of people doing math on their own survival. A hauler noses in through the outer-wall gate trailing jungle-vine and a long score down its flank that wasn't there this morning; its crew climbs down one short of the number that left. Nobody comments. In the Caravan Yard, that's just Tuesday, and everyone's already counting whether they'll be the short crew next time.

A rigger with grease to the elbows kicks a tire the size of a person and tells you, without being asked, exactly how your plan to go outside is going to get you killed.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**"Axle" Doreth** (Corran) — the Yard's master rigger and unofficial dispatcher, who refits the haulers and decides, by refusing to work on a doomed rig, who's ready to go outside; her word can scrub a run.

**Convoy-Boss Tam Iker** (human) — runs the biggest caravan operation out of Cinderhold, moving salvage and passengers across the scrub for steep pay; brave, greedy, and running out of luck.

**The Wheelmen** (mixed species) — the drivers and gunners who make the wasteland runs, a superstitious, tight-knit fraternity who know that the difference between a legend and a bone-pile is one bad bearing.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — Watch-patrolled, but mostly self-policed by the crews, who cannot afford sabotage or theft this close to a run.

The Yard is armed to the teeth by nature — every vehicle is a weapon — but violence here is bad for everyone's odds, so it's rare and settled fast.

Alert is the wall alarm; the Yard's guns become part of Cinderhold's defense, and a fueled convoy is both an asset and a bomb.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Thin; the Yard runs on Axle's private rig-mesh for vehicle telemetry and little else. Public AR shows a stale transit tag.

Every hauler carries logged run-data — routes, corruption-readings, where crews died — and Axle's archive of it is a priceless map of the near-wilds for anyone she trusts enough to share it.

Riggers report their vehicle AR going wrong the deeper into the scrub they drive; the telemetry archive is dotted with corruption-glitches that mark the truly bad ground.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Run That Needs a Crew. Tam Iker has a lucrative, dangerous run into the near-wilds and is one crew short after his last trip. The pay is real. So are the odds.

2. Sabotage in the Yard. Someone is quietly damaging rigs bound for a specific destination — someone who doesn't want anyone reaching it. Axle wants it stopped before a doctored hauler kills a crew in the scrub.

3. The Vehicle That Came Back Empty. A hauler rolls in on autopilot, crew gone, cargo intact, and a corruption-scored recording in its logs that no one wants to watch twice. What happened to them is out there still.

## The Outer Wall

*Cinderhold's ring of scavenged iron — the town's own defiance of the green dark*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Where the hive has its towering forty-foot rampart, Cinderhold has the Outer Wall — lower, rougher, but no joke: a serious defensive ring welded together from everything the wasteland gives up. Poured-concrete slabs. Shipping-container hulls stacked and filled. Sheet-metal plate, industrial girders, salvaged blast-panels off dead machinery, all lashed and welded into a continuous rampart with a fighting-walk along its top and its own smaller gates where the expedition roads punch through. It is not the hive's monolith, and everyone in Cinderhold knows it: the Outer Wall doesn't always stop what comes out of the green so much as slow it down long enough for the Watch to answer the alarm. It is a wall built of scavenged industry and the understanding that some night it won't be enough.

The sky is fully open along the wall-walk, and the wind off the scrub carries the smell of the jungle — wet green rot, strange blossom, and under it something animal and wrong. Watch-fires burn at intervals along the parapet, and the Watch walk their rounds with eyes fixed outward, on the treeline, always the treeline. In the mud beyond the ditch, you can see the tracks: three-toed, enormous, pressed deep, coming closer to the wall each season than the last.

A Watch-woman hands you a length of pipe without a word and nods toward the dark. Everyone stands a wall-shift in Cinderhold. Even guests. Especially guests.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Watch-Captain Bel Suri** (Goliar) — commands the Outer Wall defense under Marrow, an unflappable veteran who has held the wall through more bad nights than she'll admit and organizes every able body — including guests — into the defense.

**"Ditch" Kova** (Mutant) — the wall's forward scout and trap-layer, Veil-touched from too long in the scrub, who lays the pit-traps and tripline-alarms beyond the wall and reads the tracks better than anyone.

**The Wall-Shift** (everyone) — Cinderhold has no standing army large enough, so every resident and guest stands watch; the Outer Wall is defended by whoever is closest when the horn sounds.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — the town's literal edge; the Outer Wall is the security, backed by the Watch and the Greenline Post's early warning.

Defense is total and communal: the wall alarm turns every citizen into a defender, and Bel Suri's drills mean even a scratch crew knows their station.

Alert is, definitionally, the wall alarm — this is where it's answered, in pipe and gun and fire.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Effectively dead along the outer wall-walk; the Wired fails at the corruption boundary and the Outer Wall sits right on it. Warning-tags flicker and die.

Ditch Kova's tripline-alarm network is the only "tech" that works reliably out here — a low-tech mesh of physical alarms precisely because the Wired can't be trusted.

A technomancer on the wall at night feels the deep-net corruption at its strongest anywhere in the Reach; some who've reached toward it have not come all the way back.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Wall-Shift. The simplest hook in Cinderhold: the crew stands a watch, the horn sounds, and something out of the Critters chapter comes over the ditch. Survive till dawn.

2. The Weak Point. Ditch Kova has found a stretch of Outer Wall that won't hold another season, and the industrial salvage to shore it up is outside, in the scrub, where the tracks lead. Someone has to go get it.

3. Something Is Testing the Wall. The incursions aren't random — something intelligent is probing the Outer Wall for its weak point, the same stretch each night, learning. Bel Suri needs the crew to find out what, before it finds the gap first.

## The Burn-Yard

*Where the exiled become citizens — SIN-fire and second chances*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Burn-Yard is where Cinderhold's people are made. It's a stark plaza just inside the gate, dominated by a single scarred iron brazier where the newly-arrived burn what's left of their hive identity — the SIN-chips, the corp-IDs, the tracking-tags of a life the wall has ended. To come to Cinderhold is to become SIN-less on purpose, and the Burn-Yard is where that choice gets made real, in fire, in front of witnesses, one identity at a time.

Under the open sky, the brazier throws warm light on a crowd that is always part solemn, part wary: exiles saying goodbye to who they were, Cinderhold-born watching to see who the newcomers really are, and the Watch making quiet note of every face. The smell is scorched polymer and hot metal and the particular acrid tang of a corp-chip dying. Someone is always crying, and someone is always laughing, and in Cinderhold those are frequently the same person.

An old woman tending the brazier holds out a set of tongs and asks you, without judgment, whether you've come to burn something — because everyone who stays here eventually does.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Sabine** (Revenant) — keeper of the Burn-Yard brazier, an embodied-dead woman who has watched a thousand identities die into the fire and treats each with grave dignity; the closest thing Cinderhold has to a priest of its own founding rite.

**"Ledger" Tomas** (human) — a former corp records-clerk who fled with a head full of dangerous data and now helps newcomers scrub their old lives clean; knows exactly how hard the hive looks for the ones who run.

**The Newly-Burned** (any species) — a rotating population of raw exiles, some running from debt, some from crime, some from things worse; each one a potential ally, informant, or complication the crew can adopt.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — Watch-observed, because the Burn-Yard is where the hive's problems walk in and Marrow wants to know which ones will become Cinderhold's problems.

Peaceful by custom — violence at the brazier is taboo — but heavily watched; a burned SIN doesn't stop a determined corp skip-tracer from following someone here.

Alert is rare, but the Burn-Yard is the first place a corp extraction team would strike if they came for a specific exile.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

A strange dead-spot by design: the brazier's proximity and Cinderhold's thin Wired mean burned identities genuinely vanish from the net here, which is the whole point. Public AR shows only Sabine's austere memorial tag.

The moment of the burn is a small Wired event — a SIN winking out — that a watching hacker (or corp tracer) can catch if they're fast, making the Burn-Yard a paradoxical honeypot for the very people it's meant to hide.

Ledger Tomas runs a quiet scrubbing-service on a private mesh, erasing the digital trails the fire can't reach.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Tracer at the Brazier. A corp skip-tracer has followed a newcomer to the Burn-Yard and is waiting for the burn to confirm the target before extraction. Sabine won't allow violence at her fire — so the crew has to handle it another way, fast.

2. What Tomas Knows. Ledger Tomas fled with corp data valuable enough to kill for, and the corp has finally located him in Cinderhold. He'll trade everything he knows for protection — and everything he knows is a campaign's worth of leverage.

3. The One Who Won't Burn. A newcomer refuses to burn their SIN, and Marrow won't let them stay armed with a live tracking-chip inside his wall. Their reason for keeping it is either the crew's next job or the reason the corp is already coming.

## The Chapel of Ash

*Cinderhold's faith — a lamp against the dark, inside and out*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Chapel of Ash is the only building in Cinderhold that anyone bothered to make beautiful, and they did it with nothing — a small hall of salvaged glass and wasteland timber, its windows glazed with melted-down bottle-glass so the watch-fires outside throw fractured color across the floor. It's a Lamp-on-Ninth outpost, the frontier edge of the Flats' street-faith, and it exists for a simple reason: people who live one wall-breach from death need somewhere to put their fear.

Under a roof pieced from a hundred salvaged panels, the open sky shows through the gaps like stars, and a single great lamp burns at the altar — never extinguished, tended in shifts, a deliberate defiance of the dark that presses on Cinderhold from every side. The air is candle-smoke, incense improvised from jungle-blossom, and the low murmur of people praying in six tongues to whatever they still believe watches over the last unowned ground. Even the faithless come here; in Cinderhold, the Chapel is where you go to not be alone with what's outside.

A priest in a patched vestment tends the great lamp and, without turning, says you're welcome to sit — the lamp doesn't ask what anyone believes, only that they help keep it lit.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Father Kell** (human) — the Chapel's keeper, a Lamp-on-Ninth street-priest who came to the frontier to tend the faith at its hardest edge; a genuine source of real, costly faith in a place that badly needs it, and Sister Ona's superior in the order.

**The Lamp-Wardens** (mixed species) — volunteers who tend the great lamp in shifts, a cross-section of Cinderhold united by the one job everyone agrees matters; the lamp has not gone out in living memory.

**"The Returned"** (a Veil-touched Mutant) — a survivor of the deep wilds who found faith out there in the green dark and came back changed, now half-oracle, half-warning, whose corruption-tinged visions of the jungle Father Kell isn't sure are madness or prophecy.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — sanctuary by custom; the Chapel is neutral ground where even enemies may sit, and violating it is the deepest taboo in town.

Unarmed and unguarded, protected only by the community's respect — which, in Cinderhold, is stronger than most walls.

Alert almost never touches the Chapel directly; when the wall alarm sounds, it becomes the shelter for those who can't fight and the aid-station for those who did.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Deliberately Wired-dark; Father Kell keeps the Chapel off the net so that what's said here stays here. Public AR shows only a modest lamp-sigil.

A technomancer who reaches into the Wired from the Chapel finds it eerily quiet — a calm pocket in the corruption-frayed edge of the net, which some read as coincidence and some as something more.

"The Returned" speaks of the Wired and the Veil as one thing out in the jungle, and their corruption-visions occasionally contain data no one in Cinderhold should have — an unexplained, unnerving signal.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Returned's Vision. The Veil-touched oracle describes, in fevered detail, something coming out of the jungle — and the details are too specific to dismiss. Father Kell asks the crew to find out whether it's prophecy or a fever, before the wall alarm proves which.

2. Sanctuary. Someone the crew is chasing — or protecting — claims sanctuary in the Chapel, and Cinderhold's deepest taboo now stands between the crew and their job. Father Kell will not break it. What will they?

3. Keep the Lamp Lit. The great lamp's fuel is running out, the resupply is trapped outside the wall, and Cinderhold has quietly decided that if the lamp goes dark, the town's nerve goes with it. It's a small errand with the whole settlement's morale riding on it.

## The Rustworks

*Cinderhold's forge — where the wall gets rebuilt every single day*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Rustworks never sleeps, because the wall never stops needing repair. It's Cinderhold's foundry and fabrication yard, a clangorous sprawl of salvaged furnaces, jury-rigged presses, and scrap-sorting bays built against the inner face of the Outer Wall, and it turns the wasteland's endless junk into the town's endless needs: wall-plate, weapons, vehicle parts, tools, and the welded girders and blast-panels that are, quite literally, all that stands between Cinderhold and the green. The heat hits you from thirty meters out.

Under a roof of corrugated salvage, forge-light pools orange and fierce, and the open sky glows sullen through the smoke-vents above. The sound is a physical thing — hammer on plate, the shriek of cutting, the roar of the furnaces — layered over the sub-bass thrum of the great presses. Sparks fountain. Workers in scorched leathers move with the wary economy of people who handle molten metal for a living and have no attention to spare for yours. Everything here is being turned into something the wall will need before dawn.

A soot-black smith looks up from a length of glowing rebar, sizes up your gear in one glance, and asks if you're here to fix something or break something — because the Rustworks does a brisk trade in both.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Foreman Grud** (Goliar) — runs the Rustworks with a bellow and an unerring eye for metal, prioritizing wall-repair above all else; the person who decides whether the crew's job gets fabrication time or waits behind the Outer Wall's endless needs.

**"Spark" Ivane** (Corran) — the Works' master weaponsmith and grey-tech tinkerer, who builds and modifies the guns that hold the wall and will craft or repair a crew's gear for the right price or the right favor.

**The Salvage Crews** (mixed species) — the sorters and scrappers who feed the furnaces, who see everything that comes in from the wasteland first and know which "junk" is actually something dangerous, valuable, or cursed.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — Watch-linked, because the Rustworks makes the wall's weapons and its loss would doom the town; guarded accordingly.

The Works is dangerous by nature — molten metal, presses, furnaces — and self-policed by workers who know a moment's carelessness kills; deliberate trouble is dealt with by Grud personally.

Alert means the Works shifts instantly to war-production and its furnaces become part of the wall's last-ditch defense.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Thin, and drowned in electromagnetic noise from the furnaces and presses; the Wired barely functions inside the Works. Public AR shows a stale industrial tag.

Spark Ivane keeps a private fabrication-mesh with schematics for every weapon and wall-component Cinderhold uses — a small but critical dataset for anyone wanting to arm, or disarm, the town.

The salvage crews sometimes feed Veil-corrupted metal into the furnaces by accident, producing brief, alarming Wired-glitches that Spark has learned to recognize as a warning to pull the batch.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Behind the Wall's Needs. The crew needs something fabricated — a part, a weapon, a tool for the job — but Grud won't bump wall-repair for anyone. Getting to the front of the line means solving one of the Works' problems first.

2. Spark's Special Order. Someone commissioned a weapon from Spark that shouldn't exist in Cinderhold — something meant for people, not critters. She's uneasy about the buyer and wants the crew to find out what it's really for.

3. The Cursed Batch. A shipment of "clean" salvage turns out to be Veil-corrupted, and it's already in the furnace-cycle; if it goes into the wall-plate, the corruption goes into the Outer Wall itself. The Works needs it found and pulled before it becomes part of the wall.

## The Wasteland Overlook

*The edge of everything — where Cinderhold looks out at what's coming*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Overlook is the highest point in Cinderhold that isn't the gate itself — a salvaged watch-platform bolted to the tallest surviving section of old wall, reached by a switchback of welded ladders, and it exists for one purpose: to look out. From up here the whole grim geography lays itself bare. Directly below, Cinderhold's scrap-roofs huddle against the Outer Wall. Beyond the wall, the poisoned scrubland runs gray and dead to the horizon. And past that, filling half the world, the primeval jungle — huge trees like green thunderheads, mist pooling in the ravines, and the winding black thread of a river that no one in Cinderhold has followed to its end and returned.

The sky is enormous here, and empty of the hive's comforting ceiling, and the wind never stops. In the far distance, when the mist parts, you can sometimes see them: shapes moving above the canopy, or the slow bulk of something vast pushing through the trees, throwing up a wake of startled birds. The Overlook is where Cinderhold reckons with its future, because everyone who climbs up here understands the same thing at the same moment — the Wild Lands are huge, and the town is very, very small.

A lone watcher shares the platform, spotting-scope trained on the treeline, and doesn't lower it as they say: "First time seeing it? Everybody's quiet, the first time."

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**The Watcher** (rotating, any species) — whoever holds the Overlook shift, a quiet, thousand-yard-stare posting that Cinderhold treats as half-duty, half-meditation; the Watchers are the town's dreamers, mystics, and the ones who've decided to face the future head-on.

**Cartographer Nyle** (Elvani) — a Verdant-trained mapmaker who defected to Cinderhold and now works to chart the near-wilds from observation and caravan-data, building the first real map of the Wild Lands to come; brilliant, obsessive, and short on data he can only get by going out.

**"Skyeye" Bram** (wereraven Changer) — a Changer scout who flies short reconnaissance loops beyond the wall in raven-form, the only Cinderholder who regularly sees the near-jungle from above and comes back; his sightings feed both the Rangers and Nyle's map.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — lightly held; the Overlook's value is observation, not defense, though it feeds the wall alarm alongside the Greenline Post.

Exposed and high, the Overlook is safe from ground threats but vulnerable to anything that flies — and some things out there do; watchers keep one eye on the sky as well as the treeline.

Alert originates here as often as at the Post; the Overlook is frequently first to see a big incursion cross the scrub.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired is nearly gone at this altitude and this close to the boundary; the Overlook runs on optics and Skyeye's own eyes precisely because the net can't be trusted here.

Nyle's growing map — part data, part observation, part educated guess — is the single most valuable document about the Wild Lands in existence, and the seed of the eventual Wild Lands expansion; every faction that wants the wilds wants a copy.

A technomancer who reaches out from the Overlook toward the jungle touches the rawest edge of the Veil-corruption in the Wired — and more than one has reported the deep net seeming to look back.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Nyle's Map. The cartographer will pay well for firsthand data from specific points in the near-wilds — a river-bend, a ruin, a corruption-bloom — turning the crew into the surveyors of the Wild Lands to come. Each expedition fills in one more terrifying blank.

2. What Skyeye Saw. The Changer scout returns from a recon loop shaken, describing something new beyond the treeline — a structure, a migration, a threat — that changes what Cinderhold thought it knew about the wilds. Confirming it means going out.

3. The Long View. From the Overlook, an observant crew can see the shape of the whole campaign's future — the routes into the wilds, the corruption spreading, the corps circling Cinderhold. This is the POI that plants the Wild Lands hook and lets a Director foreshadow everything to come.
