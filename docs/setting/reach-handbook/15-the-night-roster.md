# The Night Roster

*Nightlife & Social Venues of the Flats*

## The Dead Line

*Cassavir's own corner bar — where the crew waits for the phone to ring*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You don't so much find the Dead Line as let the Switchboard's crowd deposit you at its door. It's built into the shell of a decommissioned signal-relay station not forty meters off Cassavir's booth — close enough that the old woman's runners use it as a waiting room, close enough that when a deal goes live you can be at her switchgear before the message stops blinking. The sign is a length of genuine dead phone-line, coiled into a noose and strung with amber lumen-wire, humming that one warm note over the door.

Inside, there is no sky — there never is, this low — only the relay station's old cable-vaulted ceiling, close and black, sweating the warm chemical rain down the dead conduit-runs so the whole bar shines faintly wet. But it's warm here, and quiet in the way the Flats almost never lets you have: the sound-wall of the concourse drops to a murmur the moment the door seals, swallowed by decades of acoustic dead-panelling the relay techs bolted up a thousand years ago. Salvaged booth-lights pool amber and low. A wall of dead switchboard jacks behind the bar has been rewired into taps. Courier-drones can't come inside — house rule, enforced — so for once nothing is watching you from the ceiling.

The barkeep is a broad Corran woman with forearms like cable-bundles and a memory like a ledger, and she has already set down the drink you didn't order yet, because you look like the last three crews who came in off a hard run and she was right about all of them.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ferra Vantoll** (Corran) — the barkeep and owner, a former guild-clan foreman who took the relay-shell as payment for a debt no one will name. She is Cassavir's in the way water is the river's: not employed, but aligned. She keeps the peace, keeps the tabs, and keeps her mouth shut, and she has thrown out corp-scouts twice her size for asking the wrong question.

**"Splice" Odarum** (wererat Changer) — a twitchy off-books decker who holds the back booth as an office, sells burner-SINs and clean comm-lines, and functions as the bar's unofficial fixer-below-the-fixer for jobs too small to bother Cassavir with.

**Tover "Two-Line" Vask** (Corran) — Cassavir's floor-runner (see the Switchboard) drinks here between errands; if the crew is owed a message, it arrives through him, at this bar, over a drink.

**The Regulars** — a rotating table of burned-out edgerunners who will trade a war-story for a round and, occasionally, a genuinely useful warning about who's hiring and who's a trap.

#### The Menu (What Nuyen Buys at the Bar)

**"Dead Air"** — house rotgut cut with synth-citrus; ¥4. The drink Ferra sets down unbidden.

**Relay Black** — real (allegedly) coffee, thick enough to stand a spoon in; ¥6. Standard for a crew about to run.

**Corran Ferment** — a sour guild-clan grain-beer, warm, communal; ¥5 the jug.

**The Long-Distance** — top-shelf smuggled Spire liquor, poured only for crews who just got paid; ¥40.

**Ferra's Plate** — whatever the kitchen has: usually cargo-noodle, grey-protein skewers, real onion if you're lucky; ¥8.

**Booth rental (back room, sound-dead, drone-proof)** — ¥50 the hour, ¥30 more if you want Ferra to forget you were there.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — under Cassavir's peace by extension. No gang claims it because everyone's deals route through here and no one wants to be the crew that soured Ferra.

No visible guns; Ferra's rule is the rule. Drawing steel in the Dead Line gets you barred from the Switchboard itself — a social death sentence in the Flats — before it gets you shot. Two of the Regulars are Ironclad-trained and slow to move, exactly like Cassavir's porters.

Alert here is social and instant: the room goes silent, Ferra makes one call, and the concourse outside closes to you.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The bar sits in a genuine **dead-air pocket** — the old relay's shielding kills most AR and comms inside, which is precisely why runners love it and corp-scouts hate it. Public overlay shows only a faded "DECOMMISSIONED — MERIDIAN SIGNAL" tag.

Splice runs a tiny private mesh from the back booth for those who know the handshake; a hacker who digs finds it, and finds it politely booby-trapped.

Cassavir's deal-threads terminate a node here — this is one of the three places in the Flats where a message from the old woman can physically reach you when the Wired can't.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Waiting Room. The crew is nursing drinks when Two-Line Vask slides in: Cassavir has a job, right now, and it starts the moment they walk out the door. The Dead Line is where most sessions can begin.

2. Splice's Small Problem. Splice sold a burner-SIN to the wrong buyer and now a Sanctum skip-tracer is sitting three stools down pretending to drink. He'll pay the crew in clean comm-lines to make the problem leave without a scene Ferra would blame him for.

3. Ferra's Debt. The unnamed debt that bought Ferra the relay-shell comes calling — a Nyx lieutenant wants the dead-air pocket as a drop-node and won't take no. If Ferra loses the bar, the crew loses their waiting room, and Cassavir loses a terminal. She won't ask for help. She shouldn't have to.

## Obsidian's Velvet Stair

*The Flats' one taste of the Spires — pleasure, sold clean*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The line starts three lanes out, and it is the best-dressed crowd you will ever see this deep. The Velvet Stair is Obsidian's flag planted in the Flats — a genuine uptower pleasure-house dropped into the underlevels like a jewel into mud, and it wants you to feel the difference. The rain still falls here, because there is no sky here either, but Obsidian has strung a canopy of warm gold heat-lamps over the entrance so the chemical drizzle turns to steam before it touches the clientele, and the effect is deliberate: even the weather is better on Obsidian's threshold.

Above, the traffic is corp-grade — a sleek private hovercab settles onto a reserved cradle, disgorging Grid-dwellers slumming for the night, while the district's usual courier-drone chaos is held back by a soft-glowing exclusion-boundary Obsidian pays Meridian to enforce. Inside the doors, the sound-wall doesn't stop so much as transform: the concourse roar becomes a low, engineered warmth of music tuned just beneath the pulse, and the light goes from neon-riot to a single seductive obsidian-and-violet glow that makes everyone look like the best version of themselves.

A hostess who is too beautiful to be an accident — Elvani, of course, they always are — reads your Lifestyle off your clothes in half a second and decides, with a smile that costs Obsidian money to train, exactly how much of the Stair you are going to be allowed to see tonight.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Madame Sévrine Cael** (Elvani) — the Stair's manager, an Obsidian company officer with three centuries' patience compressed into flawless hospitality. She remembers every face, every tab, every weakness, and files all three. She sells pleasure and buys secrets, and the second trade is the real business.

**Doss** (Goliar) — the door. Seven feet of engineered muscle in a tailored coat, unfailingly polite until the exact instant he isn't. He has never had to hit anyone twice.

**"Kitten"** (human, chromed) — a simsense dream-hostess and one of Sévrine's best listeners; every pillow-side confession she hears becomes a line in Obsidian's ledger. She'd like to stop. She can't afford to.

**The Dreamers** — the high-end simsense clientele in the upper booths, chasing tailored experiences most of the Flats will never afford; some are Grid tourists, some are addicts, one of them is always someone important pretending not to be.

#### The Menu (What Nuyen Buys — Obsidian Takes Chip Only, Never Cash)

**The House Pour** — engineered cocktails keyed to your mood-read; ¥25 and up.

**A Clean Dream** — licensed simsense, an hour of curated experience, safe and legal-ish; ¥120.

**The Velvet Hour** — private dream-booth, hostess-guided, discretion included; ¥400.

**Designer Highs** — Obsidian's own tailored intoxicants, the safest illegal thing you can put in your body; ¥60 the dose.

**The Back Menu** (invitation only) — BTL, deep-immersion illicit simsense, and appetites the front room doesn't list; priced in favors as often as nuyen.

**A Conversation With Sévrine** — free. It is never free.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — but a corporate island. Obsidian holds the Stair and two lanes around it as effective extraterritorial ground; the district's usual rules stop at the heat-lamp canopy.

Response is layered and quiet: Doss at the door, unseen Ironclad contractors on retainer, and Obsidian's real deterrent — the threat of being cut off, blacklisted from the one clean pleasure in the Flats. Physical violence is answered fast and professionally, but the Stair prefers you never see it.

Alert brings Obsidian corporate security, not street muscle — a serious escalation most crews can't afford to provoke.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer is gorgeous and total — Obsidian floods the entrance with the richest, most seductive AR in the district, all of it advertising, all of it tracking. Every guest is logged, scored, and filed the moment they cross the boundary.

Beneath it, a hacker finds Obsidian's true product: the **confession-vault**, an encrypted archive of everything every hostess has ever heard. It is one of the most valuable and most dangerous datasets in the Flats, and breaking it is a campaign-defining score.

The dream-booths run on a closed simsense-net; a technomancer who slips into it can ride a client's tailored dream — or trap them in one.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Confession-Vault. Someone — a rival corp, a blackmailed official, a jilted lover — will pay enormously to steal, copy, or erase a specific entry in Sévrine's vault. Getting in means getting close to Sévrine, who is nobody's fool.

2. The Dreamer Who Won't Wake. A high-value client has been in a Velvet Hour booth for two days and Obsidian can't rouse him without admitting the booth did it. Sévrine hires the crew, quietly, to extract him — and the reason he won't wake is worse than an overdose.

3. Cut Off. A crew member runs up a debt they can't pay and Obsidian offers the classic bargain: work off the tab, in favors, forever. The Stair is how Obsidian turns edgerunners into assets, one clean dream at a time.

## The Grind

*The dive at the bottom — where the burned come to stay burned*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Every district has a bar like the Grind, and pretending otherwise is how tourists get rolled. It's a low, wide, smoke-yellow room wedged under a load-bearing pylon where three lanes of the Flats bleed together, and it has exactly one virtue: it does not care who you are. No SIN-reader at the door. No dress code but "conscious." No music but whatever the ancient wall-speaker is mangling tonight and the low grind of the pylon's stress-bearings overhead, which is where the place got its name.

There is no sky, only the pylon's vast underside dripping rust-brown rain into buckets the staff never empty, and the light is the color of a dying battery — sodium-yellow, flickering, merciful, because at this wattage you can't see the state of the floor or the state of the other patrons. Overhead traffic doesn't bother with the Grind; the drones don't fly this low under the pylon and the cabs won't stop here, which is exactly the point. The only things that come to the Grind are the things that walk in.

The bartender is a Revenant — grey-skinned, patient, unbothered by time — and he has been pouring here longer than anyone can remember, which in a place this rough is its own kind of reference. He pours, he listens, and he says nothing, ever, and that silence is the most valuable thing on the menu.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Grale** (Revenant) — the bartender and closest thing the Grind has to law. The embodied dead unsettle the tidy uptower world, but down here Grale's changelessness is a comfort: he has seen every kind of trouble walk in and walk out or get carried out, and he keeps no side but the bar's.

**"Cinder" Mok** (Mutant) — a wasteland-scarred information-seller who works the Grind's corner table, trading rumors out of Cinderhold and the outer gate; the only person here who's been outside the wall and come back, and he'll remind you of it for a drink.

**Vess Karr** (human) — a washed-out ex-Ironclad who drinks alone and will, three drinks in, either sell you the best combat advice in the district or start the fight that gets everyone barred.

**The Barred Table** — a corner where crews the rest of the Flats won't touch drink together in wary truce; if the crew needs muscle nobody else will hire, it's sitting right there, dangerous and cheap.

#### The Menu (Cash Only; the Chip-Reader "Broke" Years Ago)

**The Grind** — whatever's in the tank, poured until you stop asking what it is; ¥2.

**Battery Acid** — the Grind, but they charge it and add a slice of something citrus-adjacent; ¥3.

**Wasteland Brew** — Cinder's own import, fermented outside the wall, tastes like green corruption and courage; ¥5, and Grale won't vouch for it.

**The Quiet** — a booth in the back and Grale's guaranteed silence; ¥10, no questions, no memory.

**Food** — there is a pot. It is always the same pot. It is ¥3 and it has never once made anyone sick, which is a miracle nobody examines.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — genuinely lawless, self-policed by Grale's presence and the mutual understanding that the Grind only works if it stays neutral ground.

No guns drawn is the only rule, and it's enforced by consensus: pull steel in the Grind and every hard case in the room, including the Barred Table, has reason to end you before you become their problem. Grale keeps a shotgun no one has ever seen him reach for.

No Alert response of any kind — nobody's coming. That's the danger and the appeal. What happens in the Grind gets settled in the Grind.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Near-total dead-air, and not by design — the Grind is just too far under the pylon and too poor to matter, so the Wired barely renders it. Public AR shows a decades-stale tag and nothing else.

This makes the Grind a natural low-tech meeting ground: no logging, no tracking, no ad-noise. A hacker finds almost nothing, because there's almost nothing here to find, which is itself a kind of privacy money can't buy uptower.

Cinder Mok sells the one valuable data-stream in the room: word-of-mouth intelligence from outside the wall, unlogged and unverifiable, worth everything or nothing.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Man From Outside. Cinder Mok has seen something in the wilds beyond Cinderhold that has him too scared to go back — and too broke to stay. He'll sell the crew the location for a fortune, and the location is where the campaign's next act lives.

2. Vess's Last Fight. The ex-Ironclad has a name, a grudge, and a corp bounty on the man who burned his old unit. He wants a crew. He is either the best lead the crew will get this month or the fastest way to die, and telling the difference is the job.

3. Neutral Ground No More. A gang decides the Grind's dead-air pocket is too useful to leave neutral and moves to claim it. If they succeed, the Flats loses its one truly open table — and every crew that ever needed the Grind now owes the wrong people.

## The Choir Room

*The night-market's underground pulse — where the Flats dances so it doesn't scream*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You feel it in the floor before you find the door — a sub-bass pulse coming up through the soles of your boots, threading through the Neon Shambles' medieval tangle of market-lanes until you follow it down a stairwell that shouldn't lead anywhere and it opens into sound. The Choir Room is a converted cistern, a vast domed drum of a space three levels below the night-market, and the entire Flats' worth of people who can't afford the Velvet Stair come here to lose themselves instead.

There is no sky — there is the cistern's great curved ceiling, streaming condensation and reclaimed rain down the old overflow-channels so the walls themselves seem to weep in time with the music, every drop lit by a storm of salvaged holo-projectors throwing pink and cyan and gold across a thousand moving bodies. No air-traffic reaches this deep; the only things in the air are the light, the heat, and the smell of a crowd that has been dancing since the last shift ended. The music is not tuned beneath the pulse like Obsidian's — it is the pulse, a live technomancer-driven set that reads the room's mood off the Wired and answers it, so the whole crowd moves as one enormous animal.

Somewhere on a scaffold-stage above the churn, a figure with wires running from their temples to the sound-rig lifts a hand, and the entire cistern inhales with them.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mira "The Choir" Sen** (Technomancer, human) — the resident set-runner, who mixes live by riding the crowd's emotional signature off the Wired and feeding it back as sound. She is a genuine folk-hero of the Flats' night-culture and, quietly, one of the best informal read-the-room intelligence assets alive — she can feel when a room turns.

**Bexx** (wereraven Changer) — the door and the lookout, perched literally above the entrance stair, who reads every face coming down and caws a coded warning to the floor if it's the wrong one.

**The Dust Brothers** (two Corran cousins) — the dealers who work the edges, selling club-grade designer highs cut cheaper and rougher than Obsidian's; tolerated because they keep the doses survivable and the overdoses out of the Choir Room's reputation.

**Sanctum's Eyes** — somewhere in the crowd, always, at least one insurance-actuarial watcher counting bodies and calculating the room's illegal occupancy against the day they decide to raid it.

#### The Menu (Chip or Cash; the Bar Is a Converted Overflow-Pipe)

**Cistern Water** — the house drink, luminous and cheap, glows under the holos; ¥3.

**The Drop** — a stronger pour timed to the set's big moments; ¥6.

**Dust** (from the Brothers, off-menu) — club-grade designer high, ¥15 the dose, "survivable, mostly."

**A Word With The Choir** — access to Mira between sets, worth more than any drink; priced in favors, information, or genuine loyalty, never nuyen.

**The Cool-Down Loft** — a quieter mezzanine above the churn for deals, recovery, or a private conversation; ¥20 the seat.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Neon Shambles turf, unclaimed by any single gang because the Choir Room belongs to the scene, and the scene protects itself. Bexx's warning-network is the first line; the crowd is the second.

No hard security and no guns tolerated — the danger here is the crush itself and the occasional bad dose. A drawn weapon empties the room in seconds, and the crowd will not protect whoever drew it.

Alert means Sanctum finally moves — an occupancy raid, a shutdown order, a checkpoint at the top of the stair. It's the sword hanging over the whole venue.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Choir Room is an overlay phenomenon — Mira's set floods the local Wired with the crowd's live emotional signature, making it one of the richest, strangest AR-spaces in the Flats. A technomancer who drops in feels the whole room's mood as a tangible thing.

Public AR shows nothing; the venue is unlisted, passed hand to hand. Finding it the first time is a small rite of passage.

Beneath Mira's set, a hacker can find the raw crowd-read data — a real-time map of a thousand people's feelings, which is either the most beautiful thing in the district or, in the wrong hands, a targeting solution.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Read The Room. Mira felt the crowd turn wrong three nights running before three separate crews vanished, and she's convinced someone is using the Choir Room as a hunting ground. She'll trade the crew everything she knows for someone willing to be bait.

2. The Raid. Sanctum finally moves on the Choir Room, and the crew is inside when the checkpoint seals the top of the stair. Getting a thousand panicking dancers — and themselves — out through the overflow-channels is the run.

3. The Choir's Price. Mira's read-the-room gift is drawing corporate attention; Meridian wants to hire or acquire her, and Obsidian wants her set silenced so the Velvet Stair has no rival. She needs a crew to keep her free, and neither corp intends to lose gracefully.

## Marrow's Lantern

*Cinderhold's one true tavern — the last warm room before the wild dark*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

This is the only entry in the Roster that sits outside the wall. Marrow's Lantern is the heart of Cinderhold, a great low hall of salvaged plate and wasteland timber built right against the outer gate's shadow, and it is the last place with a roof, a fire, and a door that locks before the wilds begin. Where every other venue in the Flats has no sky, the Lantern has too much of one — step out its rear door and the hive's ceiling is gone, replaced by the real, terrible open dark and, on the horizon, the black wall of primeval jungle where the huge trees stand like the pillars of a drowned cathedral and things move that the Flats has no names for.

Inside, though, it is warm — genuinely, gloriously warm, a real wood-and-scrap fire in a central pit, the smell of roasting meat that walked on its own legs this morning, and light from a hundred hanging lanterns because out here the Wired is thin and a lantern is a thing you can trust. The crowd is unlike any inside the wall: Greenline Rangers off the frontier, wasteland caravaneers, Cinderhold salvagers, hive-exiles who can never go back, and the occasional edgerunner crew staging for a job in the green. They drink hard and they drink early, because everyone here knows the odds of the ones who go out the back door.

Behind the bar, a weathered man with a Ranger's old field-coat and a gatekeeper's hard eyes pours a measure, sets it down, and looks at you the way a man looks at weather coming in.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ranger-Captain Cael Marrow** (human), "the Gate" — Cinderhold's protector and the Lantern's owner, a former Greenline Ranger who holds the settlement by being harder, fairer, and more feared than anyone who'd take it. He keeps a cold professional back-channel to Mama Cassavir inside the wall; the two of them are the twin switchboards of the hive's edge. He pours drinks himself because it's how he takes the measure of everyone who passes through his gate.

**Sister Ona** (Corran) — a Lamp-on-Ninth street-priest who came out to Cinderhold and stayed, running a soup-line and a vigil-lamp from a corner of the Lantern; the settlement's conscience and its field-medic both.

**"Longtooth" Garr** (Goliar) — a caravan-master and wasteland guide who knows the safe game-trails through the near-jungle better than anyone still breathing; he'll guide a crew outward for a steep price and a signed understanding of the risk.

**The Ones Who Came Back Wrong** — a quiet table of survivors who went into the deep wilds and returned changed — Veil-touched, haunted, marked. Cinderhold shelters them because Marrow will not turn away his own, and they are the settlement's grimmest early-warning system for what the jungle is doing.

#### The Menu (Cash, Barter, or Salvage — Cinderhold Runs on All Three)

**Gatefire** — a fierce distilled wasteland spirit that Marrow swears is safe and Sister Ona swears is not; ¥3 or a decent trade.

**Real Meat** — whatever the hunters brought in, roasted over the pit; ¥6, and you don't ask what it was.

**Ranger's Ration** — hardtack, brine-protein, and hot chicory-analog, the meal you take before you go out the back door; ¥4.

**Green Ferment** — Cinderhold's own brew, made from wasteland crops, an acquired and slightly hallucinogenic taste; ¥3.

**A Guide's Retainer** — Longtooth's fee to take a crew into the near-wilds; ¥200 and up, plus a cut of anything they drag back, plus his terms, which are not negotiable.

**Marrow's Ear** — a seat at the end of his bar and his honest assessment of whatever you're about to do; free, and worth more than everything else on the menu combined.

#### Security

Zone: Cinderhold — outside all corp authority, held by Marrow's ad-hoc militia of retired Rangers, Goliar kinship-crews, and anyone who can hold a line. The Lantern is the settlement's safest room and its command post both.

Marrow's law is simple and absolute: no fighting under his roof, and the gate closes at full-dark for everyone, no exceptions, because what comes out of the jungle at night does not knock. Break either rule and you're put outside the wall to reconsider.

Alert here isn't corp security — it's the wall alarm, the horn that means something big has come out of the treeline, and when it sounds every drink in the Lantern hits the table at once and every hand reaches for a weapon.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired is thin out here — Cinderhold sits at the ragged edge of the hive's data-web, and past the wall it fails entirely. Inside the Lantern, AR flickers and drops; most of Cinderhold runs on lanterns, hand-signals, and Marrow's word precisely because the Wired can't be trusted this far out.

A technomancer this close to the wilds feels something the hive never shows: the Wired doesn't just thin toward the jungle, it goes strange — Veil-corruption bleeds into the data-layer, and the deep net out here whispers in a voice that isn't the hive's.

Marrow keeps one hardened, hive-linked comm-line — his back-channel to Cassavir — physically locked in a strongbox behind the bar. It is the single most secure and most valuable node in Cinderhold, and everyone knows it exists.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Back Door. A crew needs to go into the wilds — for a job, a person, or a thing Cassavir wants recovered — and Marrow's Lantern is where every such expedition begins. Longtooth will guide them; the question is whether they come back through the front door or get carried in, or don't come back at all.

2. The Wall Alarm. The horn sounds while the crew is drinking, and something enormous has hit the Outer Wall. Cinderhold is undermanned tonight, Marrow is counting hands, and he's looking straight at the crew. Hold the wall until dawn, and you drink free forever.

3. The Clean Route. Kestrel Dynamics' representatives arrive at the Lantern with a contract that would end Cinderhold's poverty and its freedom in the same signature. Marrow is the fulcrum, the settlement is splitting, and the crew's choices — help Kestrel prove the corridor safe, help Nyx keep it "dangerous," or help Marrow hold the gate free — decide whether the last unowned ground stays unowned.
