# Cinder Market

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // The Grey-Tech Forge*

## The Forge Row

*Drone & chrome fabrication core — the beating anvil of the Flats*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The heat finds you before the street does — a dry wave rolling out from a hundred open shed-mouths, so different from the wet chill everywhere else in the Flats that your skin prickles awake. There is no sky above Forge Row, only the black corrugated underside of the level above, dripping its slow chemical rain onto tin roofs that hiss and steam it away almost as fast as it falls. Chimney-stacks rise in ragged ranks between the sheds, venting gouts of sodium-orange sparks that hang a moment in the dark like dying stars before the rain kills them.

Overhead the traffic never stops being strange to newcomers: cargo-drones thread between the stacks in low, careful lines, dodging the heat-shimmer; a rust-scarred hovercab picks its way at rooftop height, running lights strobing pink through the smoke; below it all, ground-rigs loaded with pig iron and chassis-frames grind along rails half-sunk in the street, horns bawling at foot traffic that refuses to move for anything short of impact. The noise is a forge-noise now, layered under the usual six-tongue babble and sub-bass pump-thrum: hammer-strikes, the shriek of cutting torches, servo-whine, the deep intermittent thoom of drop-forges landing.

Every doorway glows a different color depending on what's burning inside — arc-white, slag-orange, the sick green of chem-etching baths — and the neon signage bolted above each shed has long since half-melted into abstract shapes, still legible if you already know what you're looking for. This is where the Flats build the things that keep it alive. And the things that end other people.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Master-Smith Orya Devanko** — matriarch of the Row's largest fabrication cooperative, a Corran guild-clan elder with a chrome left arm she forged herself. Sets informal law for the block through favor-debt rather than force. Wants the Row's independence protected from Ferrum's creeping lease-buyouts.

**"Sixfinger" Bellamy Cho** — a drone-frame specialist who lost three fingers to a press and replaced them with two extra, claims it improved his soldering. Runs a semi-legal chop-shop for military drone chassis; sells to whoever pays, including Ironclad off-books buyers.

**Petra Nakase** — a young Nyx-connected fixer who brokers between the Row's smiths and buyers who can't be seen here. Quiet, precise, always working three deals at once.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — held loosely by Devanko's cooperative council; no single gang owns the whole Row.

Violence here is bad for everyone's business, so the smiths police it themselves: theft or bloodshed on the Row brings every shed's workers out with whatever's in reach — pipe-wrenches, welding rigs, the occasional illegal arc-cannon. Alert response is fast, physical, and collective rather than corporate; Ironclad or Sanctum enforcers who come sniffing get stonewalled by a hundred simultaneous "didn't see anything."

Cinderhold runners occasionally pass through as neutral couriers; harming one draws quiet, patient reprisal from further down the strata.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired here is thick with proprietary schematics traffic — smiths lease designs to each other over short-range mesh nets that never touch the public net, making the Row a dead zone for casual surveillance despite the density of activity.

A skilled hacker can find drifting "ghost-orders" — abandoned fabrication jobs whose specs were never collected, some of them military-grade and worth a fortune to the right buyer.

AR tags above certain sheds glow only for those with a guild-recognized credential; outsiders see blank, sputtering placeholders.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Buyout. Ferrum's real-estate arm has quietly bought the lease under three sheds and plans to "modernize" — corporate speak for clearing the Row's independents. Devanko wants the crew to find who's selling out the cooperative from inside before the papers finalize.

2. Ghost-Order. A courier drone crashes nearby carrying an uncollected fabrication spec for an Ironclad prototype combat-frame. Whoever posted the order never came back for it — and now three factions want to know why.

3. Sixfinger's Debt. Bellamy Cho owes a debt to a Nyx enforcer he can't pay in scrip. He offers the crew a discounted drone-frame refit if they run one "delivery" for him — a delivery that turns out to be a live prototype stolen from a Grid-level lab.

## Scrap Cathedral

*Salvage-metal exchange; huge shed*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You hear the Cathedral before you see its doors — a vast, resonant clatter of falling metal, like rain made of chain-link, echoing out from a shed so large its far wall is lost in steam-haze. It was a rail depot once, before the Reach forgot the shape of trains; now the arched roof, patched a thousand times with scavenged sheet-tin, is the closest thing to a sky the crew will find down here, and even it drips warm chemical rain through gaps punched a century apart.

Inside, mountains of salvage rise in ordered chaos — stripped drone chassis, gutted hovercar frames, corp armor plate scored down to bare alloy — sorted by hand and by decades of habit no ledger has ever fully captured. Overhead, a gantry crane the size of a hab-block trundles on rusted rails, hauling shipping-crates between towers of scrap while a courier-drone squadron ducks and weaves beneath it like sparrows around a heron. Out front, cargo-crawlers idle nose-to-tail waiting to unload, their drivers arguing prices in six languages over the boom and crash of the yard. Neon strung along the rafters in loops of dying color makes the whole shed glow the color of a bruise.

Somewhere in that mountain range of dead machines is exactly the part you need — if you can find someone who remembers where it's buried, and if you're willing to pay what it's actually worth instead of what the sign says.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Old Han Voss** — the Cathedral's unofficial archivist, a Cinderhold exile who has cataloged more of the scrap-mountains in his head than any database ever managed. Trades knowledge for stories and small mercies.

**Foreman Iset Kray** — runs the crane crews and the yard's daily order; a Ferrum contract-employee who quietly resells the best finds before they hit the open floor, cutting his employer out of the profit.

**The Widow's Boys** — a loose crew of teenage salvage-runners who climb the scrap-mountains for coin, fast and fearless, and know every hidden crawlspace in the yard.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — nominally Ferrum-leased ground, loosely policed by yard foremen; real authority belongs to whoever controls the crane schedule.

Theft is rampant and mostly tolerated as the cost of doing business; violence is not. A fight in the yard risks bringing a scrap-mountain down, so the foremen respond fast and hard, backed by improvised crane-hooks and a couple of off-books stun-batons. Serious trouble draws Ferrum "asset security" — private muscle more interested in protecting the crane than the people under it.

Sanctum occasionally sends inspectors to appraise "recoverable corp assets" among the salvage; their visits are announced days in advance and universally hated.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Public AR tagging in the yard is decades out of date — half the salvage piles are still marked with the corporate inventory codes of long-defunct owners, a treasure map for anyone who knows how to read dead branding.

A hacker digging past the surface can find Kray's private resale ledger, hidden in a spoofed maintenance-log partition — proof of years of skimming that would end his contract and possibly his life.

Dead-drop tags cluster near the crane's base, used by the Widow's Boys to trade finds without foremen noticing.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Buried Frame. Somewhere in the scrap-mountains is an intact Ironclad combat-frame chassis, dumped after a black-ops job went wrong years ago. Old Han knows roughly where. Several buyers want it before the others find out it exists.

2. Kray's Ledger. Iset Kray's skimming has finally caught Ferrum's attention. He hires the crew to retrieve and destroy evidence of his resale operation before an audit team arrives — evidence that also happens to implicate half the yard's foremen.

3. What the Crane Found. The gantry crane's crew uncovers a body in the scrap — SIN-tagged, corp-affiliated, very recently dead, and very much not supposed to be down in the Flats. Whoever put it there wants it to stay buried.

## Grafthouse Prime

*Best deniable ripperdoc in the Flats*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Grafthouse doesn't advertise. You find it the way everyone finds it — a rumor whispered at the right forge-shed, a scuffed green cross painted over a service door between two chimney-stacks, easy to miss unless you already know to look. Rain drums soft and warm on the corrugated overhang, chemical-slick and faintly luminous where it pools, and above you there's nothing but the sooty underbelly of the level above, close enough that the forge-glow from Forge Row two streets over throws long orange shadows across the wet brick.

A drone loiters on the corner opposite, running lights dimmed to near-invisible — not a threat, just a watcher, logging faces the way every entrance to the Grafthouse is logged by someone who trusts no one. Ground traffic here moves quieter than the rest of the Cinder Market; the wounded don't want witnesses, and a battered ambulance-rig with the marks scraped off its hull sits idling at the mouth of the alley, engine ticking as it cools. Somewhere above, an aircab banks past on a private line, running fast and dark, someone rich enough to skip the Grid entirely on the way to somewhere they'd rather not be seen leaving.

Inside, past the door, the smell changes from ash and ozone to antiseptic and copper — and something older, warmer, almost sacred in how carefully everything is arranged.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Doctor Imre Solveth** — Grafthouse Prime's chief ripperdoc, formerly Caduceus-certified before he was quietly delisted for "unauthorized procedures." The best chrome surgeon south of the Grid, and utterly without corporate loyalty now. Charges high, asks few questions, remembers everyone who's ever lied to him.

**Sister Yeva** — a street-faith initiate who volunteers as Solveth's surgical assistant, treating the work as a kind of ministry; she's the reason the Grafthouse never turns away someone who truly can't pay.

**Renner "Clean" Osei** — the Grafthouse's discreet security and driver, ex-Ironclad, who vets every patient before they're let past the second door and has quietly buried more than one body that came in as a "complication."

#### Security

Zone: Flats — independent, fiercely neutral ground; no gang claims the Grafthouse and every gang needs it too much to try.

Weapons are surrendered at the first door, no exceptions, and Osei's vetting is thorough — anyone who can't explain their injury credibly gets turned away. Trouble inside draws immediate, disproportionate response: this is one of the few places in the Cinder Market where rival gang members set injuries aside at the threshold, because Solveth's neutrality is worth more to everyone than any grudge.

Caduceus occasionally sends "recovery" contractors sniffing for their delisted doctor; Osei has standing orders to warn Solveth the moment corporate credentials show up nearby.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Grafthouse runs entirely dark on the Wired — no SIN-readers, no patient logs on any network, payment taken in scrip or barter and recorded nowhere but Solveth's own head. It is, deliberately, one of the only true blind spots in the district.

A hacker probing the building finds almost nothing — which is itself the tell. Digging into the building's power draw and comparing it to its official (nonexistent) registration reveals the operation, if you know to look for absence rather than presence.

AR tags nearby are scrubbed clean by a standing jammer disguised as a forge-noise suppressor, courtesy of a favor Solveth once did for a technomancer.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Delisting. Caduceus wants Solveth back — or silenced. A "recruiter" approaches the crew with a generous offer to deliver him quietly, dead or alive, to a pickup point at the edge of the district.

2. Complication. A patient dies on Solveth's table — someone important enough that the death can't stay quiet. The crew is hired (by Solveth, or by the dead patient's enemies) to find out whether it was chrome-rejection, sabotage, or murder before Osei's usual cleanup makes the question moot.

3. Sister Yeva's Ledger. Yeva has been quietly treating burned-SIN refugees from deeper in the Warrens for free, logging their true names in a hidden notebook against Solveth's wishes. If that notebook falls into the wrong hands, it's a hunting list.

## The Tool Library

*Rentable heavy fab; Wrench guild*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Tool Library announces itself with a smell — hot metal, cutting oil, and the ozone tang of a dozen fabricators running at once — long before the sign does, a heavy iron placard shaped like a stylized wrench, lit from within by a guttering arc-lamp that's been relit so many times its housing has half-melted into the wall. There is no sky here, only the low black ceiling of the level above, and the rain that finds its way down through service shafts falls in fat, warm, deliberate drops, hissing where it lands on the loading dock's hot pavement.

The building itself used to be three buildings; the Wrench guild knocked the walls between them decades ago and never quite finished reinforcing the result, so the whole structure creaks under its own machinery. Above the loading dock, a tangle of cargo-drone lanes crosses paths with a rickety funicular line that hauls parts up to a mezzanine workshop, while ground-level a queue of handcarts, cargo-crawlers, and one very illegal boosted delivery van wait their turn at the rental counter. Neon strip-lighting, guild-yellow, buzzes along every beam, staining the steam that rises off the dock a sickly gold. The rain drags all of it — yellow, orange, the distant cyan flicker from a rival guild sign three blocks over — down into the wet street in long, smeared streaks.

This is where the Flats' independents come when a job needs a press, a lathe, or a forge too big to own — rented by the hour, guild rules enforced by guild fists.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Guildmaster Denna Iyer** — head of the Wrench guild's Cinder Market chapter, a broad-shouldered Corran matriarch who treats every piece of rented equipment like a loaned family heirloom. Enforces guild rules personally and remembers every debt owed and paid.

**"Torque" Aldous Fenn** — the Library's chief equipment tech, who can coax another decade out of any dying fabricator and moonlights running unlicensed jobs for clients who can't be seen renting official time.

**Ressa Volk** — a young apprentice fast-tracking through the guild ranks, ambitious enough to cut corners on safety protocol if it gets a job done faster, which worries everyone who's worked with her.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Wrench guild territory, tightly self-policed; the guild's authority here is near-absolute on matters of equipment and conduct.

Damage guild property or break guild rental etiquette and you're blacklisted district-wide within a day — a genuinely feared punishment, since no other rental house south of the Grid has this much heavy fabrication capacity. Physical violence draws guild enforcers armed with improvised but brutally effective tools; lethal weapons are rare, but a guild beating with a pipe-wrench is a well-known deterrent.

Ironclad occasionally tries to lease bulk fabrication time for off-books military contracts; Iyer allows it only at extortionate rates and with guild oversight of what's actually being built.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Every piece of equipment is logged obsessively on a private guild network — usage hours, wear, and rental history tracked with an almost religious precision, since the guild's whole business model depends on knowing exactly what's been done with their machines.

A hacker who penetrates the guild ledger can find a detailed record of every job run through the Library for the past several years — a goldmine of blackmail material and evidence of dozens of grey-market fabrication runs.

AR overlay here is guild-branded and aggressively commercial, plastering rental rates and safety warnings across every surface; it's also, unusually, quite accurate.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Ressa's Shortcut. Ressa Volk skipped a mandatory safety lockout on a rental job for an outside client, and the resulting fabrication defect is now installed in a combat-frame currently in the field. The guild wants it quietly recalled before it fails catastrophically — and before anyone learns whose fault it was.

2. The Blacklist. A rival fabrication house is trying to get an independent smith blacklisted from the guild on fabricated evidence of equipment abuse, to strangle their business. The smith hires the crew to prove the sabotage before the guild tribunal convenes.

3. Off the Ledger. Torque Fenn has been running unlicensed jobs after hours for a client who pays well and asks too many technical questions about weapons-grade fabrication tolerances. Iyer suspects Ironclad is testing the guild's limits and wants the crew to find out who Fenn's real client is.

## Piston Yard

*Combat-frame refit & test pit*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You feel Piston Yard before you see it — a rhythmic, bone-deep thud that syncs itself to your pulse, the sound of a combat-frame's hydraulic leg slamming a test-pit floor over and over, forty meters down and rising up through the girders under your feet. There's no sky, of course, just the level above pressing down close and dark, rain sheeting off a maze of gantries and support struts in curtains that catch the arc-lights and turn silver-orange-red as they fall past each colored floodlamp in turn.

The Yard is an old foundry pit repurposed, walls scarred by a hundred failed and successful refits, ringed three stories up by observation catwalks where techs in rain-slick ponchos lean on rails to watch. Overhead, a heavy-lift drone hauls a stripped combat-chassis on cables thick as your arm, swinging it toward an open bay, while a pair of armored ground-rigs idle at the loading ramp, engines grumbling, waiting to haul the next frame in for work. Sparks fountain up from the pit floor bright enough to throw shadows against the rain itself. The noise is total — hydraulic hiss, servo-scream, the crowd's ragged cheering when a test-run frame either walks clean or drops in a shower of failure-sparks — and under it all, that same relentless thud, thud, thud that never quite stops.

Whatever brought you here, it walks on legs now, or it will by the time you leave.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Coach Marrow** — Piston Yard's legendary chief refit engineer, a former Ironclad combat-frame technician who left the corp (or was pushed out) over an unspoken disagreement about "acceptable losses." Runs the test pit like a proving ground and treats every frame like a soldier under his command.

**Little Vey Okonkwo** — the Yard's bookmaker, running odds on test-pit trials and underground frame-duels; connected enough to Nyx to launder the betting money cleanly.

**Sergeant Priya Anand (ret.)** — a decommissioned Ironclad NCO who now moonlights teaching pilots how to actually survive their frames instead of just fight in them; deeply respected, deeply tired of watching kids die in badly refit chassis.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — informally controlled by Coach Marrow's crew, tolerated as neutral testing ground by every gang that needs frames refit.

The pit itself is dangerous by design; Marrow's people manage crowd safety with barked orders and physical barriers rather than weapons. Serious trouble — sabotage, theft of a frame, a fixed duel gone lethal — brings Marrow's refit crew out with whatever heavy tools are closest, and word travels to every gang with a frame currently being serviced, which is most of them.

Ironclad watches the Yard from a distance, uneasy about a former asset training independent pilots to beat their doctrine, but unwilling to move against a facility half the district depends on.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Every test-pit run is recorded and logged for insurance and betting purposes on a semi-public feed, making the Yard one of the most heavily surveilled — and most legally ambiguous — spots in the Cinder Market. Sanctum actuaries occasionally buy footage to model combat-frame failure rates.

A hacker digging into Little Vey's betting ledger can find patterns suggesting certain duels are fixed — valuable blackmail, dangerous knowledge.

AR tags along the catwalks show live odds, frame stats, and pilot rankings, glowing brighter with every hit landed in the pit below.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Fixed Duel. Little Vey has been quietly rigging test-pit duels for a cut of the winnings. A pilot who lost badly — and lost their frame as collateral — wants the crew to prove it before they're forced into debt-service for a corp that "helpfully" bought their marker.

2. Marrow's Ghost Contract. Ironclad wants Coach Marrow back for a black project and is applying pressure through his old NCO, Sergeant Anand, to broker the deal. Marrow asks the crew to find leverage against the corp before Anand is forced to choose between him and her old chain of command.

3. The Frame That Walked Away. A combat-frame undergoing refit vanishes from the Yard overnight — chassis, weapons mounts, and a black-box logging unit that shouldn't have been installed in the first place. Whoever stole it either wants the frame or wants what it recorded.

## Slag Relay

*Pirate broadcast tower; technomancers*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The tower doesn't look like much from the street — a rust-eaten chimney-stack that stopped venting smoke decades ago, wrapped now in a birds'-nest tangle of salvaged antenna and cable that climbs into the dark overhead until it's lost against the black underside of the level above. Warm chemical rain slicks the cabling until it gleams like wet cartilage. No one who doesn't already know would guess that this dead stack is the loudest illegal voice in the Cinder Market.

Getting close, you feel it before you understand it — a faint electric hum in your fillings, in the chrome behind your ear if you're carrying any, a static-prickle that makes the hair on your arms rise. Courier-drones give the tower a wide, instinctive berth, veering off their usual lanes; only the boldest hovercabs cut close overhead, running lights flickering oddly as they pass through whatever field the tower generates. Down at street level, a knot of ground-rigs and battered cargo-crawlers cluster at a respectful distance, engines idling roughly, like animals near something they don't trust. The neon nearby doesn't glow so much as flicker, and rain falling through the tower's aura sometimes seems to fall wrong — slower, or sideways, just for a heartbeat.

Somewhere up in that tangle of dead metal, someone is talking to the Wired the way no corp-sanctioned technician ever could.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Static** — a technomancer of uncertain age and origin who has lived inside the Slag Relay's tower for years, communing with the Wired in ways that unsettle even other technomancers. Broadcasts pirate signal-truth into the district's ad-feeds at unpredictable intervals: corp lies corrected, warnings, sometimes just noise that makes people uneasy in ways they can't explain.

**Kettle Oyelaran** — Static's sole trusted intermediary, a wereraven in human guise who carries messages and salvage parts up the tower and translates Static's increasingly abstract pronouncements into things ordinary people can use.

**Ferro Munez** — a Meridian signal-tech assigned to hunt down the pirate broadcast; competent, quietly sympathetic to the tower's message, and slow-walking his own investigation for reasons he hasn't examined too closely.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — nominally no one's, practically Static's, respected out of a mix of fear and superstition rather than force.

The tower has no guards because it doesn't need any — local wisdom holds that harming Static invites bad luck at best and a worse fate at worst, and enough strange incidents have happened to skeptics that the rumor persists. Meridian occasionally sends technical teams to trace and cut the broadcast; they tend to leave with malfunctioning equipment and no clear memory of why they didn't finish the job.

Anyone the tower doesn't want found simply isn't found; the technomancer's relationship with the local Wired runs deeper than any conventional stealth.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

This is one of the strangest nodes on the Wired in the whole district — a place where the signal doesn't behave. Standard AR tagging glitches, doubles, or shows information that seems to be from a slightly different, slightly wrong version of the district.

A hacker approaching the tower's true network architecture finds something that isn't quite code — patterns that feel more like a conversation than a system, and which respond, unnervingly, to being read.

Dead-drop tags around the tower's base shift position without anyone visibly moving them; Static's chosen contacts always seem to know where to look regardless.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Correction. Static's latest broadcast exposed a Sanctum actuarial scheme quietly reclassifying whole Cinder Market blocks as "high-loss, low-priority" ahead of a planned service cutoff. Corran guild-clans want the crew to verify the leak and turn it into pressure before the cutoff happens.

2. Munez's Choice. Ferro Munez's superiors have noticed his stalled investigation and are sending a harder operative to finish the job. The crew is hired — by Kettle, or by Munez himself — to either protect the tower or help Munez fake a convincing failure that satisfies Meridian.

3. The Wrong Broadcast. A signal goes out from the Slag Relay that Static insists they never sent — coordinates, in a cipher used by a Wired-entity no one wants to think about, pointing toward something waking up deeper in the Sinks.

## The Kiln

*Furnace-district gang throne*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Kiln used to be exactly that — a district furnace-house, its great brick stack still standing forty meters over the block like a watchtower nobody built on purpose. It burns again now, but for different reasons: firelight spills from its lower windows in long orange bars across the wet street, and the rain hisses to steam wherever it lands near the open furnace-doors. There's no sky above any of this, just the low black ceiling of the level overhead, rain dragging the Kiln's orange glow and the sour green of a rival tag sprayed defiantly across the wall into long smeared streaks on the ground.

The approach tells you everything about who's in charge before anyone says a word. Ground-rigs belonging to the gang sit parked in a deliberate, arrogant row across half the street, forcing foot and cargo-crawler traffic into a single narrow lane where every face gets seen. A pair of drones on a short leash — someone's personal, hacked security rig, not corp-issue — circle the stack's upper reaches, running lights a hard, watchful red. Somewhere above, an aircab banks wide around the block entirely, giving the Kiln's territory the same respectful berth every smart pilot in the district has learned to give it.

Music thumps from inside, distorted by distance and brick, layered under the ever-present sub-bass thrum of the Reach's ancient pumps. Whatever business the crew has here, they don't walk in — they're let in, or they don't come out.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ferrik "Furnace" Doss** — boss of the Kiln Boys, the gang that rules the furnace-district's core blocks. Built his reputation by literally throwing a rival into an active furnace-mouth years ago; runs his territory with brutal, personal authority and an odd, genuine respect for the smiths whose trade routes he protects for a cut.

**Coya Reyes** — Doss's second, sharper and more patient than her boss, quietly building her own network of favors against the day she has to take over or take him down.

**The Stoker** — a chem-augmented enforcer who tends the Kiln's ceremonial furnace personally, more feared for the ritual than the violence; disputes are sometimes "settled" by trial-of-fire, a barbaric and effective deterrent.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Kiln Boys' territory, absolutely, and enforced without subtlety.

Unescorted strangers are noticed within a block and questioned within two; disrespect the gang's authority and the response is fast, physical, and public — meant to be seen, as a lesson to everyone else on the street. Alert here means a whistle-relay running block to block faster than any corp system, bringing armed Kiln Boys converging within minutes.

Ironclad occasionally tries to broker "protection contracts" with Doss to secure Forge Row's supply lines; he takes their money and gives nothing resembling loyalty in return, which everyone involved understands and no one says aloud.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Kiln's territory is aggressively tagged in gang-only AR — territorial glyphs, warnings, and a running public ledger of debts owed to Doss, visible to anyone who's done business here before.

A hacker who breaches the gang's private channel finds detailed protection-money records — a map of exactly which businesses on Forge Row and beyond are paying the Kiln Boys, and how much.

Dead-drop tags near the furnace-stack are Coya's private channel, used for deals she doesn't want Doss to know about yet.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Trial of Fire. A local smith refuses to pay the Kiln Boys' protection tax and is challenged to trial-of-fire by the Stoker. The crew is asked — by the smith, or by Coya, hedging her bets — to find a way to save him without breaking gang law outright.

2. Coya's Gambit. Coya Reyes wants Doss gone and offers the crew a substantial cut of the Kiln's territory-tax income if they help her stage his fall in a way that looks like a rival gang's doing.

3. The Ironclad Ledger. Proof surfaces that Doss has been selling Forge Row's fabrication schedules to Ironclad for years, quietly profiting off the same smiths he claims to protect. Whoever exposes it could break the Kiln's grip on the furnace-district — or get buried trying.

## Ember Room

*Simsense den for burned-out techs*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Ember Room's sign is barely a sign at all anymore — a cracked pink neon glyph, half the letters dead, sputtering weakly against the rain that beads and runs down the storefront glass like something crying slow, warm, chemical tears. There's no sky above the alley it sits in, just the underside of the level above pressing low and close, and the rain that finds its way down here seems to fall softer, quieter, as if even the weather knows to lower its voice near this place.

You can hear it before the door opens — a low murmuring hush, the opposite of Forge Row's hammering two streets over, threaded through with faint simsense feedback-whine. Overhead, the usual traffic thins near here; drone couriers and hovercabs alike seem to avoid loitering, giving the block a strange stillness even as ground-rigs rumble past on the main strip beyond. The neon that does work bleeds a deep rose-pink across the wet pavement, mixing uneasily with the distant furnace-orange glow from the district's forges, like two different kinds of fire that never quite touch.

Through the fogged window, you can just make out shapes in reclined chairs, wires trailing from temple-ports, faces slack with borrowed peace. Some of them used to build the district's best chrome. Now they just want to forget what building it cost them.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mora Lindqvist** — the Ember Room's proprietor, a former Grafthouse surgical tech who burned out on the psychic weight of installing chrome into desperate people and now sells a gentler kind of numbness instead. Genuinely cares for her regulars, in her way.

**Doc Feed** — the den's simsense supplier, an Obsidian-adjacent dealer who provides both legitimate tailored-dream chips and BTL bootlegs for those who can't afford the real thing, walking a line that will eventually get him killed.

**Ashen** — a regular who used to be Forge Row's finest drone-frame engineer before the work broke something in him; still sharp enough, between sessions, to be worth a fortune to the right buyer if anyone could get him to leave the chair.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — informally protected by an understanding with the Kiln Boys, who take a small cut and otherwise leave it alone.

The Ember Room avoids trouble by design: Mora enforces a strict no-weapons, no-debt-collection policy inside, backed by two quiet, heavily built doormen who deal with disputes by simply removing people, gently but firmly. Real danger here is rarely violent — it's the slow kind, chip-debt and burnout, and Mora's biggest fear is a batch of bad BTL turning her den into a morgue.

Obsidian occasionally sends "quality control" agents to check Doc Feed's product isn't undercutting their premium lines too badly; so far he's stayed just useful enough to tolerate.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Ember Room keeps a deliberately thin footprint on the Wired — payment is scrip-only, faces aren't logged, and Mora jams SIN-readers at the door with a battered but effective signal scrambler.

A hacker probing deeper finds Doc Feed's supply-chain metadata leaking through an insecure simsense chip's firmware — a trail that leads, eventually, to an Obsidian distribution node further up the strata.

AR near the den is muted and pink-tinted by an ambient calm-filter Mora installed herself, one of the only deliberately soothing overlays in the whole district.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Ashen's Price. A rival fabrication house wants Ashen's engineering knowledge badly enough to pay for his "recovery" — meaning kidnapping him out of the chair and forcing him sober by whatever means necessary. Mora wants the crew to get to him first.

2. Bad Batch. A shipment of Doc Feed's BTL bootlegs turns out to be cut with something that's putting regulars into comas instead of dreams. Mora needs the source found and stopped before Obsidian uses the deaths as an excuse to shut her down entirely.

3. The Quality Control Visit. Obsidian's "quality control" agent stops being polite and starts making real threats against Doc Feed's supply line. The crew is caught between protecting a small-time dealer they've come to know and a corp that doesn't forgive competition.

## Ash Shrine

*Smith's-faith forge-temple*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Ash Shrine rises where three service alleys meet, built from the fused, slag-black remains of a foundry that burned down and was never fully cleared — the smiths who worship here call it holy precisely because it survived its own destruction. No sky watches over it, only the endless dark underside of the level above, and the warm chemical rain that falls elsewhere as a nuisance falls here almost like a rite, hissing to steam against votive braziers kept burning at the shrine's threshold no matter the hour.

You feel the heat first, then see it — dozens of small forge-fires tended by robed attendants, their light throwing huge trembling shadows across walls scarred black and shining where centuries of soot have been polished smooth by touch. Overhead, the usual district traffic gives the shrine a wide, respectful lane; even the boldest courier-drone pilots slow their approach here, and a hovercab or two hangs back at a distance, waiting for a passenger who came to pray rather than deal. Ground-level, the crowd moves slower too, voices dropping from the district's usual roar to something more like murmur, six tongues still, but softer.

At the shrine's heart, in a nave open to the rain, an enormous cracked anvil sits enthroned on a dais, ancient beyond any smith's memory, and offerings of scrap and prayer-chits are piled high around its base. Whatever the Ash Shrine is, it is older than the gangs, older than the Kiln, maybe older than the corps that now own the sky.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Kessa Vantiel** — the Shrine's senior smith-priest, a Corran elder who has tended the sacred anvil for thirty years and remembers a version of the furnace-district before the Kiln Boys carved it up. Offers counsel, blessing-rites for new chrome, and quiet sanctuary to anyone who asks properly.

**Brother Talvo** — a young acolyte and former Ironclad conscript who found the Shrine after deserting; still jumps at loud noises, still learning to trust that no one here will make him fight again.

**The Anvil-Keeper** — an unnamed, ancient chrome-and-flesh figure who maintains the sacred anvil's mechanisms and is rumored, depending on who's telling the story, to be either the Shrine's first priest kept alive by faith and grafts, or something else entirely that simply wears the role.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — sanctuary ground, respected by nearly every faction in the district out of genuine belief or careful superstition.

The Shrine keeps no weapons and needs none; violence on consecrated ground is considered a curse waiting to land, and even hardened Kiln Boys enforcers cross themselves (metaphorically or literally, chrome-augmented as they are) before stepping inside. Anyone who breaks the peace here finds the district's goodwill toward them evaporate fast — Mother Vantiel's disapproval carries real social weight.

Street-faith networks from across the Flats keep loose contact with the Shrine, meaning trouble here echoes far beyond the furnace-district.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired goes strangely quiet near the sacred anvil — not jammed, exactly, just less present, as though the old iron predates the concept of being tracked. Locals call it the anvil's "hush" and consider it proof of something sacred.

A technomancer investigating the hush finds genuine anomalous data-behavior around the anvil, unexplained by any known jamming tech — a mystery even the Shrine's own priests don't fully understand.

AR tags near the entrance mark it, respectfully and universally, as sanctuary ground — one of the few overlay conventions every faction in the district honors without exception.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Talvo's Old Unit. Brother Talvo's former Ironclad squad has tracked him to the furnace-district and wants him back — or silenced as a deserter. Mother Vantiel asks the crew to intervene without breaking the Shrine's sanctuary by spilling blood on its ground.

2. The Anvil's Hush. A Meridian data-survey team wants to study the anomalous quiet around the sacred anvil, offering the Shrine significant nuyen for "access." Vantiel is torn between the community's needs and fear of what corporate study might disturb.

3. Sanctuary Broken. Someone violates the Shrine's sanctuary — a killing on consecrated ground, staged to look like it happened elsewhere. The Shrine's moral authority across the Flats depends on the truth coming out, and Vantiel turns to the crew because she trusts no one closer to the crime.

## Cold Store

*Refrigerated contraband vaults*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Cold Store doesn't look like it belongs in a district built on heat — a squat, windowless block wrapped in frost-slick insulated paneling, steam boiling off its exterior vents in a way that has nothing to do with the district's usual forge-heat and everything to do with what's kept cold inside. There's no sky above it, same as everywhere, just the black ceiling of the level overhead and the warm chemical rain falling steady onto a roof engineered to shed it fast before it can compromise the seals below.

The approach feels wrong in a way you can't quite name until you place it: the air itself is colder here, a strange pocket of chill bleeding out from refrigeration units running hard against the ambient furnace-warmth of the whole Cinder Market. Overhead, delivery drones queue in a tight holding pattern, waiting their loading-dock slot with unusual discipline; a couple of unmarked cargo-crawlers idle at the dock itself, engines running just to keep their own cargo cold on the last leg of the journey. Neon here is sparse and utilitarian, sodium-yellow warning strips more than decoration, and what light there is gets swallowed fast by the rain and the vent-steam into a general gray murk.

Whatever's inside is worth keeping cold, worth keeping quiet, and worth the very particular kind of nervous energy radiating off everyone loitering near the loading dock, pretending they're not watching the door.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ilse Novak** — Cold Store's manager of record, officially just a refrigerated-logistics contractor; unofficially a senior Nyx logistics broker who treats the vaults as a trust built over decades and guards that trust ruthlessly.

**Dr. Amel Fasso** — a Caduceus-trained biologist working off the books, contracted to keep the vault's more delicate biological contraband — grafts, cloned tissue, experimental compounds — viable in transit.

**Grinder** — Cold Store's chief loading-dock enforcer, a hulking ex-dockworker with cold-weather grafts of his own, who has personally never lost a shipment and takes the record very seriously.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Nyx-controlled ground, run with corporate-grade discipline rather than street-gang chaos.

Security is understated but real: biometric locks, a small permanent security detail, and an absolute rule that nothing enters or leaves without Novak's sign-off. Theft or tampering triggers a fast, professional, and quiet response — Nyx doesn't want a public spectacle any more than it wants product lost, so trouble tends to be handled with efficient violence well away from witnesses.

Sanctum inspectors occasionally sniff around, suspecting (correctly) that the "refrigerated logistics" cover doesn't match the volume of traffic; Novak's paperwork has, so far, always been just clean enough.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Cold Store's public-facing systems are boringly, deliberately legitimate — a logistics company's ordinary inventory feed, clean and unremarkable to a casual scan.

A hacker who pushes past the cover finds a second, hidden inventory system tracking the vault's actual contents: grafts, black-market pharma, biological samples too sensitive for any legal manifest, cross-referenced with buyer codes instead of names.

Dead-drop tags near the loading dock rotate on a tight schedule known only to trusted couriers; showing up at the wrong tag at the wrong time marks you as someone worth questioning.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Cold Chain Break. A refrigeration failure threatens a shipment of viable cloned graft-tissue worth a fortune — and worth someone's life if it spoils before a scheduled transplant. Dr. Fasso needs discreet help moving it to backup cold storage across the district before it's ruined.

2. Novak's Trust. A shipment goes missing from Cold Store's ledger with no sign of forced entry, meaning the theft was almost certainly internal. Novak hires the crew quietly, trusting no one on her own staff, to find the leak before Nyx's higher-ups start asking why her decades of clean record just ended.

3. The Sanctum Audit. A Sanctum inspection team finally gets a warrant that actually sticks, threatening to expose Cold Store's real inventory. Novak needs the vault's contents moved, hidden, or destroyed within hours — and needs hands she can trust not to ask what's in the crates.
