# Critters of the Reach

*The Non-Humanoid Life of the Reach*

The hive is not empty between the people. It crawls, it flutters, it swarms, and — in the Wired — it hunts. This chapter collects the non-humanoid life of the Reach: the vermin, beasts, and machine-things of the physical world (the **Prime**), and the sprites, ICE, and data-predators of the digital one (the **Wire**). None of these are people; all of them are encounters.

## The Prime — Physical Critters

### Chrome-Rat

*Species/Type: [beast, vermin] — engineered rodent, feral*

A wererat's lesser cousin that never got the gift of shape — just the augments. Corp labs seeded the Warrens with cyber-enhanced rodents for waste-cycling generations ago, and they bred true and hungry: matte-metal teeth, a data-sniffing nose that homes on live wiring, and a swarming boldness that makes them the single most common critter in the Flats. Alone, a nuisance. In numbers, they strip a body — or a comm-relay — to the frame.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Chrome-Rat).*

**Hook.** The cheapest way to start a session in the dark. If the crew's mission depends on a device, the rats found it first.

### Tunnel-Bat

*Species/Type: [beast] — echolocating cave-flyer, Warrens-adapted*

Where there's a shaft, there's a roost. Tunnel-bats hang in the Reach's countless vertical dead-spaces, navigating the no-sky dark by a screaming echolocation that fritzes cheap AR rigs. Harmless until disturbed — and everything disturbs them. A crew's gunfire, a dropped flare, a bad landing off a jump: any of it can bring a living ceiling down in a shrieking, blinding cloud.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Tunnel-Bat).*

**Hook.** Not a threat so much as an alarm with teeth. Wake the roost and every hostile in earshot knows exactly where the crew is.

### Scrap-Hound

*Species/Type: [beast] — feral augmented guard-dog (Sprawl Cur strain)*

The descendants of corporate guard-hounds gone to ground, chrome-scarred and pack-smart. Where the Sprawl Cur roams the mid-Flats, the Scrap-Hound is its meaner Warrens variant: jaw-plated, wired for pack coordination, and territorial to the death. They den in collapsed maintenance levels and answer an alpha's call as one animal.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Scrap-Hound).*

**Hook.** Someone's been feeding them. Find out who, and why they wanted this level guarded.

### Sink-Crawler

*Species/Type: [beast, mutant] — Veil-touched deep-dweller*

Down in the Sinks, where Ferrum's engineers stopped digging and started lying, the Dark has settled into the living things. The Sink-Crawler is what a blind cave-scavenger becomes after generations of drinking corruption: pallid, wrong-jointed, silent, and far too large. It does not hunt so much as wait, and the first sign of one is usually the absence of every other critter.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Sink-Crawler).*

**Hook.** The deeper the crew goes, the older and bigger these get. The one that took the last crew is still down there.

### Gutter-Serpent

*Species/Type: [beast] — augmented drainage predator*

Slackwater's overflow channels and the Spillway's flood-runs are home to these long, oil-slick ambush hunters — part eel, part reptile, wholly opportunistic. They lie submerged in filthy water with only a sensor-frill breaking the surface, and they strike anything that crosses a channel or lingers on a dock.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Gutter-Serpent).*

**Hook.** The body in the channel wasn't murdered. Probably. Getting the cargo back means going in the water with it.

## The Wild (Beyond the Gate) — Apex Critters

### Canopy-Stalker

*Species/Type: [beast] — primeval jungle apex predator*

Past Cinderhold's Outer Wall, the huge trees hide the reason the gate closes at full-dark. The Canopy-Stalker is a jungle apex the size of a hovercab — a fast, feathered, hooked-claw pack-hunter that drops from the canopy onto anything crossing the game-trails. The bone-piles in the wild clearings are its table-scraps.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Canopy-Stalker).*

**Hook.** Longtooth's caravan didn't come back. The Rangers found the wagon, and only the wagon.

### Reach Behemoth

*Species/Type: [beast, apex] — dinosaur-scale megafauna, "and worse"*

The thing the wall was built for. Vehicle-scale primeval megafauna roam the deep jungle — herd-beasts and the horrors that eat them — and when one strays toward Cinderhold's Outer Wall, the wall alarm sounds and every hand reaches for a weapon. Most crews will never fight one and live. That is the correct instinct.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Reach Behemoth).*

**Hook.** This is not an encounter to win. It's an encounter to survive — bar the gate, break line of sight, and pray it loses interest before dawn.

## The Wire — Digital Critters

The Wired is an ecology too. Not everything in the Overlay was written by a corp; much of it drifts, breeds, and hunts on its own. These are encounters for a decker or technomancer riding the deep net — resolved in the Wired's own space, though a jacked-in runner's meat body pays the price for losing. Species/Type tags run [construct] for made things and [spirit] for Veil-touched data-life.

### Data-Sprite

*Species/Type: [spirit] — free-drifting Wired micro-entity*

The Wired's equivalent of insects — tiny, semi-sentient scraps of orphaned code that flock through the Overlay feeding on stray data. Mostly harmless, occasionally useful (a technomancer can shoo or herd them), and a reliable sign of the local data-weather: where sprites flock thick, something rich is leaking; where they flee, something is hunting.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary.*

**Hook.** The sprites are all fleeing one direction. Whatever they're running from is coming up the same channel the crew's decker is on.

### Wisp

*Species/Type: [spirit] — residual data-ghost*

When data dies badly — a wiped SIN, a murdered decker's last session, a corp's shredded archive — it can leave a Wisp: a mournful, half-coherent echo that haunts the node where it died, replaying fragments. Not hostile, but not safe: a Wisp will latch onto a passing runner and feed off their signal to stay coherent, dragging on their actions like a data-anchor.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary.*

**Hook.** The Wisp is replaying the last moments of exactly the person the crew was hired to find. It knows something. It just can't stop screaming it.

### Watchdog ICE

*Species/Type: [construct] — standard corporate intrusion countermeasure*

The Wired's guard-dog. Every corp node of value runs Watchdog ICE — patrol routines that pace the datascape, sniff for intruder signatures, and raise the Alert when they catch one. Non-lethal by design (the corp wants you traced and arrested, not dead), but relentless, and the thing that summons the nastier ICE behind it.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Watchdog ICE).*

**Hook.** It already saw the decker. The clock is running. Do you kill it fast, or spoof it before it finishes the trace?

### Scrambler ICE

*Species/Type: [construct] — disruptive countermeasure*

The mid-tier defense: aggressive ICE built to break a runner's concentration and corrupt their tools rather than trace them. Scramblers manifest as jagged, strobing geometry and attack the decker's own gear — glitching cyberdecks, scrambling loaded programs, and inducing the disorientation that gets sloppy runners flatlined.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Scrambler ICE).*

**Hook.** It's not trying to trace the crew's decker — it's trying to strand them in the node long enough for something worse to arrive.

### Black ICE

*Species/Type: [construct] — lethal countermeasure*

The line corps aren't supposed to cross, and cross anyway on their most valuable nodes. Black ICE is built to kill — to reach back down the connection and stop the intruder's heart, fry their brain, or flatline the deck with the runner still jacked in. Encountering Black ICE means the crew hit something a corp values more than the law. It is the Wired's apex predator, and it does not bluff.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary (Black ICE).*

**Hook.** The score is real, then. Nobody guards nothing with Black ICE. Whatever's behind this wall is worth a life — is it worth the decker's?

### Ghost-in-the-Wire

*Species/Type: [spirit, awakened] — emergent Veil-touched intelligence*

The rarest and strangest Wire critter: an emergent intelligence born where the Wired runs too close to the Veil, neither corp construct nor summoned spirit but something grown in the deep net. A technomancer's holy grail and worst nightmare — it can be communed with, bargained with, or antagonized, and it remembers. Some technomancers whisper that the deep Wired past Cinderhold is thick with them, and that they are getting louder.

*Stats: see the Ghostwire Bestiary.*

**Hook.** It doesn't want to kill the crew's decker. It wants something — a memory, a favor, a way out of the deep Wired. What it offers in return could make or unmake the whole run.
