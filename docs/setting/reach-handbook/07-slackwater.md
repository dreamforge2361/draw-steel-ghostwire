# Slackwater

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // The Flooded Docks*

## The Long Pier

*Nyx smuggling wharf; goods up from the Sinks*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The road runs out before the district does. Where the last dry rockcrete cracks and slumps into black water, the Long Pier keeps going anyway — six hundred meters of grey ferrocrete on drowned pilings, walking out over the cistern-sea until its far end is just a smear of running lights in the rain-haze. There is no sky above it, only the sagging underside of the level above, a ceiling of pipe and conduit dripping that warm chemical rain in long silver threads that the wind off the black water bends sideways. The lights don't help you see far — cyan sodium lamps strung on sagging cable, cargo-drones stacked three deep in holding patterns over the loading cranes, and below them a slow parade of flat-bottomed barges nosing in along the pilings, engines burbling low so as not to wake anything sleeping in the water.

You feel it in your teeth before you're halfway out: the pier hums, a bone-deep vibration from something working far beneath the boards, down where the water goes solid black and the charts stop pretending to know the bottom. Ground-rigs with reinforced treads roll past you on the outer lanes, hauling pallets nobody labeled; overhead a courier-drone drops altitude to skim the crowd, reading faces it isn't supposed to be reading. Voices carry strange out here — flattened by the water, swallowed by the rain — so the deal happening ten feet from you sounds like a whisper from a mile off, and the one forty feet away sounds like it's in your ear.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Old Rae Voss** — Nyx wharfinger who's run the Long Pier's manifest book for eleven years; decides which barges dock and which mysteriously develop engine trouble two piers down. Takes a cut of everything, reports the interesting cuts upward.

**Ferro "Ninefinger" Okoye** — dockhand crew boss, technically Ferrum payroll, actually loyal to whoever's paying this week. Knows every smuggler's rhythm on the pier because he's helped run half of them.

**Sable** — a Wereraven who roosts in the crane gantries, watching the water for things surfacing that shouldn't. Trades what she sees to Nyx for fish and quiet.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Nyx-controlled wharf, tolerated by Ferrum's dock authority in exchange for a cut nobody writes down.

Nyx muscle patrols in pairs, unarmored, unremarkable-looking — the point is that you don't clock them until they've already clocked you. Weapons stay holstered; disputes get settled by quiet word to Voss, who settles them by taking your dock privileges. Real violence draws Nyx enforcers from the barges within minutes, and they come by water, which most Edgerunners forget to watch.

**Alert** triggers on unauthorized cargo scans or Ferrum inspectors showing up uninvited — the whole pier goes quiet and cargo starts vanishing into the water on quick-release rigs before anyone official gets close.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Public AR shows a defunct Ferrum cargo terminal, half its tags broken, nothing to see. It's a lie of omission — Nyx runs a shadow-ledger under the surface, barge manifests encoded in fishing-license metadata that reads as noise to anyone without the cipher.

SIN-readers exist at the shore checkpoint only; the pier itself, past that line, is a dead zone by design — cameras "malfunction" on a rolling schedule that Voss controls.

Dead-drops are physical, not digital: a specific bollard, a specific crane hook, chalk marks in six colors that change meaning weekly.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Manifest Nobody Signed. A barge surfaces at the Long Pier with cargo that isn't on Voss's book and isn't from any crew she recognizes — meaning something from deeper than the Sinks found its own way up. She needs deniable hands to find out what, quietly, before Nyx leadership notices she lost control of her own dock.

2. Ninefinger's Debt. Okoye owes a debt to a Cinderhold crew he can't pay in scrip. He wants the crew to move one "quiet" shipment past Nyx's own toll — a betrayal that, if discovered, could cost him his hands and them their standing on every Nyx wharf in the Reach.

3. What Sable Saw. The wereraven scout has spotted something rising through the black water near the pier's far end at night — not a barge, not fish. She'll trade the sighting for a favor, but whoever hired her to watch may already know the crew is asking.

## Drowned Market

*Half-flooded warehouse bazaar*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The warehouse doors are gone — torn off decades ago and never replaced — so the Drowned Market spills its light and noise straight out into the flooded street like a wound that won't close. Knee-deep black water fills the old loading floor, lit from below by submerged strip-lumens that turn every wake into a ribbon of gold and sick green, and across that shallow lake a hundred stalls stand on stilts and salvaged pallets, close enough to touch, far enough apart that you have to wade or hop the plank-bridges between them. There's no sky here, only the warehouse's own sagging roof-vault two stories up, and above that the endless grey underside of the district stacked overhead, weeping its rain down through skylight-holes that someone long ago gave up patching, so it rains inside the market same as out.

Hoverdollies glide low over the water ferrying crates between stalls, their downdraft kicking up a fine chemical mist; somewhere above the roofline a cargo-hovercraft idles on its fans, waiting its turn at a hatch too small for it, engines a bass note under everything else. The sound-wall here is thick and close: six languages of haggling, the slosh of boots and wheels through standing water, a busted vox-speaker looping the same three bars of a jingle for a product that stopped existing years ago. Neon signage reflects double — once in the air, once in the flood beneath your feet — so the whole market seems to hang suspended between two skies that are both, in the end, just more water.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Ilse** — matriarch of the stall-families, unaffiliated with any conglomerate by fierce insistence; settles disputes with a ladle and absolute authority. Everyone owes her a favor or fears becoming the example.

**Petro Quill** — a Corran guild-clan tech-fence who sells salvaged Meridian relay gear "recovered" from flood damage that was, in several documented cases, arranged.

**Dassa Wynn** — runs the only stall with a working freshwater still; sells clean water at a price that's either extortion or mercy depending who's asking, and both answers are true.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — self-governed by the stall-families under Mother Ilse's informal court; no gang holds it outright because no gang wants the maintenance headache of a market that floods on a schedule.

Disputes are handled socially first — Ilse's word, backed by the collective muscle of everyone who doesn't want their stall burned next. Drawn weapons over standing water is a bad idea for everyone including the shooter; most who try slip.

Real trouble — Ferrum trying to condemn the block, Sanctum auditors sniffing for untaxed trade — draws the whole market's quiet non-cooperation: nobody sees anything, nobody remembers a face.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

AR tags every stall with cheerful, slightly-outdated pricing and a permit number that half the stalls never actually filed. It's a market pretending to be more legal than it is, and everyone plays along.

A hacker who dips into the local mesh finds it's stitched together from a dozen scavenged relay nodes, patched by Petro Quill, carrying more than commerce — private message threads ride the same signal, hidden in the noise of transaction spam.

Dead-air pockets bloom wherever the flood-water shorts a relay, which is often, and locals have learned to use those blackout seconds to say what they don't want logged.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Still's Secret. Dassa Wynn's "freshwater" still is producing suspiciously pure output for something jury-rigged from scrap — because it's tapping a Caduceus runoff line no one's supposed to know exists. Someone from Caduceus wants it found and shut before word spreads.

2. Petro's Ghost Relay. One of Quill's salvaged Meridian nodes never stopped listening for its old masters. It's quietly reporting the market's private traffic upstream, and the fallout — for Ilse, for everyone whose secrets rode that mesh — could be brutal.

3. The Ladle and the Ledger. Mother Ilse asks the crew to find who's been paying stall-families to quietly abandon the market's north corner — a slow, deniable land-grab that smells like Ferrum clearing ground for something bigger, and closer to the water than anyone's zoning allows.

## The Sinkmouth

*Sealed channel down to the Sinks*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

There is a place in Slackwater where the black water doesn't stop at the seawall — it goes down, through a channel wide enough for a barge, sealed at its throat by a blast door three stories tall and stained the color of old rust and older blood. This is the Sinkmouth, and everyone who works the docks learns to lower their voice near it without ever being told why. There's no sky above the channel, just the raw underbelly of the district stacked overhead, dripping its warm chemical rain onto water that steams faintly where it meets something warmer welling up from below. Drones don't linger here; they cross the airspace fast and low, like they don't like it either. A single ground-crane stands sentinel over the door's winch mechanism, unused for longer than anyone present has been alive, its cyan running-lights the only clean color in a frame of corroded gold and dying neon signage nobody's replaced because nobody wants the job of standing here that long.

The sound-wall thins out near the door — the market noise and dock-chatter fall away behind you, and what's left is water lapping at rusted plate, a distant metal groan from deep in the mechanism, and your own pulse, suddenly very audible. Somewhere below that door is the last dry step before the Sinks, and the Sinks don't officially exist on any surface manifest. Every Edgerunner who's ever stood on this threshold has felt the same thing: the tower's whole thousand years of light and noise and traffic pressing down from above, and beneath your boots, nothing but dark, waiting.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Warden Iona Meck** — Nyx's appointed keeper of the Sinkmouth locks; controls who and what passes the door, for a price that always includes information. Answers to Nyx leadership directly, which makes her untouchable by local muscle.

**The Tideclerk** — an unnervingly calm Sanctum actuarial agent who logs "unofficial" transit through the Sinkmouth for risk-modeling purposes that no one has ever gotten a straight answer about.

**Brother Ossa** — a street-faith vigil-keeper who sits by the door most nights, praying over anyone who goes down and lighting a candle for the ones who don't come back up.

#### Security

Zone: Flats, at the absolute edge of it — Nyx holds the door and the traffic through it; beyond the seal is understood to belong to no one and nothing friendly.

The blast door only opens for Warden Meck's authorization, cycled through mechanical locks deliberately kept offline from the Wired — nothing about the Sinkmouth's operation can be hacked from a distance, by design. Getting past it uninvited means going through Meck, or through the black water around the seawall's edge, which has its own reasons to be avoided.

**Alert** here escalates fast and grim: unauthorized attempts on the door bring Nyx enforcers who assume the worst — that someone's trying to open a way for something to come up.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer shows nothing at all — a certified dead zone, flagged on every civic map as "flood infrastructure, non-operational." That flag has not been updated in decades and everyone treats it as gospel anyway.

A hacker who forces a connection near the door finds an old, isolated Meridian relay stub still transmitting a single looping ping into the dark below — a signal check that's never once, in the logs anyone can access, gotten a reply.

Dead-drops cluster at Brother Ossa's vigil point — messages left for people who went down and are hoped to come back.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Unanswered Ping. That old relay stub just received a reply for the first time in recorded memory. Meridian wants deniable hands to find out what answered — and Nyx wants to know first, and separately, and quietly.

2. Meck's Missing Manifest. Someone who went down through the Sinkmouth on Warden Meck's authorization never should have had it — a name on the transit log that traces back to a conglomerate with no business near the Sinks. She needs it erased or explained before her own bosses ask why.

3. Brother Ossa's Vigil. A candle Ossa lit for someone lost below has started, impossibly, to relight itself each time it's snuffed. He asks the crew to go down just far enough to learn if it means someone's still alive down there — or if something is using his grief as a door.

## Salt Cranes

*Cargo-lift yards; Ferrum dock-works*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Salt Cranes rise out of the flood like the skeleton of something enormous that died mid-stride — forty meters of corroded gantry-crane strung in a ragged line along the shore, each one still working, still hauling, still owned. There's no sky above the yard, only the low iron ceiling of the level overhead, and the chemical rain comes down through the crane lattices in patterned drips that hit the water in a rhythm almost like music if you squint your ears. Salt-crust and rust-bloom paint every strut gold-brown under the sodium lights, and the whole yard smells of brine, ozone, and hot hydraulic fluid. Cargo containers swing overhead on groaning cables while ground-rigs queue beneath them, drivers leaning out cab windows to shout coordinates up at operators who can't hear them over their own machinery, and a Ferrum inspection hovercraft drifts along the crane-line at a lazy, proprietary altitude, running scans nobody on the ground bothers pretending to ignore.

You feel the yard's whole operation as a kind of violence made procedural — the groan of chain, the slam of container corners against container corners, the constant threat of forty meters of falling steel turned into a Tuesday. Dockworkers move through it with the practiced calm of people who've made peace with the odds. Ferrum badges are everywhere: on jackets, on crane cabs, on the checkpoint booth where a bored guard checks manifests against a ledger that always seems to know more than it should about who's really shipping what.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Foreman Dez Kalb** — Ferrum's yard boss, twenty years in, missing two fingers to prove it; runs a tight legal operation on the surface and looks the other way for the right price underneath.

**Ase Thorn** — crane operator and secret Nyx spotter, radios cargo weights and routes to Long Pier contacts before Ferrum's own logs finish processing them.

**Inspector Vale Corr** — a young, ambitious Sanctum risk-auditor who's noticed the yard's numbers don't add up and hasn't decided yet whether reporting it is worth what it'll cost her.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Ferrum's dock-works authority, the closest thing to formal corp presence in Slackwater; armed, badged, procedural.

Ferrum guards are professional and visible: checkpoint booths, manifest scans, patrol walks along the crane-line. They respond to violence with overwhelming, contracted-out Ironclad muscle rather than their own hands — Ferrum pays for force, doesn't provide it.

**Alert** here means a lockdown of the crane-line and a full manifest audit — bad for everyone with something to hide, which in Salt Cranes is nearly everyone.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Dense, official, and thoroughly surveilled — SIN-readers at every checkpoint, crane telemetry broadcasting constantly, Ferrum's ledger visible (in redacted form) to anyone with dock clearance.

A hacker who gets past the redactions finds two ledgers: the one Ferrum shows Sanctum, and the real one, where certain containers' weights and destinations quietly don't match — Ase Thorn's fingerprints are all over the discrepancy if you know where to look.

AR overlay tags every crane with live load capacity and safety warnings; a good rigger reads it like a second set of eyes and knows exactly which crane is one bad cable away from a very public accident.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Second Ledger. Inspector Corr has found the discrepancy and doesn't know yet that Ase Thorn is Nyx's man on the crane-line. She'll pay well for proof — or Nyx will pay better to make sure she never gets it.

2. A Very Public Accident. Someone wants a specific crane to fail during a specific lift, burying cargo, evidence, or a person under a few hundred tons of container. The crew's hired to make it look natural — or to stop it from happening to someone they know.

3. Kalb's Quiet Arrangement. Foreman Kalb's decades of looking the other way have made him rich and comfortable — and now Ferrum corporate wants to audit the yard properly, which means his arrangement with Nyx either gets buried deeper or gets him buried.

## The Wet Chapel

*Drowned-shrine; dockworker faith*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Half the Wet Chapel is underwater and everyone insists that's the point. It was a transit substation once, and when Slackwater flooded for good, the dockworkers didn't abandon it — they consecrated it, hanging lumen-candles from the drowned rafters and building a floating altar-raft that bobs gently in the black water where the old service floor used to be. There's no sky above the chapel's broken dome, only the underside of the level above showing through a wide, jagged skylight-wound, rain falling steady and warm through the gap to patter on the water like a congregation's own quiet applause. Outside, the district's noise goes on — drone-whine, hovercab horns, the distant groan of the Salt Cranes — but stepping through the chapel's ruined doorway drops the volume like a hand closing over your ear, leaving mostly the drip of rain and the soft slosh of the altar-raft's chain.

A handful of dockworkers kneel at the water's edge in wet coveralls straight off shift, heads bowed under candlelight that reflects gold and green across the flood. Someone is singing, low, in a Corran work-chant older than the chapel itself. It's easy, standing in that doorway with the district's chaos at your back and the water's stillness ahead, to understand why people down here need somewhere to believe in something that isn't a corp ledger — and why the crew, whatever they came here for, should probably lower their voice before they ask for it.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Sister Maren Voss** — street-faith keeper of the Wet Chapel (no known relation to Old Rae Voss, a fact she's tired of explaining); runs the soup line, the washing rite, and a quiet network of dockworkers who tell her things they'd never tell a corp or a cop.

**Brother Callum** — a former Ironclad contractor turned penitent groundskeeper, keeps the altar-raft's chain in good repair and keeps his old skills sharper than he admits.

**Ninny Voss-Karr** — a child of the docks who "sees things in the water" and whom half the congregation treats as touched by something and the other half treats as in need of a ripperdoc.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — unclaimed by any gang; the chapel survives on the same unwritten guarantee that protects Cassavir's Switchboard, but smaller and more fragile.

No weapons, no muscle — the chapel's only defense is its own reputation. Dockworkers from every crew, gang, and faction pass through here, and violence against the sanctuary is a fast way to become unwelcome on every pier in Slackwater.

**Alert** essentially doesn't apply in the conventional sense; the real danger is social — earning Sister Voss's distrust closes doors across the whole dockworker community, quietly and permanently.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer shows an unremarkable, deconsecrated transit substation — no tags, no ads, a rare patch of visual silence in Slackwater's neon wall.

A hacker digging deeper finds the chapel's real infrastructure is a dead-drop network disguised as prayer requests — written petitions left at the altar that Sister Voss (or someone using her sanctuary) relays onward, unlogged.

Ninny's "visions" occasionally correlate, disturbingly, with real signal bleed from the Sinkmouth's old relay stub — enough that a technomancer visiting the chapel might feel something respond to her that has no business responding to anyone.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Petition Wall. Someone has been using the chapel's prayer-petitions to pass real intelligence — ship movements, names, warnings — and it's just gotten a dockworker killed. Sister Voss wants it found and stopped before it costs her the sanctuary itself.

2. What Ninny Sees. The girl's latest vision described, in disturbing detail, a place and an event that hasn't happened yet — matching a job Nyx or Meridian is already planning near the Sinkmouth. Someone will pay handsomely to know if she's psychic, wired, or something the Wired itself is reaching through.

3. Callum's Old Contract. A face from Brother Callum's Ironclad past shows up at the chapel looking for him, not to reminisce — a debt or a death warrant from his mercenary days, and Sister Voss won't let it become violence on her floor if she can help it.

## Fishgut Row

*Protein-vat stalls & grey food trade*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Fishgut Row announces itself long before you see it: a wet, briny, faintly sweet reek of vat-protein and grey-market seafood that clings to the rain and won't let go. It's a single long street of stalls and lean-to kitchens built into the shells of old dockside warehouses, awnings sagging under the weight of standing water, cook-fires hissing every time a drip finds them. There's no sky here — just the district's low ceiling weeping its warm chemical rain onto sizzling grills, steam rising to meet it halfway and hanging in sheets of gold-lit fog under the neon. A hover-fryer rig, somebody's whole mobile kitchen slung under drone-lift fans, threads low overhead between the awnings, ferrying orders to workers on the Salt Cranes who can't leave their shift; ground-carts loaded with vat-tanks rattle along the row's spine, wheels throwing up black water in fantails behind them.

The sound here is appetite made audible — sizzle, chop, the clatter of ladles on steel, vendors shouting orders and prices over each other in Corran-inflected slang thick enough to need translating. Somewhere under the counter of every third stall is a second menu, unwritten, for things that didn't come from any licensed vat — real fish, real meat, pulled up through channels nobody asks about, sold to those who know to ask for it by the right made-up name. The crew's nose finds it before their eyes do: this is where the docks feed themselves, licensed and not, and everyone eating here already knows which parts of dinner not to ask about.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Big Tanaya** — Row matriarch, runs the largest legal vat-stall and, more quietly, the network that moves the illegal catch through everyone else's kitchens; feeds anyone who's hungry regardless of debt, and remembers every favor owed.

**Ru Petrelli** — a grey-market fishmonger who buys directly off Long Pier barges, no questions, and sells "off-manifest" catch to those who know his stall's second menu.

**Corvin Dace** — a Caduceus-trained field medic gone independent, technically inspecting stalls for contamination, actually running a cheap clinic out of a back room for dockworkers who can't afford Caduceus prices.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — informally governed by the Row's vendor collective under Big Tanaya's loose authority; no single gang holds it because feeding people is bad business to disrupt.

Disputes get settled with fists or ladles before they escalate to guns; the Row's density and constant foot traffic makes gunplay a good way to hit six bystanders and earn Tanaya's permanent enmity.

**Alert** spikes when Sanctum health inspectors show up unannounced — the illegal catch vanishes into hidden coolers in under a minute, a drill every vendor has practiced.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

AR tags every stall with cheerful menus, health ratings, and prices — mostly accurate for the legal half of the business, silent on the rest.

A hacker who checks Tanaya's supply chain against Long Pier's shadow-ledger finds them lined up almost perfectly — the Row is effectively the retail end of Nyx's smuggled catch, laundered through a hundred small honest-looking transactions.

Dead-drops here are edible: coded orders ("the usual, extra spice") that tell a vendor exactly what's really being requested, no digital trace at all.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Bad Catch. Something pulled up through the Long Pier's channels and sold on the Row's second menu is making people sick in ways Corvin Dace can't explain — and can't treat. Tracing it back means asking questions at the Sinkmouth that nobody wants asked.

2. Tanaya's Favor Owed. Big Tanaya calls in an old debt from the crew (real or invented by a rival to frame them) — deliver a "food order" across district lines that's clearly not food, for reasons she won't explain and they'll regret not asking about sooner.

3. The Inspector's Price. Corvin Dace has evidence the Row's illegal catch carries a contaminant traced to something disposed of near the Sinkmouth — and Caduceus corporate would pay very well to bury that finding rather than investigate it.

## The Barge

*Floating Obsidian pleasure-hulk*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Barge doesn't dock so much as loom, a converted cargo-hulk the size of a city block riding low and lit on the black water past the Salt Cranes, strung end to end in pink and violet neon that bleeds long trembling reflections across the flood. There's no sky above it — just the district's grey underbelly and the endless warm rain, but up here the rain barely matters; it beads and runs off a forcefield-canopy stretched across the upper deck, turning the whole hulk into a glowing dome of dry warmth surrounded by wet, cold dark. Hovercabs and private skiffs queue at the boarding ramp in a lazy, glittering line, their running-lights doubled in the water below; overhead, a security drone-swarm patrols in slow figure-eights, less interested in traffic than in guests who might be someone's rival before they're anyone's customer.

Music leaks out across the water before you're close enough to see faces — a bass-heavy pulse under strings of laughter and simsense-vendor patter, all of it slightly too loud, slightly too bright, the way everything Obsidian sells is built to feel like more fun than it is. Bouncers built like Ironclad surplus stand at the ramp in tailored black, scanning every approaching craft with an ease that says they already know who's on the guest list and who's about to have a very short, very polite conversation about turning back. The Barge is the one warm, dry, gorgeous thing in all of Slackwater's flooded dark — and everyone who works the docks knows exactly what that beauty costs somebody, somewhere, every single night.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Madam Iset Calloway** — the Barge's proprietress, an Obsidian sub-license holder with old debts to the Velvet Room she's still paying off in favors and silence; charming, dangerous, never raises her voice.

**Dr. Vell Ashwin** — house simsense curator, designs "tailored dreams" for high-paying guests and occasionally for interrogation purposes Calloway doesn't put on the books.

**Kesh Oraya** — head of boarding security, ex-Ironclad, runs the ramp like a checkpoint and the guest list like scripture; nothing gets aboard she hasn't cleared twice.

#### Security

Zone: Flats (floating, technically unmoored from any land jurisdiction) — Obsidian-run private security, among the best-equipped in the district.

Weapons are checked at the ramp, discreetly, by staff who know how to disarm a guest without embarrassing them in front of their date. Trouble aboard gets handled fast, quietly, and out of sight — problem guests simply don't reappear on the return skiff manifest.

**Alert** aboard the Barge means a full lockdown of the boarding ramp and forcefield canopy sealing shut — nobody leaves until Kesh's people decide the problem's solved.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Barge projects a lush, curated AR façade — menus of experiences, guest reviews, mood-lit advertising layered thick as the real neon. It's the densest, glossiest overlay in Slackwater, deliberately drowning out the district's grimmer signal underneath.

A hacker who breaches the guest-list encryption finds names that shouldn't be on any manifest anywhere — corp executives, Sanctum auditors, even quiet Nyx money, all here paying Calloway for the same thing: a few hours nobody official can prove happened.

Dr. Ashwin's simsense rigs run a private, heavily firewalled subnet; anything recorded there is worth a fortune to blackmailers and worth someone's life to whoever's on the recording.

#### Plot Hooks

1. A Guest Who Isn't. Someone boarded the Barge on forged credentials and hasn't left — Calloway wants deniable hands to find them before Kesh's people do, because she suspects the intruder is after Ashwin's recordings, and she'd rather sell those files herself.

2. The Recording. A specific simsense session, recorded weeks ago, shows something that could break careers or start a war between conglomerates. Copies are worth dying for, and someone already has.

3. Calloway's Old Debt. The Velvet Room calls in a favor Calloway can't refuse: host a "private event" for a guest whose identity she isn't told and isn't allowed to ask about. The crew's hired as quiet insurance in case the mystery guest is trouble — and they are.

## Tidewatch

*Nyx harbor-eyes & toll house*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Tidewatch squats on a spit of reinforced rockcrete jutting into the flood, a toll-house turned watchtower turned into something more like a nerve cluster — antenna masts and sensor dishes bristling from its roof at every angle, half of them clearly older than the building, half of them clearly not supposed to be there at all. There's no sky above it, just the grey underside of the level pressing low and close, rain sheeting off the tower's slanted roof in silver curtains that catch the cyan running-lights strung along its rails and throw them shivering across the black water. Small craft — skiffs, low hover-launches, the occasional armored barge — queue at the tollgate below, engines idling, while overhead a tight formation of Nyx courier-drones cycles in and out of a rooftop hatch on a schedule too precise to be casual.

There's a particular quiet to Tidewatch that unsettles people who've spent any time in the rest of Slackwater's noise — not silence, exactly, but a sense of being listened to that presses on the back of your neck. Every boat that passes gets logged. Every face at the tollgate gets read twice, once by the bored clerk in the booth and once by something behind her eyes that isn't bored at all. The crew can feel it as they approach: this small, unassuming tower sees more of Slackwater's water traffic than anyone else alive, and it never, ever forgets what it sees.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Toll-Warden Yuen Marsh** — runs the visible half of Tidewatch, collects tolls, keeps the manifest book Nyx actually cares about hidden three layers beneath the one Ferrum's allowed to audit.

**The Watcher** — an unnamed, unseen Nyx signals specialist who lives in the tower's upper sensor-deck and speaks to almost no one; the true reason Tidewatch matters at all.

**Dross** — a courier-drone technician who services the rooftop hatch fleet, knows more about who's really moving through Slackwater's water lanes than half of Nyx's leadership.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Nyx intelligence outpost, lightly staffed but heavily instrumented; the tower's real defense is that it sees trouble coming long before trouble arrives.

Visible security is minimal — a couple of tollgate guards, unarmored, unthreatening. The real response to danger is informational: Tidewatch simply routes every relevant Nyx asset in the district to converge on a threat before it reaches the tower itself.

**Alert** at Tidewatch means the Watcher has flagged you specifically — a quiet, patient, and very well-informed kind of trouble that doesn't announce itself until it's already too late to avoid.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer shows a mundane toll authority — rates, wait times, nothing remarkable. It's almost aggressively boring, which is itself a tell to anyone who knows Nyx's habits.

Underneath, Tidewatch runs the single densest sensor-fusion node in Slackwater — every SIN-reader, dock camera, drone-feed, and hydrophone in the district's water routes feeds back here, watched over by the Watcher and cross-referenced against Nyx's own priorities.

A hacker skilled enough to breach the tower's shell doesn't find a market or a ledger — they find raw surveillance, a live model of everyone and everything moving on Slackwater's water tonight, including, uncomfortably, themselves.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Ears in the Water. The Watcher has detected an anomalous signal rising from near the Sinkmouth — not a barge, not a drone, something that doesn't match any known transponder. Nyx wants deniable eyes sent to confirm it before they commit real assets.

2. Marsh's Second Book. Someone's been altering entries in Tidewatch's hidden manifest, covering specific barges' movements — a job for someone inside Nyx itself, and Marsh needs it found before her own superiors assume she's the leak.

3. Dross Talks Too Much. The drone technician let something slip to the wrong person about a shipment's timing, and now a rival smuggling crew knows exactly when to hit the Long Pier. The crew's hired to either fix Dross's mistake or make sure he never makes another one.

## Rustdock Fences

*Stolen-cargo laundering yards*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Rustdock is a graveyard that got back up and went into business. Rows of gutted cargo containers, stripped hulls, and half-scrapped ground-rigs stack three high along a curved shoreline road, their edges softened by rust-bloom and standing rain, strung together with sagging tarps that catch the warm chemical downpour and funnel it into a hundred small waterfalls between the stacks. There's no sky above the yard, only the district's iron ceiling and the neon glow bouncing off wet metal in smeared gold streaks; a lattice of makeshift gantry-walks lets workers move cargo overhead while ground-crews below hose down "recovered" containers to strip old serials before repainting new ones. A cargo-hovercraft with its running-lights deliberately dimmed drifts in low over the yard, waiting for a stack to clear before it sets down — nobody here likes drone traffic watching too closely, and the yard's own jury-rigged jammers make sure most of it doesn't.

The noise is industrial and furtive at once: grinders shrieking against old paint, the hiss of solvent, hushed transactions conducted at exactly the volume that doesn't carry past the next stack of containers. Everyone here is doing something to something that used to belong to somebody else, and the whole yard runs on the unspoken rule that nobody asks whose it was originally. As you approach, workers glance up just long enough to price your gear, your rig, and your desperation, then go back to work — Rustdock has seen every kind of trouble walk in, and most of it eventually walks back out a little poorer.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Gil Aster** — yard boss, Cinderhold by birth, runs Rustdock's laundering operation with an engineer's precision; can make a stolen Ferrum cargo-hauler disappear and reappear as three "legitimate" vehicles inside a week.

**Nadia Pell** — a Corran-trained forger who handles the serial numbers and manifest paperwork; her fake documentation is good enough that Sanctum auditors have used it as a reference by mistake.

**Big Ondo** — muscle-for-hire who guards the yard's perimeter, technically freelance, actually on retainer to whichever crew is paying Aster's protection fee that month.

#### Security

Zone: Flats, lawless in practice — no single faction owns Rustdock outright, but Nyx takes a cut for looking the other way and Ferrum's dock authority is paid handsomely to lose the paperwork.

Security is improvised and situational: jury-rigged jammers, lookouts on the gantry-walks, Big Ondo's crew responding to trouble with whatever's closest to hand. It's brutal but disorganized — a determined, well-planned intrusion has a real chance against it.

**Alert** here means the yard scatters — workers vanish between the stacks, cargo gets shoved into the flood rather than be found, and Aster personally decides whether the trouble's worth fighting or worth fleeing.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer shows an inactive scrap-reclamation lot, permitted and boring. The jamming field makes anything more detailed hard to get without physically entering.

A hacker who gets inside the jamming radius finds Nadia Pell's forged paperwork trail — a shockingly thorough, almost artistic record of exactly which stolen goods became which "legitimate" assets, useful as blackmail or as evidence, depending who's asking.

Dead-drops are cargo-tagged: specific containers, marked with chalk symbols only the yard's regulars can read, used to pass instructions without anyone speaking a word.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Hauler That Wasn't Stolen. A vehicle Aster's crew "recovered" and laundered turns out to have belonged to someone dangerous who wants it back — quietly, and specifically, before its new owner notices what's really under the hood.

2. Nadia's Masterwork. Pell's forged documentation has gotten so good that a rival forger — or a Sanctum investigator who's finally caught on — wants her stopped, bought out, or made an example of, and the crew gets caught in the middle of deciding which.

3. Ondo's Split Loyalty. Big Ondo's freelance muscle contract runs out the same week two rival crews both want exclusive protection over Rustdock — and Aster, caught between them, needs deniable hands to make sure the yard doesn't burn down in the crossfire.

## Marrow's Landing

*Cinderhold supply barge tie-up*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Marrow's Landing is the one stretch of Slackwater's shoreline that feels, however briefly, like somewhere people actually live rather than just work. A cluster of Cinderhold supply barges rides at permanent anchor here, lashed hull to hull into a floating shantytown strung with laundry lines and cook-smoke, gangplanks bridging the gaps between them in a maze only the residents walk with any confidence. There's no sky above the Landing — just the district's low grey ceiling and its endless warm rain, caught here by a patchwork of tarps and salvaged solar-catch panels that residents have rigged into a leaking, jury-built awning over half the barges. Beneath that awning, strings of scavenged lumen-bulbs burn soft green and amber, a gentler light than the rest of Slackwater's hard neon; overhead, a battered Cinderhold cargo-hovercraft makes its slow weekly approach, running-lights blinking a recognition code that lets it through checkpoints nobody else can clear.

The sound here is domestic in a way nothing else in the district is — children's voices, someone's radio playing an old Corran ballad half-drowned in static, the creak and slosh of barges shifting against their moorings. But under it, always, is the reminder of what Marrow's Landing really is: the last legal tie-up before the water gets serious, home to people the Reach's ledgers have written off, close enough to the Sinkmouth that everyone here has a story about someone who went down and a candle burning for them somewhere on deck.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Elder Sorrin Cade** — Cinderhold community elder who mediates disputes aboard the barges and negotiates supply terms with anyone willing to trade fair; carries decades of grudges against every conglomerate equally and evenly.

**Little Tam Marrow** — the Landing's unofficial namesake, a scavenger-runner kid who knows every gap in every checkpoint between here and the Long Pier and sells that knowledge for favors, not scrip.

**Doc Priya Nell** — a Caduceus-trained medic who burned her SIN rather than report a patient to corp authorities; runs the Landing's only real clinic out of conviction, not obligation.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Cinderhold community-governed, fiercely self-protective; outsiders are watched, not necessarily unwelcome, but never quite trusted either.

The Landing has no formal muscle, but every resident is a lookout, and the barge-maze itself is a defense — outsiders who don't know the gangplank routes get lost, turned around, or quietly escorted back to the shoreline before they find anything worth finding.

**Alert** here means the whole floating community goes still and watchful at once — word passes barge to barge faster than any radio, and by the time trouble reaches the center of the Landing, everyone who matters has already had time to hide, arm, or vanish.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public layer barely acknowledges Marrow's Landing exists — no permits, no ads, a grey smear on the civic map that Ferrum's surveyors haven't bothered updating in years.

Underneath, the Landing runs its own tight, low-tech mesh network, deliberately kept simple and hard to trace — messages relayed barge to barge by hand and short-range signal rather than the open Wired, a community that has learned the hard way not to trust the overlay with its business.

Little Tam's checkpoint knowledge is itself a kind of dead-drop — not digital, not written anywhere, carried entirely in a scavenger kid's head, which makes it either priceless or one bad day away from lost forever.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Barge That Ran Late. This week's Cinderhold supply barge is overdue, and Elder Cade fears it was intercepted between here and its Cinderhold source — a cargo loss that could mean real hunger on the Landing, and a trail that leads uncomfortably close to Rustdock's fences.

2. Tam's Last Favor. Little Tam has gone missing after bragging about a checkpoint gap near the Sinkmouth that "even Nyx doesn't know about." Whether he's hiding, caught, or gone down further than he meant to, the Landing wants him found before word of that gap spreads to people who'd misuse it.

3. Doc Nell's Patient. A badly injured stranger washed up at the Landing's edge, refusing to say where they came from, bearing augmentation scars that don't match any clinic Doc Nell recognizes — and someone is already asking around the docks about a person matching that description, offering money for information Nell has no intention of giving.
