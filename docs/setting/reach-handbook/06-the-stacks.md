# The Stacks

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // Worker Housing Blocks*

## Block Central

*Old company plaza; gang parliament*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The avenue opens all at once, the way a throat opens to scream. Eight lanes wide, dead straight, laid out a thousand years ago by an architect who believed in rulers and quotas — and above it, four identical megablocks lean in from the corners like they're conferring, their thousand identical windows dark or flickering with a thousand identical lives. There is no sky here, only the gray concrete gut of Grid-level Forty pressing down forty meters overhead, sweating that warm chemical rain in long silver threads that catch the plaza's dead fountain and never quite fill it.

Traffic stacks in layers you feel before you see: a Ferrum maintenance drone crawls the girder-line above, dragging a cone of work-light through the drizzle; a hovercab clips past at shoulder height, throwing up a wake of gutter-steam; ground-rigs — cargo-crawlers, a boosted flatbed missing its logos — nose bumper to bumper around the plaza's dead fountain, where a crowd has gathered that is not shopping and not fleeing, just watching. Neon bleeds off a hundred stacked signs, pink and sodium-gold pooling in the rain until the street looks lacquered. And underneath the drone-whine and the six languages arguing at once, there's a slower sound — gavel-taps, real wood on real stone, some ancient boardroom table dragged out into the open a century ago and never dragged back in.

That's Block Central. That's the Parliament in session. Every gang with a flag on this grid has a seat at that table, and today, someone new is standing at the podium.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Speaker Odalys Rein** — self-appointed chair of the Gang Parliament, once a Ferrum block-super before she "went local." Runs the table with theatrical fairness because she profits most when everyone believes the process is honest. Wants the Stacks to stay just orderly enough that Ferrum never sends Ironclad in force.

**Tarn Vosk** — enforcer-captain of the Grey Ledger crew, the largest bloc at the table; ex-Corran labor organizer turned collections boss. Believes the Parliament is the only thing standing between the Stacks and a Ferrum crackdown, and will kill to keep it standing.

**Little Priya** — a Sparrows courier-runner (see Kids' Run) who sits silent in the Parliament's public gallery every session, memorizing every deal cut for people who pay well to know things before Ferrum does.

**The Vacant Chair** — an empty seat left permanently at the table for "the block that has no boss yet," a standing invitation/threat that keeps ambitious newcomers from starting wars — and a running campaign thread if the crew ever wants to claim it.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — no corp presence inside the plaza itself; the Gang Parliament enforces its own peace by consensus, backed by Grey Ledger muscle and a rotating honor-guard from whichever bloc hosts session.

Violence during session is nearly unthinkable — every gang present has too much invested in the ritual of order. Outside session hours the plaza reverts to contested ground between blocks, and old grudges settle after dark. Alert here means the Parliament itself turning on you: banishment from the table, a bounty posted through six gangs at once, and Ferrum quietly informed of your face.

Ironclad never patrols the plaza directly — Ferrum leases the idea of security here, not the fact of it, and everyone knows the difference.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Officially, Block Central reads as a "community amenity space" on Ferrum's public overlay — a cheerful lie laid over a place Ferrum has quietly written off. SIN-readers on the plaza's dead lampposts are corroded and mostly cosmetic, which is exactly why the Parliament meets here.

A hacker who digs past the surface finds the plaza is a dead zone by design — Meridian relay coverage was pulled from this block years ago after "repeated vandalism," leaving a pocket where the Wired goes thin. Deals cut here don't log anywhere unless someone brings their own rig.

AR dead-drop tags cluster on the fountain's dry basin — anonymous postings for jobs, debts, and grudges, refreshed after every session.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Vacant Chair. A rising bloc wants the empty seat and needs the crew to make a case for it — through evidence, extortion, or a demonstration of force that won't tip the Parliament into open war.

2. The Honest Broker. Speaker Rein privately hires the crew, off the books, to find out which gang at her table is secretly taking Ferrum money to feed the housing authority intel on the others — because if it's discovered publicly, the Parliament burns and the whole district goes to war.

3. Ferrum's Patience Ends. Word reaches the crew through a Cassavir line that Ferrum's board has finally approved a "restructuring initiative" for the Stacks — a euphemism for Ironclad clearance teams. Someone needs to get the Parliament the warning before the convoys roll, and someone else may want it to arrive too late.

## The Standpipe

*Only clean-water tap for 8 blocks*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You hear the Standpipe before you see it — the low communal murmur of a line that never ends, hundreds of voices and jerry-cans and prayers layered under the endless static hiss of rain on corrugated cover. It's not much to look at: a rust-scabbed junction of Ferrum-stamped pipe jutting from a megablock's foundation, one brass tap worn bright by a million hands, and a witch's-hat awning someone welded overhead less to keep the chemical rain off than to make the pretense of shelter. There is no sky above it, only the black underside of the block that houses it, forty stories of identical windows staring down at forty stories of nothing to see.

The line itself is the neighborhood. It stretches two full blocks down a grid-straight avenue built for eight lanes of company traffic that mostly isn't there anymore — a few ground-rigs idle at the curb, a hovercab drifts overhead threading between drone-couriers on their overnight runs, and everyone in the water-line watches the traffic without watching it, the way people watch weather. Neon runs down the block's flank in cheap sodium-gold streaks, catching in every can and bucket and cracked visor, so the whole line looks dipped in dirty gold.

At the tap itself, a woman in a Corran guild jacket with a stopwatch counts seconds against buckets, and everyone obeys her count like scripture, because out here, she's the only law that keeps anyone from drowning in each other's need.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Iset Dray** — the Standpipe's self-appointed tap-warden, a retired Corran pipefitter who keeps the line fair by pure force of reputation. Knows every family on eight blocks by their water ration and will go to war over favoritism.

**Deacon Row** — a Lamp-on-Ninth street-priest (see Ninth Lamp Annex) who works the line twice a week blessing water no one can prove is clean, and quietly testing it with grey-tech sensors no one can prove exist.

**"Drip" Kalen Ossa** — a Ferrum meter-tech who skims the tap's flow logs to sell black-market access codes to gangs wanting a second unmonitored tap; despises his job and fears Ferrum finding out almost as much as he fears the gangs.

**The Widow's Bucket** — not a person but an institution: an unclaimed bucket left permanently at the head of the line "for whoever needs it most today," filled by whoever can afford to that morning. The line enforces its sanctity with fists if it must.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — no gang holds the tap outright; it's treated as neutral ground under a fragile truce brokered years ago through the Gang Parliament at Block Central. Breaking the truce here draws every bloc's anger at once.

Violence at the Standpipe is the fastest way to make every gang on the grid your enemy simultaneously — water access is too valuable to let anyone monopolize by force, so the line self-polices, hard, before Ferrum notices trouble. Repeated incidents draw a Ferrum "efficiency audit," which is code for shutting the tap down for weeks out of pure spite.

No visible corp security; the closest thing to law is a hundred angry people with buckets who will hold you down until Mother Dray decides what happens next.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Ferrum's public overlay lists the Standpipe as "temporarily reduced flow — maintenance pending," a notice that's been live for six years. It's a technically true statement about a tap Ferrum has no intention of ever repairing to capacity.

A hacker probing the tap's meter finds Drip Kalen's skimmed logs — evidence that Ferrum bills the block for full-capacity water it deliberately throttles, pocketing the difference at the district level. That data alone is worth a great deal to the right buyer.

AR graffiti tags along the queue mark which families are owed favors, who's sick, who's dying — an informal, encrypted ledger of need that outlives any one person's memory.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Skimmed Meter. Drip Kalen's side-deals have finally attracted Ferrum's attention. The crew is hired — by the gangs who buy his codes, or by Ferrum's internal auditors — to find him first, for very different reasons.

2. The Real Shortage. Deacon Row's sensors have detected something in the water that isn't supposed to be there — a contaminant traced back to runoff bleeding down from a Caduceus disposal site higher in the Grid. Someone upstream needs this quietly buried before eight blocks realize they've been drinking it for months.

3. The Second Tap. A rival gang has found and hidden an unmapped junction point that could relieve the line's pressure — and break Mother Dray's authority over the neighborhood if it goes public wrong. The crew can broker its opening, sell its location, or blow it up before the balance of power shifts.

## Camera Cross

*Sanctum test-grid; total surveillance*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The intersection looks ordinary from two blocks out — the same rigid grid, the same identical megablocks squatting under the same low gray underside of the Grid above, rain dragging the same tired neon down the same tired girders. It's only as you get close that you notice the density: camera-clusters knuckled onto every lamppost, every window-ledge, every drone-perch, in numbers no ordinary intersection could ever justify. A Sanctum service-drone hangs dead-still at second-story height, rain sliding off its lensed belly in a way that looks less like weather and more like it's tracking the drops.

Everything else about the street insists it's normal. Ground-rigs idle at a crosswalk that hasn't had working lights in decades. A hovercab driver argues with a fare over a fare he clearly can't afford. Courier-drones cut low overhead in tight, metronomic lanes, and you realize after a beat too long that they're not delivering anything — they're flying test patterns, back and forth, over and over, mapping the crowd below like a grid being calibrated. The wall of sound here is the same six-language murmur and drone-whine as anywhere in the Stacks, except that under it, faint and constant, is a chorus of tiny electronic chirps — SIN-readers pinging every chip that walks through, dozens of times a second, cataloguing a neighborhood that has no idea it's a laboratory.

Nobody who lives here calls it Camera Cross by accident. They call it that the way you'd name a wound.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Actuary Wen Ostrelle** — the Sanctum field-analyst who "manages" the test-grid; sees the entire intersection as a dataset, the residents as noise to be modeled out. Polite, bloodless, genuinely doesn't understand why people are afraid of her.

**Halden Corbo** — a Meridian-trained signal-tech gone freelance, sells "ghost passes" — short scrambler bursts that blind a SIN-reader's log for the seconds it takes to cross unrecorded. Half legend, half real, entirely hunted.

**Reyna "Glass" Ubode** — a block resident turned informal watch-captain, who has mapped every camera's blind arc from years of forced observation and trades that knowledge for favors and food.

**The Cross Choir** — a loose, half-joking name for the handful of street-kids who've learned to walk the intersection's dead zones like a dance, and who Sanctum has started quietly flagging as a "statistical anomaly requiring further study" — a designation that has never gone well for anyone tagged with it before.

#### Security

Zone: Flats, nominally — but functionally a soft Sanctum enclave. No gang holds Camera Cross; the corp's data-harvest is worth more to Sanctum than any rent Ferrum could extract, and the two corps have an understanding.

There is no visible muscle. Security here is total observational coverage: every face logged, every transaction pattern flagged, every anomaly escalated up a chain that ends, eventually, in a real response — Sanctum liaison drones first, Ironclad contractors if the anomaly resists. Alert here builds silently and invisibly until it's already too late to talk your way out of it.

The intersection's one mercy: Sanctum doesn't care about petty crime, only about deviation from predicted pattern. Steal a loaf of bread and nothing happens. Do something the actuarial model didn't expect, and the response is instant and disproportionate.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired here is not dense — it is total. Every SIN pings on entry, every device is silently profiled, every purchase, conversation snippet, and gait pattern feeds a live Sanctum actuarial model running in real time on the block. It is, quietly, one of the most surveilled square blocks in the entire Flats.

A hacker who taps the local relay finds raw modeling data: Sanctum isn't just watching, it's testing thresholds — how long before residents route around known cameras, how fast rumor of a "ghost pass" spreads, how a population behaves when it knows it's being priced as risk. This is a live actuarial experiment on human beings, and the data is worth a fortune to the right (or very wrong) buyer.

Dead-air pockets exist only in Reyna Ubode's mapped blind arcs — a handful of unmonitored square meters that shift every time Sanctum recalibrates, making them a currency of their own.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Ghost Pass Trade. Halden Corbo's scramblers have started failing, and he suspects Sanctum has quietly patched the exploit — meaning every "ghost" who bought one this month is retroactively exposed in the logs. He needs the crew to find out how deep the exposure goes before Sanctum acts on it.

2. Statistical Anomaly. One of the Cross Choir kids has vanished — not arrested, not dead that anyone can find, just gone, the same week Sanctum flagged them by designation. The Sparrows want to know what "further study" means, and they're willing to pay to find out.

3. The Model Doesn't Lie. Reyna Ubode has noticed the actuarial model quietly reallocating "risk scores" that correlate suspiciously with residents who've spoken out against Ferrum rent hikes at the Gang Parliament. If someone's weaponizing Sanctum's own surveillance grid to punish dissent, that's a story — and a crime — that reaches well above the Stacks.

## The Underhang

*Black-market arcade beneath a block*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You duck below street level down a ramp never meant for pedestrians — a loading bay some forgotten architect drew for trucks a thousand years gone — and the noise changes before the light does. Up above, the avenue runs its usual wet neon hum under the low concrete gut of the Grid; down here, under the full mass of the megablock's foundation, there's no rain at all, just the drip and sweat of condensation off a thousand load-bearing pillars strung with pirated lumen-wire and stolen signage. The Underhang doesn't have a sky to not have. It has a ceiling, low and close and studded with old rebar, and that's somehow worse.

It's a market that isn't supposed to exist stacked into a space that wasn't meant to hold one — stall after stall crammed between support columns, hawkers running grey-tech and knockoff chrome and simsense chips of dubious legality, a boosted ground-rig backed halfway down the ramp still running its idle in case a fast exit is needed, courier-drones darting low overhead under the low ceiling in defiance of every navigation law on the books. The neon down here is scavenged and mismatched, pink bleeding into cyan bleeding into a stolen HALO ad glowing serenely above a stall selling counterfeit anti-rejection meds. The wall of sound is close and layered — barter in six tongues, a generator's diesel cough, somewhere a busted speaker looping a jingle from a product that stopped existing decades ago.

Everyone down here is selling something they'd rather Ferrum, or Sanctum, or somebody's landlord never found out about.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Old Pell** — the Underhang's informal proprietor, a Nyx-connected fence who's run this space for twenty years by paying the right bribes to the right building super and never, ever letting anyone start a fire near the support columns.

**Kessa Vrain** — a ripperdoc who works a folding table near the back, deniable and unlicensed, patching chrome and wounds alike for those who can't afford Caduceus rates or can't afford Caduceus's paperwork.

**Denz "Two-Face" Aurelio** — sells salvaged Meridian relay parts and "cleaned" SIN chips; not to be trusted, not to be underestimated, definitely selling to more than one gang at once.

**The Building Super** — never named, always present in rumor; the Ferrum employee who's paid to not notice the Underhang exists, and who everyone quietly understands could end the whole market with one phone call he's terrified to make.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — deep lawless, run informally by Old Pell's understanding with Nyx; no gang holds it outright because no gang wants the structural liability of a cave-in on their conscience.

Violence is bad for business and Old Pell enforces that personally with a small crew of debt-collectors turned bouncers; anything that risks bringing Ferrum structural inspectors (and therefore attention) gets shut down fast and hard.

If Ferrum ever formally discovers the Underhang, the response isn't Ironclad — it's demolition-crew "safety condemnation," which clears the market and the people in it with equal indifference. Everyone down here knows this and it's the one thing that keeps the peace.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Underhang doesn't appear on any official overlay at all — it's built in a structural void Ferrum's own maps forgot to update, which is exactly why it works. SIN-readers don't reach this deep; most transactions here are effectively invisible to corp logging.

A hacker who wants to find the Underhang has to go looking off-grid — through rumor, through a Cassavir contact, or through Denz's black-market relay parts, which occasionally still ping a ghost signature back to Meridian's network before someone strips them.

Dead-drop tags are physical here, not digital — chalk marks and scratched symbols on the pillars, a market running on analog trust because digital trust is too easy to trace.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Super's Price. Word reaches the crew that Ferrum has finally offered the Building Super real money — enough to make him talk. Old Pell wants the crew to find out how serious the offer is, and handle it, before the Underhang gets condemned out from under a hundred livelihoods.

2. Cleaned Chips, Dirty Trail. One of Denz's "cleaned" SIN chips turns out to have belonged to someone Sanctum is actively hunting — and now that identity has resurfaced walking around the Stacks under someone else's face. The crew has to find the buyer before Sanctum does.

3. Structural Integrity. Kessa Vrain has noticed water damage spreading through the support columns that nobody with authority seems to care about. If she's right, the Underhang isn't just illegal — it's sitting under a slow structural failure that could take the whole megablock's foundation with it, and someone needs to prove that before it becomes a mass grave.

## Rooftop Farms

*Gang-run hydroponics & lookout nests*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The climb up is the point. Forty flights of stairwell, elevator dead since before anyone alive can remember, and every landing marked with a different gang's tag until you reach the roof access and step out into — not sky, never sky, but the closest thing the Stacks has to an open horizon: a flat gray megablock rooftop stretching a full city block, ringed by the underside of the tier above close enough that the chemical rain falls here in a fine, constant mist rather than sheets, beading on row after row of jury-rigged hydroponic troughs glowing sickly violet under grow-lamps salvaged from three different corps' dumpsters.

From up here you finally see the district whole — the rigid grid of identical megablocks marching away in every direction under their shared ceiling of Grid-underside, avenues glowing gold and pink and cyan below like rivers of spilled light, and threading between the rooftops at your exact height, the air-traffic layer most of the Stacks never sees directly: hovercabs running block-to-block shortcuts, cargo drones on long hauls, and — closer, always closer than you'd like — a Ferrum patrol-flyer sweeping its light across rooftop after rooftop on its slow, incurious circuit.

Somewhere among the troughs, a lookout with a modified hunting rifle has had eyes on you since the stairwell, and everyone up here already knows exactly how many of you there are.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Marisol "Greenhouse" Adaeze** — runs the rooftop's hydroponics operation for the Grey Ledger crew; part farmer, part quartermaster, feeds three blocks' worth of gang loyalty with fresh vegetables no one else can get. Fiercely protective of her plants and her people in equal measure.

**Skiv Talharn** — chief lookout, a former Ironclad washout who trades roof-watch duty for a cut of the harvest and a place to disappear from his old employer's notice.

**The Bird-Man, Corvin Ashe** — a suspected wereraven who visits the rooftops irregularly, trading rare seed-stock and news from other districts for grown produce; nobody's sure if he's Nyx, Wired-touched, or just strange, and nobody's rude enough to ask directly.

**Patrol Officer Dessa Kray** — flies the Ferrum rooftop-sweep circuit; bribed into incuriosity for years, but new to the route and not yet fully bought in, a live variable everyone on the roof is nervously managing.

#### Security

Zone: Flats, rooftop tier — held by the Grey Ledger crew as a resource asset; lookout nests positioned at every rooftop's edge give the gang eyes across multiple blocks simultaneously.

Approach is watched from the moment you enter the stairwell; armed response is fast and disproportionate to threats against the farm itself, since it feeds hundreds of people who'd otherwise go hungry between Standpipe rationing and Ferrum rent hikes. Alert here means every lookout on the rooftop network relaying your description block to block within minutes.

Ferrum's aerial patrols are tolerated, not fought — Greenhouse pays enough in bribes through intermediaries to keep patrol-flyers from reporting the unlicensed agriculture, but that arrangement is only as solid as the officer flying it.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Officially, the rooftop is registered on Ferrum's overlay as "non-habitable structural surface, access restricted" — a designation that keeps corp attention away and gives the gang plausible deniability if pressed.

A hacker who checks Ferrum's maintenance logs finds the rooftop hasn't been inspected in eleven years, a gap Greenhouse's people have exploited fully — and evidence, if anyone wanted it, that Ferrum's building safety compliance across the Stacks is a fiction on paper only.

The Bird-Man leaves no data trail at all; Wired-savvy residents whisper he doesn't route through the network the way people do, which either means old grey-tech discipline or something the Wired itself can't fully see.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The New Officer. Officer Kray hasn't yet agreed to look the other way. Greenhouse wants the crew to either bribe her properly, dig up leverage, or scare her off the route entirely before she reports the farm and Ferrum sends a clearance team.

2. Seed Debt. Corvin Ashe's last seed-stock trade came with strings — he wants the crew to carry something small and unmarked to another district's rooftop, no questions, and the Bird-Man's favor is worth more than anyone on the roof is willing to say out loud.

3. The Harvest War. A rival gang's block has run out of growing space and is eyeing the Grey Ledger farm with open envy. Skiv thinks it's building to a raid; the crew can help fortify the rooftop, broker a produce-sharing truce, or discover the rival gang is being quietly funded by someone in Ferrum who'd love to see the two gangs bleed each other over lettuce.

## Ninth Lamp Annex

*Street-faith outreach; Lamp on Ninth kin*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The lamp is the first thing you see from three blocks off — a real oil-flame lantern, absurd and stubborn in a city of neon, hung above a doorway carved into the base of a megablock like a wound someone decided to heal on purpose. There is no sky above the Annex any more than anywhere else in the Stacks, just the same low gray gut of the Grid sweating its warm chemical rain down through the same rusted gantries — but here the rain seems softer somehow, or maybe it's just that the crowd sheltering under the Annex's awning isn't hiding from anything, just waiting patiently for soup.

The avenue outside runs its usual wet current of light and machinery — ground-rigs queued for the corner charging point, a hovercab dropping a fare who flinches at the fare-meter, courier-drones threading low overhead between the megablocks' identical windows — but the noise softens at the Annex's threshold, the six-tongue clamor of the street giving way to murmured names, a hymn half-remembered, the clatter of ladles against steel pots. Neon still bleeds pink and sodium-gold across the wet pavement, but here it's mixed with candlelight, warm and unsteady and entirely out of place.

A street-priest in patched Corran work-clothes stands in the doorway, and the moment she sees you — soaked, edged, carrying whatever trouble brought you this deep into the grid — she doesn't ask why you've come. She just steps back to let you in.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Sister Aveline Corrick** — keeper of the Ninth Lamp Annex, Corran by blood and street-faith by calling; runs soup lines, a free clinic staffed by grey-tech medics, and washing rites for the dead who have no one left to claim them. Knows every family's grief on this grid by name.

**Brother Tull** — a former Caduceus orderly who left the corp after "one too many ledger decisions about who gets treatment," now runs the Annex's clinic with stolen protocols and a great deal of improvisation.

**Deacon Row** — the same itinerant priest who works the Standpipe line (see above), based out of the Annex and its most trusted courier of news, medicine, and quiet warnings between the two sites.

**Ghost-Eyed Emrick** — a burned-SIN Cinderhold exile who sleeps in the Annex's back room in exchange for standing watch at night; says little, sees everything, trusted completely by Sister Corrick and almost no one else.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — sanctuary ground by long-standing, unwritten consensus; even the hardest gangs on the grid observe the old rule that the Lamp's doorway is neutral and no blood is spilled inside it.

The Annex has no weapons and no muscle by design — its security is entirely social, built on a thousand small debts of gratitude across every gang and block it's fed or healed. Breaking the sanctuary rule here is one of the few things that can unite the entire Gang Parliament against a single offender.

Ferrum tolerates the Annex because shutting it down would cause a level of unrest even the housing authority doesn't want to manage; that tolerance is thin and has never been tested by a real crisis.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Annex keeps a deliberately dark footprint — no SIN-readers, no ads, no corp overlay tagging at all, a rare true blank spot in the Wired that reads to any hacker as either sacred or suspicious depending on who's asking.

Beneath that quiet, Brother Tull runs an off-books medical relay — patient notes, med requests, and warnings about bad batches of street drugs or contaminated water, passed hand to hand and occasionally burst-transmitted to trusted contacts like Deacon Row, never stored anywhere Sanctum could subpoena.

Dead-drop tags don't exist here in the usual sense; the Annex itself functions as a dead-drop for the whole district — messages, small packages, even people, pass through its sanctuary status untouched by anyone who respects the Lamp.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Unclaimed Dead. Brother Tull has treated three overdoses in a week from a simsense batch that shouldn't exist — cut with something that mimics BTL crash symptoms but isn't. Sister Corrick wants the source found and stopped before the Annex's soup line becomes a mortuary line.

2. Sanctuary Tested. A desperate fugitive from Camera Cross's surveillance grid claims sanctuary at the Annex, and the gang or corp hunting them is deciding whether the old rule still applies to them. The crew may be asked to enforce the sanctuary — or to quietly extract the fugitive before it becomes a fight nobody can win.

3. The Caduceus Ledger. Brother Tull still has partial access to an old Caduceus ledger system — a moral liability he's never used. If he finally decides to expose which "ledger decisions" at a nearby clinic condemned patients to die for cost reasons, the crew becomes his only protection when Caduceus comes looking for the leak.

## The Rent Office

*Ferrum housing authority; hated ground*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Nothing on the grid is hated quite like this building, and nothing on the grid looks less like it deserves the hatred — just another slab of Ferrum-stamped concrete on a corner like any other, squatting under the same low ceiling of Grid-underside, the same chemical rain sliding down its flank in dirty silver sheets. But the line outside never gets shorter, rain or not, and the faces in it carry a specific, exhausted dread that you learn to recognize fast once you've spent any time in the Stacks.

Above the building's flat roofline, the vertical traffic of the district goes on with total indifference — a Ferrum survey-drone circling on its endless assessment loop, a cargo-hovercraft grinding past at altitude with a load of prefab wall-panels for some other block's "renovation," ground-rigs idling at the curb with landlords' collection crews inside them, engines running in case they need to leave fast. Neon from the surrounding blocks bleeds sideways across the Rent Office's dead façade, the only building on the avenue that never bothers to light its own sign — it doesn't need advertising. Everyone already knows exactly where it is.

The wall of sound here isn't the usual six-tongue market clamor; it's quieter and worse — the particular hush of people rehearsing what they'll say when they finally reach the window, and the crackle of a payment-kiosk that eats scrip and coughs back error codes more often than receipts.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Assessor Coral Meng** — the Rent Office's senior clerk, a Ferrum company lifer who has denied more rent appeals than anyone else on the grid and takes visible pride in her efficiency ratings. Not cruel by nature — worse, indifferent by policy.

**"Fair" Terrin Osk** — a former tenant-advocate turned informal fixer who haunts the Rent Office line, helping desperate families word appeals correctly for a cut of whatever they save; half the grid loves him, the other half thinks he's a Ferrum plant.

**Gorin Vashe** — captain of a Ferrum-contracted collections crew, technically Ironclad-adjacent muscle on a civil contract; enforces evictions with a checklist and no visible feeling about any of it.

**The Ledger Ghost** — rumor of a Rent Office clerk, never confirmed, who's been quietly flagging vulnerable tenants for "priority eviction" in exchange for bribes from a rival housing broker looking to clear blocks for redevelopment.

#### Security

Zone: Flats, under direct Ferrum administrative control — the one building in the Stacks that functions as genuine corp ground rather than gang-contested territory.

Security is bureaucratic first, physical second: armed collections crews only deploy for scheduled evictions, arriving in force and leaving fast. Disrupting an eviction in progress draws an immediate, disproportionate Ironclad-contracted response, because Ferrum treats any successful resistance here as a contagion risk across the whole grid.

Petty disruption — shouting matches, sit-ins, appeals gone loud — is tolerated with weary contempt; Ferrum has learned that overreacting to noise just makes martyrs, so low-grade unrest here rarely escalates unless it threatens an actual eviction schedule.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Rent Office runs one of the densest, most rigorously logged overlays in the entire district — every tenant's payment history, appeal record, and risk-flag status sits in a live Ferrum administrative feed, cross-checked against Sanctum's actuarial models from Camera Cross two blocks over.

A hacker who breaches the local terminal finds the raw eviction-priority algorithm — and the uncomfortable truth that "risk score" correlates less with missed payments than with tenants who've engaged in organizing, complained publicly, or shown up at Gang Parliament sessions. The system isn't just collecting rent. It's managing dissent.

No dead-drops here — Ferrum scrubs the physical space aggressively of any AR tagging that isn't corp-sanctioned, making the Rent Office one of the few genuinely "clean" overlay zones in the Stacks, in the worst possible sense.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Ledger Ghost. Fair Terrin has gathered enough whispers to believe someone inside is selling eviction lists to a redevelopment broker. He wants the crew to get proof before whole blocks get cleared for a project no tenant has been told about.

2. Stop the Eviction. A family the crew has come to know — maybe through the Standpipe line or the Ninth Lamp Annex — is on tomorrow's eviction schedule. Gorin Vashe's crew will execute it on time and without mercy unless the crew can find leverage, funds, or a legal wedge fast.

3. The Dissent Algorithm. Someone in Sanctum or Meridian has noticed the eviction-priority correlation with organizing activity and wants it exposed — or buried, depending on who's paying. Either way, the data lives on a terminal inside the most heavily surveilled corp building in the district, and getting it out clean will take more than luck.

## Deadbox Relay

*Failed Meridian node; ghost-signal*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The relay tower doesn't look dead from a distance — it's still lit, still hung with the same tangled cable-runs and warning-strobes as every other Meridian node stitching signal across the Stacks. It's only up close, craning your neck against the low gray ceiling of the level above, rain dragging its usual chemical sheen down the antenna mast, that you notice the strobes are looping a fault code instead of a status ping, and have been for longer than anyone official seems to care about.

The intersection below is busy in the ordinary Stacks way — ground-rigs cutting through on the cross-street, a hovercab idling under the tower's shadow while its driver argues with a dispatch signal that keeps dropping, courier-drones rerouting mid-flight with visible, jittery confusion as they cross beneath the dead node's coverage gap. Neon from three surrounding blocks pools thick and directionless here, pink and cyan bleeding together without the usual Meridian ad-feed to organize it into anything coherent, and under the drone-whine and six-tongue chatter there's a new sound layered in: a faint electronic murmur, almost like static, almost like a voice, bleeding out of every device that strays too close to the tower's base.

Locals call it the Deadbox and give it a wide, superstitious berth. The static isn't supposed to say anything. Sometimes, if you listen wrong, it almost does.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ondine Yarr** — a technomancer squatting in the relay's maintenance shed, drawn here by the ghost-signal like a moth; believes the dead node is bleeding something real out of the Wired's substrate and has been documenting it obsessively.

**Foreman Bettik Vray** — the Meridian contractor technically responsible for the node's repair, who's filed for maintenance funding six times and been denied six times; deeply frustrated, secretly grateful the malfunction hasn't been "fixed" so cheaply that his job disappears with it.

**Corvin Ashe** — the same possible wereraven who trades with the Rooftop Farms, seen occasionally at the Deadbox's base at odd hours, listening to the static like it means something to him specifically.

**The Voice** — not confirmed to be a person at all; a recurring signal-ghost in the static that some residents swear repeats fragments of old Meridian dispatch chatter from a disaster nobody living remembers, and others swear is something newer, and worse.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — nominally Meridian infrastructure, functionally abandoned; no gang has claimed the tower itself because the superstition around it is worth more as a deterrent than any resource inside it.

There is no active security response here — Meridian's monitoring for this node has been quietly deprioritized for years. The only danger is the malfunction itself: devices near the tower glitch, drones misroute, and rumor holds that anyone who lingers too long near the base starts hearing the Voice in their own head, whether or not that's technically possible.

If the node were ever repaired or reactivated with force, Meridian's dormant security protocols for the site would snap back online instantly — a fact almost no one currently squatting here knows.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Officially the node reads as "offline — scheduled maintenance," a lie six years stale. Meridian's public overlay simply routes around the gap, leaving a visible dead patch in the district's coverage that every local navigation AR has learned to detour around.

A hacker who taps directly into the relay's dying hardware finds something Meridian's engineers apparently never fully purged: fragments of an old emergency broadcast loop from deep in the tower's memory, corrupted, looping, bleeding into the ambient signal — Ondine's obsession made almost, but not quite, explicable.

Technomancers report the site differently than hackers do — less like broken equipment, more like a wound in the Wired's fabric that hasn't healed, which is either poetic nonsense or the single strangest true thing in the whole district.

#### Plot Hooks

1. What the Static Says. Ondine Yarr has finally isolated a clean fragment of the ghost-signal and believes it's not archival at all — it's live, and it's coming from somewhere else in the Reach entirely. She needs the crew's help (and protection) to trace it before Meridian notices someone's finally paying real attention to the Deadbox.

2. Foreman's Gambit. Bettik Vray has quietly decided to fake a catastrophic failure at the node to force Meridian's hand on funding — but faking it convincingly means someone needs to disable the relay's last dormant security protocols first, and he can't be seen doing it himself.

3. The Voice Answers Back. Someone using a scavenged relay rig has managed to transmit into the Deadbox's static and gotten a response that used their own name. Word is spreading through the Stacks that the Deadbox isn't a malfunction — it's listening. The crew is hired to find out what, exactly, and whether it can be reasoned with, sold, or needs to be shut down for good.

## The Fold

*Obsidian scrip-parlor & dream debt*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Fold announces itself the way every Obsidian storefront does — too much light, too soft a color, a doorway that breathes warm mist into the wet street like it's exhaling. It's tucked into the ground floor of an otherwise unremarkable megablock, wedged between a shuttered noodle stall and a wall of peeling Ferrum eviction notices, and against that backdrop its glow looks almost obscene: pink and rose-gold neon pooling in the standing rain, spilling out from under an awning that keeps the chemical drizzle off just long enough to make the doorway feel like a mercy.

Outside, the avenue does what the avenue always does — a hovercab idling for a fare who never seems to come out, a drone-courier looping patiently overhead waiting on a delivery to a customer who's lost track of the hour, a ground-rig's headlights cutting long wet reflections across the pavement. The low ceiling of the Grid above presses close as always, sweating its endless rain, and the six-tongue murmur of the street rolls on — but right at the Fold's threshold, all of it goes muffled and soft, swallowed by a hum of simsense feed-static and a chime that plays every time the door opens, sweet and small and designed by someone who understood exactly how lonely this street gets at night.

Through the mist inside, you can just make out the rows of reclined chairs, and the faces in them, slack and radiant, dreaming rent they will never be able to pay back.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Madame Loray Vance** — the Fold's proprietor, an Obsidian franchise-holder who runs the parlor with a courtesan's warmth and a loan shark's arithmetic; genuinely likes her regulars, which makes the debt spirals she runs on them somehow worse.

**"Sweet" Denbo Fitch** — the Fold's debt-collector, deceptively gentle, specializes in convincing people to sign over rooms, ration cards, or favors instead of scrip they don't have; violence is a last resort he's paid extra to avoid using.

**Ilsabet Kroy** — a regular customer three dream-cycles deep in debt, functionally a prisoner of the parlor's payment plan, who the crew might meet as a job, a warning, or a ghost of what could happen to any of them.

**The Quiet Buyer** — an unnamed contact who periodically purchases the Fold's most vivid, most illegal custom-commissioned dreams — memories, real or fabricated, of a life in the Spires — for a clientele Madame Vance refuses to name even under pressure.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Obsidian holds this location directly as corp-franchised territory, a rare pocket of genuine corporate presence embedded in gang-contested ground, tolerated by local blocs because the Fold is too profitable a vice pipeline for anyone to want it shut down.

Security is soft and social until it isn't: Sweet Denbo and two houseshift bouncers handle disputes quietly, but Obsidian keeps a standing arrangement with a nearby Ironclad contract detail for anything that threatens the franchise itself — theft of equipment, harm to staff, or interference with the debt-collection pipeline.

Alert here is triggered less by violence than by anything that threatens the parlor's revenue model — freeing a debtor, exposing the payment terms publicly, or tampering with the dream-rigs draws corp-grade retaliation fast.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Fold's public overlay is lush and inviting — curated simsense previews, glowing reviews, soft-focus AR mist drifting out into the street to lure in passersby. It's some of the most effective, most predatory advertising in the district, precisely tuned to a neighborhood with nothing else to hope for.

A hacker who digs into the parlor's back-end finds the real product: individualized debt-ladders keyed to each customer's psychological profile, engineered by Obsidian actuaries to maximize repeat visits before outright predatory collection. It's legal, technically, and it's monstrous, practically.

Dead-drop tags float discreetly near the alley entrance — a back-channel for buyers and sellers of "grey dreams," bootleg BTL content banned even by Obsidian's own loose standards, moved here under the parlor's legitimate cover.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Buying Back Ilsabet. A desperate relative hires the crew to get Ilsabet Kroy out from under her dream-debt before Sweet Denbo converts it into a lien on her family's housing unit — a race against a payment plan built to never actually end.

2. The Quiet Buyer's Order. The Fold's Quiet Buyer has commissioned something unusually specific and unusually dangerous: a fabricated memory built from real surveillance footage stolen from Camera Cross. Whoever's selling that footage has crossed a line Sanctum will not forgive quietly.

3. Grey Dreams. A batch of bootleg BTL moving through the Fold's back alley is causing real neurological harm — worse than the usual crash. Someone (Caduceus, a rival parlor, or a Cinderhold chemist gone rogue) is cutting product with something untested, and Madame Vance would rather the crew fix her problem quietly than let Obsidian corporate find out she lost control of her own supply chain.

## Kids' Run

*Child-courier network HQ; the Sparrows*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You'd walk right past it if you didn't know to look — a gap between two megablocks barely wide enough for a grown adult to turn sideways in, screened by a curtain of drying laundry and salvaged tarp, the kind of forgotten seam the district's rigid grid always leaves somewhere. Above, the usual ceiling of Grid-underside presses down gray and close, chemical rain sluicing off the megablocks' flanks in twin sheets that meet in this alley's middle like a curtain of their own. But the moment you duck inside, you understand why it was chosen: it's a shortcut, a vertical seam threading up through gaps and fire-ladders and load-bearing gaps between four different blocks, invisible to anyone who doesn't already know the route.

Outside, at the alley's mouth, the wider avenue goes on with its usual traffic — ground-rigs, a hovercab's headlights sweeping past, a drone-courier on a scheduled run overhead threading between neon signs bleeding pink and cyan into the wet street — but inside the seam, you hear a different rhythm: quick bare feet on wet metal stairs, a birdlike two-note whistle relayed block to block faster than any Meridian signal, laughter that stops the second a stranger's shadow falls wrong.

A face appears above you on a fire escape, no older than ten, sizing you up with the flat professional calm of someone who has moved things nobody was supposed to see for money that matters more than it should to someone that young.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Little Priya** — the same young courier seen in the Gang Parliament's gallery at Block Central, and the Sparrows' de facto senior runner despite being barely into her teens; smarter than most adults on the grid and painfully aware of it.

**"Uncle" Foss Marrow** — not actually anyone's uncle; a burned-SIN Cinderhold exile who feeds, half-shelters, and organizes the Sparrows in exchange for a cut of courier fees, genuinely protective of "his" kids in a way that complicates every adult who tries to exploit them instead.

**Reeve** — the Sparrows' fastest runner, maybe twelve, who's memorized every gap, ladder, and blind spot between six megablocks and never once been caught carrying.

**The Buyer** — an unnamed, unseen adult client the Sparrows call only by that title, who pays premium rates for deliveries the kids are pointedly never told the contents of — the network's most lucrative and most dangerous standing contract.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — technically no one's territory, tolerated by every gang on the grid because the Sparrows' courier network is too useful to disrupt; even hostile blocs pay the kids to move messages they don't trust to adults or the Wired.

The Sparrows' security is invisibility and speed, not force — kids scatter at the first sign of real danger, and Foss Marrow's rule is absolute: no child fights, no child dies for a delivery, ever. Adults who threaten the kids directly find every gang on the grid suddenly, unexpectedly united against them, since the network serves everyone.

Sanctum's Camera Cross grid two blocks over is the network's real enemy — the Sparrows' entire business model depends on routes the surveillance grid can't map, and every expansion of Sanctum's coverage shrinks their safe passages a little more.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Sparrows deliberately avoid the Wired entirely — no SIN chips used on runs, no digital comms, just the whistle-code and word of mouth, making Kids' Run one of the last genuinely analog information networks left in the Stacks.

A hacker looking for the Sparrows in the Wired finds essentially nothing — which is itself notable, a conspicuous absence in a district otherwise thick with data-trails, and a strong hint to anyone paying attention that something here is being kept deliberately, expertly dark.

The one digital trace that exists is the Buyer's payment channel — a scrubbed, laundered scrip transfer that Foss Marrow receives through a Nyx-adjacent cutout, the network's single point of exposure if anyone ever traced it back.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Buyer's Package. The Sparrows are hired for a delivery heavier and stranger than usual, and Reeve — against every rule — peeks inside. What she sees ties directly into the war climbing the Reach's foundations, and now the Buyer wants the kid who saw it found before she talks.

2. Shrinking Ground. Sanctum's Camera Cross coverage is expanding into the Sparrows' last safe routes. Uncle Foss wants the crew to find a way to blind, bribe, or route around the new cameras before the courier network collapses and a hundred desperate arrangements across the grid fall apart with it.

3. Little Priya's Ledger. Priya has quietly started keeping her own written record of every deal she's overheard at the Gang Parliament — insurance, she says, in case the Sparrows ever need leverage against an adult who tries to use them. If that ledger falls into the wrong hands (or the right ones), it could upend the balance of power across the entire district.
