# The Interchange

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // The Highway Sprawl*

## The Cloverleaf

*Rig-runner staging & wheelman market*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The ramps find you before you find them — four sweeping curves of cracked ferrocrete braiding up and over each other into a knot two hundred meters wide, stacked three levels deep, and every centimeter of it is moving. There is no sky above the topmost ribbon, only the gray underbelly of the level above, dripping its slow warm rain down through a thousand expansion joints so the whole cloverleaf glistens like something alive. Headlights streak the wet concrete in long smears of white and red. Above the ramps, courier-drones stitch between the girders in tight formation; a rusted cargo hovercraft grinds along on its cushion, too big for the lane it's claimed, air-horn screaming at the ground-rigs weaving beneath it. And down in the bowl at the center of the cloverleaf — the dead lot where four ramps used to funnel toward a highway that hasn't existed in three centuries — that's the market.

Wheelmen lean on hoods under strings of salvaged lumen-wire, engines idling in a chorus that never quite stops, headlamps and taillights painting the standing rain gold and blood-red. Somebody's sound system thumps a bassline that fights the sub-foundation heartbeat for dominance and loses. A dozen crews watch you cross the lot before you've taken five steps — sizing up your boots, your chrome, whether you're buying a ride, selling one, or about to become a problem.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ressa "Redline" Okonkwo** — unofficial queen of the Cloverleaf market; runs the informal exchange where crews rent, sell, or wager rigs. Corran guild-clan by blood, freelance by temperament. Takes a cut of every deal she witnesses and witnesses everything.

**Dob the Appraiser** — a squat, chrome-armed grease-priest of an ex-Ferrum line mechanic who can price any vehicle by ear alone. Owes Nyx money he'll never openly admit to.

**Little Static** — teenage technomancer runner who scouts buyers for stolen rigs by riding the Wired ahead of the market; skittish, brilliant, and terrified of Meridian.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — contested, held loosely by whichever wheelman crew has the most rigs parked at a given hour. No standing gang owns the Cloverleaf; it survives on mutual need.

Violence here is bad for business, so it's rare and fast when it happens — someone pulls a weapon, someone else revs an engine as a warning, and within seconds six vehicles have boxed the offender in. **Alert** triggers if gunfire draws a Sanctum drone flyover; the market scatters into the ramps in under a minute and reconvenes an hour later at a different level.

Redline keeps two "lot marshals" — retired Ironclad muscle — who mostly just glare.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public AR is a mess of overlapping "for sale" tags, half of them scams, hovering over hoods like ghosts arguing with each other. Meridian's traffic-net paints the ramps above in live congestion color — and the market exploits gaps in that coverage to move stolen rigs invisibly for a few minutes at a time.

A hacker who digs past the noise finds a private trade-ledger broadcast on a rotating frequency only Redline's regulars know — real prices, real ownership history, real warrants.

Little Static maintains a whisper-network dead-drop tagged only as a flickering taillight icon — contact for anyone who needs a rig to vanish from every registry that matters.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Ghost Title. A rig on the lot carries forged Sanctum registration good enough to pass checkpoints district-wide — and someone from Sanctum's actual fraud desk has noticed the pattern and is quietly buying up "test" units to trace the forger. The crew's ride might be one of them.

2. Redline's Debt. Nyx wants a cut of the Cloverleaf's trade that Redline has refused for years. She hires the crew to make one very public example of the next Nyx collector who tries to muscle the lot — knowing it may start a war she can't win alone.

3. Little Static's Find. Riding the Wired to scout a buyer, Little Static stumbles on convoy manifests suggesting Ironclad is running unmarked cargo through the district's ramps at night — cargo that isn't on any legitimate contract. She wants protection in exchange for the data.

## Pump Row

*Fuel-cell black market & charging pits*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Heat hits you before the light does — a wall of it rolling off a hundred jury-rigged charging pits sunk into what used to be a maintenance yard beneath the eastbound ribbon. Fuel-cell racks glow low amber and sick green through cracked housings, venting steam straight up into the underside of the level above, where it mixes with the endless warm rain and drifts back down as a fine caustic drizzle that tastes like batteries on your tongue. There's no sky here, just girders and drone-lanes and, far off through a gap in the ramps, the cold burn of the Halo, utterly indifferent to the men and women haggling over cell charges twenty stories beneath it.

Ground-rigs queue three-deep at the good pits, engines ticking as they cool, while overhead a lattice of makeshift power-lines feeds off a tapped Ferrum grid line nobody up top has noticed missing yet. A cargo-crawler eases past with a load of scavenged cells rattling in its bed; a hovercab drops low to top off at a pit clearly not meant for aircraft, its driver arguing price with a vendor in three languages at once. The noise is engines and static and the hiss-crackle of bad wiring, and under it all, the sub-bass pulse of the tower's ancient pumps — a heartbeat Pump Row seems to have decided to imitate, badly, and on purpose.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Marsh "Volt" Calder** — runs the largest string of pits on the Row; former Ferrum grid technician who "liberated" his skills along with a truck of equipment when he left. Fair dealer, ruthless about theft.

**Sister Amara** — not truly clergy, but the street-faith washing-rite tradition rubbed off on her; she blesses fuel cells before big runs for a donation, and half the wheelmen genuinely believe it helps.

**The Skinner** — Nyx-connected cell smuggler who moves military-grade power cores stripped from Ironclad surplus; deals only through intermediaries and never shows his face twice to the same buyer.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — lawless in the sense that no faction claims it, but Volt's reputation functions as law. Cross him and no pit on the Row will serve you.

The real danger isn't people — it's the pits themselves. Bad cells vent, overloaded lines arc, and at least once a tenday something catches fire. **Alert** here usually means a HALO-contracted fire-suppression drone descending uninvited, which draws Sanctum interest fast and empties the Row in a panic.

Volt keeps a rotating crew of "line-walkers" armed with fire-axes and stun batons, more firefighters than guards.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Ferrum's grid-management AR paints the tapped lines as fully accounted for — someone with real access is falsifying the load reports, and has been for years. Pull that thread and you find a Ferrum insider taking a cut to keep looking away.

A hacker can read cell-charge histories off any rack to trace where a given power core has traveled — useful for tracking stolen Ironclad hardware back through the Skinner's supply chain.

Sister Amara's blessing-rite includes a genuine service: she runs a low-key dead-drop for runners needing to pass warnings up and down the Row without the Wired ever seeing it, using chalk marks on cell housings.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Tapped Line. The Ferrum insider falsifying grid reports has gotten greedy, rerouting enough power to risk a district-wide brownout. Volt wants the leak found and fixed quietly before Ferrum audits the whole Row out of existence.

2. Military Grade. The Skinner has a lot of six Ironclad combat-frame power cores to move — hot enough that Ironclad has already sent quiet inquiries down the chain. The crew can fence them, steal them first, or sell the tip to Ironclad for a bounty.

3. Sister Amara's Warning. A chalk-mark dead-drop message warns of "bad cells going out fast" — someone is deliberately selling rigged power cores that overload on the highway, and the pattern points at a rival trying to discredit Volt's whole operation.

## Toll Ghost

*Abandoned Sanctum checkpoint; ambush spot*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The booths are still standing — six of them, straddling the ramp in a row like broken teeth, their toll-arms frozen at half-mast and their Sanctum sigils long since spray-slagged past recognition. Whatever actuarial calculus decided this checkpoint wasn't worth the premium anymore, it left the infrastructure behind: dead scanner-gantries overhead, cracked plexi booths, a faded holo-sign still trying to flicker RISK ASSESSMENT MANDATORY into rain that has nowhere else to fall except straight down from the underside of the ramp above. No sky, just wet concrete ceiling forty meters up, strobed gold and cyan by the drone-traffic and the one flickering streetlight that refuses to die.

Ground-rigs slow here out of old habit even though nothing enforces it anymore, engines echoing between the booths in a way that makes every vehicle sound louder, more alone. A courier-drone hangs motionless above the center booth, scanning — but its registry ping comes back blank, a ghost of Sanctum surveillance still running on inertia with no one home to read the feed. Something about the stillness after the engine-noise fades is worse than any of the district's louder places. You can feel eyes that might not be attached to anyone at all.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Harlan Vess** — squats in booth three, a former Sanctum toll-clerk who never got the memo that the checkpoint closed; sells "toll passage" to spooked newcomers who don't know better, and genuine intel to those who do.

**The Quiet Crew** — a small ambush gang (four to six) who use the booths' blind angles to stage highway robbery on solo rigs; loosely Cinderhold-adjacent, desperate rather than organized.

**Ozel** — a technomancer drifter who camps in the dead scanner-gantry, listening to the checkpoint's residual Wired traffic like static from a dead radio station; knows things she shouldn't.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — nominally abandoned, actually claimed by whoever's holding the booths that week. No standing authority.

There is no real enforcement, which is the whole danger — the Quiet Crew relies on the checkpoint's reputation to keep victims compliant until it's too late. **Alert** here means the ambush has gone loud enough to draw attention from Ironclad convoy escorts on nearby routes, who will not hesitate to make an example.

The dead scanner-gantry occasionally still triggers false Sanctum alerts, which can bring a genuinely confused automated response unit that hasn't been decommissioned from the district's old contract.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The checkpoint's AR is a corrupted loop — old toll-rate holograms replaying degraded fragments of pricing structures a decade obsolete, ghost-data nobody bothered to purge.

A hacker digging into the dead gantry finds a still-active but forgotten Sanctum surveillance log, silently recording every plate and SIN that passes through — a goldmine of blackmail material or evidence, uncollected for years.

Ozel has tapped that log for her own purposes and will trade fragments of it for things she needs — food, parts, company.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Forgotten Log. That dormant Sanctum surveillance feed has been quietly recording every vehicle through the Toll Ghost for a decade — including, the crew learns, a very specific convoy movement someone powerful would pay enormous scrip to have erased.

2. Harlan's Toll. Harlan's fake toll-collection racket has accidentally intersected with a real Sanctum audit trying to figure out why decommissioned equipment is still generating revenue logs. The crew can protect him, expose him, or use his cover for their own passage.

3. The Quiet Crew's Last Job. The ambush gang has bitten off more than they can chew — their latest victim was an Ironclad courier under convoy protection, and Ironclad wants blood. The crew is offered payment to either hand the gang over or help them disappear before the convoy escort arrives.

## The Underpass

*Squatter camp beneath the stack*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Where three ramps stack directly over each other, the space beneath becomes its own kind of shelter — dry-ish, dim, and claimed. Tarps and salvaged sheet-metal lean against the support pylons in a sprawl that's grown organically over years, laundry lines strung between rebar stubs, cookfires smoking under the lowest ribbon where the chemical rain can't quite reach. There is no sky, obviously — there's barely a ceiling you can see, just black concrete disappearing up into the gap between levels, lit by the passing headlights of the ramp traffic above throwing long moving shadows across the camp like a slow-motion strobe. Somewhere close, a ground-rig downshifts hard on the curve overhead and the whole underpass seems to shudder with it.

Children run between the pylons with a freedom that would get them killed two levels up. An old man tends a still made from a stolen fuel-cell heating element. The air traffic above never stops — you can hear the drones, the hovercraft, the endless engine-drone bleeding down through the gaps in the ramp deck — but down here, under the stack, it's muffled into something almost like weather. As you approach, dozens of eyes track you from the shadows between the tarps, and nobody moves to greet you, which is its own kind of message.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Ilsabet** — the camp's de facto elder, a Cinderhold exile who has held this underpass together for eleven years through sheer stubbornness and a reputation for fairness; decides who's welcome.

**Petro** — sharp-eyed teenage lookout network coordinator; runs kids as spotters for approaching trouble in exchange for food and protection.

**Dr. Yuen** — a burned-SIN former Caduceus intern who runs a threadbare clinic out of a cargo container, doing what she can with black-market meds and old training.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — self-governed squatter law under Mother Ilsabet; outsiders tolerated on good behavior, not otherwise.

The camp has no weapons to speak of beyond improvised tools, but numbers and knowledge of the terrain make it dangerous to raid. **Alert** here typically means a Ferrum "clearance" notice — the conglomerate technically owns the ground and occasionally remembers it, sending contracted muscle to threaten eviction that never quite completes.

Petro's lookout kids can empty the camp of anything valuable in under two minutes flat when trouble is spotted early enough.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Officially, the space beneath the stack registers as "unoccupied structural clearance" — the camp doesn't exist on any Ferrum survey, which is exactly how Mother Ilsabet wants it.

A hacker who looks closely finds that someone has been quietly feeding false sensor data to Ferrum's property-management AI for years to keep the camp off eviction schedules — a debt owed to a sympathetic insider nobody in the camp has ever met.

Dr. Yuen runs a dead-drop medical request board via a scavenged terminal, visible only to those who know the camp's private frequency — she trades information for supplies as often as scrip.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Real Eviction. This time the Ferrum clearance notice is real — a new superblock development wants the underpass cleared for construction staging. Mother Ilsabet needs the crew to either buy time, find leverage against the developer, or help the camp relocate before Ironclad muscle arrives.

2. The Sympathetic Insider. The Ferrum employee who's been hiding the camp from eviction schedules has gone quiet — missing, reassigned, or caught. Without them, the camp's protection collapses within weeks, and someone needs to find out what happened.

3. Petro's Discovery. One of Petro's lookout kids found something in the ramp support structure during a game — a Sanctum-era data cache, sealed decades ago, that several very interested parties would kill to recover before the camp realizes what it's sitting on.

## Chop Circle

*Roundabout chop-shops; boosted cars*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The roundabout was built for traffic that stopped needing it three centuries ago, and what's grown up around its edge is a ring of chop-shops packed so tight their corrugated awnings overlap — a dozen bays lit sodium-orange, each one mid-surgery on some stolen rig, engines pulled half-out on chain hoists, chassis stripped to skeletons. Above the circle, the ramps spiral up and away into the dark underside of the next level, chemical rain sheeting off their edges in curtains that catch the shop-lights and throw them back gold and rust-red across the wet asphalt. No sky — just that endless concrete belly overhead, and threading through the gap between it and the roundabout, drones and a lone battered hovercraft circling patiently, waiting for airspace to clear over the chop bays below.

The sound here is specific: grinders, pneumatic wrenches, a radio playing something with too much bass, and underneath all of it the idle rumble of engines being tested, revved, argued over. Every bay's crew watches the roundabout's center lane where the "clean" traffic still loops through, out of habit more than law, and watches you too — because anyone walking into Chop Circle on foot is either buying, selling, or lost, and the third option gets expensive fast.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Big Fen Okoro** — controls the largest bay and enforces a rough peace among the circle's rival chop crews; ex-wheelman himself, missing three fingers from a job gone wrong, deeply superstitious about "cursed" rigs.

**Whiskey Tam** — freelance re-VIN specialist who can make a stolen rig's registry history vanish for the right price; paranoid, brilliant, works alone.

**Detective Oyelaran (unofficial)** — a Sanctum fraud investigator who's gone rogue-adjacent, taking bribes from some bays to look the other way while building a case file on others; nobody's quite sure whose side she's actually on.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — contested among chop crews, held together by Big Fen's enforced truce more than any real authority.

Violence between bays is common but contained — territorial disputes, not open war, since burning down a rival's bay tends to draw exactly the kind of attention nobody wants. **Alert** escalates sharply if a "clean" theft victim tracks their rig here personally or with hired muscle; the circle closes ranks fast and rigs get re-chopped or moved within the hour.

Big Fen keeps a small standing crew of enforcers who mediate disputes before they become gunfights — mostly.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public AR shows Chop Circle as a legitimate salvage-and-repair market — a Ferrum-registered front that provides just enough cover for the actual business underneath.

A hacker digging past that front finds Whiskey Tam's re-VIN ledger, a goldmine of laundered vehicle histories that could unravel half the district's rig ownership if leaked.

Detective Oyelaran maintains a private surveillance feed on three specific bays, building a case file she hasn't decided what to do with — accessible to anyone skilled enough to find it, and dangerous to whoever does.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Oyelaran's Case File. The rogue Sanctum detective's case file, if it goes anywhere official, would collapse half of Chop Circle overnight. Someone wants it stolen; someone else wants it protected; the crew gets caught choosing a side.

2. The Cursed Rig. A vehicle that passed through Big Fen's bay has left three chop-crews dead under strange circumstances — Fen's superstition might be right this time, and the truth traces back to a technomancer's ghost-marked getaway car from a much bigger job.

3. Whiskey Tam's Ledger. Tam's re-VIN ledger has been copied — by whom, nobody knows, but every bay owner in the circle now suspects each other, and the resulting paranoia is about to turn violent unless someone finds the real thief fast.

## Roadside Shrine

*Wreck-memorial; runner blessing stop*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

It started as a single wrecked rig, decades ago, left where it crashed against a support pylon because moving it wasn't worth the effort. Now it's a shrine three meters tall — layers of melted candle-wax, chrome trinkets, faded photographs laminated against the rain, and hundreds of small offerings wired or taped or simply wedged into every gap in the wreck's rusted frame. Above it, the ramp curves close enough that headlights sweep across the shrine every few seconds, a slow strobing benediction, and beyond that curve there's no sky at all — just the dark gap between levels where a courier-drone hovers a moment too long, as if paying respects of its own, before continuing on its route.

The rain here runs down the wreck's ruined windshield like something almost like tears, chemical-warm, catching the shrine's candlelight in a way that makes the whole memorial glow faintly gold against the wet dark. Somewhere behind you a rig-runner slows almost to a stop — an old habit, a tap of the horn, a murmured word — before accelerating back into traffic. You feel the weight of it as you approach: a place where a hundred small griefs have been left, and are still being left, one candle at a time.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Brother Casimir** — a street-faith caretaker in the Lamp-on-Ninth tradition who tends the shrine, refreshes candles, and remembers every name carved into the wreck, whether he knew the person or not.

**Wysa "Ghostmarker" Renn** — a wheelman who lost her crew in a highway ambush years back and now paints memorial marks on rigs before dangerous runs; half-superstition, half genuine ritual the district has adopted wholesale.

**The Listener** — an unidentified figure who leaves cryptic offerings at the shrine every few days; some say it's a technomancer paying respects to a friend lost to the Wired itself.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — unclaimed, but universally respected; even hostile crews observe an unspoken truce at the shrine.

There is essentially no violence here by unwritten consensus — it would be an act every wheelman crew in the district would remember and punish collectively. **Alert** effectively doesn't apply; disturbing the shrine itself is the only real trigger, and it summons social consequences (ostracism, refused service district-wide) rather than armed ones.

Brother Casimir's only real defense is the district's respect for him, which has so far been enough.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The shrine carries no official AR tags at all — a rare deliberate blank in the Wired, maintained by mutual agreement among local data-runners who've quietly blocked advertisers from tagging it.

A hacker who looks will find something unexpected: hundreds of tiny encrypted memory-fragments hidden in the shrine's offerings by grieving runners, informal digital wills and last messages, an accidental archive of the district's dead.

The Listener's offerings, if traced, carry a recurring data-signature that matches no known SIN — suggesting either extreme skill at erasure or something not entirely human leaving them.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Listener's Signal. The unidentified figure leaving offerings is tracked, via the Wired signature, to a technomancer believed dead in a run gone wrong years ago — dead, or something else, and the truth matters enormously to someone still grieving them.

2. The Encrypted Wills. One of the shrine's hidden memory-fragments contains evidence of a corp conspiracy the deceased runner died trying to expose — and now their message is finally found, years too late to save them but perhaps in time to matter.

3. Breaking the Truce. A new gang, ignorant of or indifferent to the shrine's sanctity, desecrates it for a message to a rival. The district's collective fury needs somewhere to go, and the crew can direct it, exploit it, or get caught in its path.

## Meridian Gantry

*Traffic-net relay; watched*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The gantry rises from the center of the interchange like a spider that decided to stay, a lattice tower of dishes and repeater arrays bolted to the highest ramp-support in the district, humming with a frequency you can almost feel in your teeth. Meridian's sigil glows steady cyan near the top, one of the only lights in the district that never flickers, never dims, immune to whatever chaos moves beneath it. There's no sky above it either — just the underside of the next level up, close enough that the gantry's upper dishes nearly scrape it, chemical rain sluicing off the array in sheets that the gantry's own floodlights turn into falling cyan static.

Beneath it, the interchange's traffic flows exactly as Meridian's relay dictates — ground-rigs slotted into lanes by signal, hovercraft holding pattern on cue, courier-drones weaving in patterns too precise to be anything but managed. You feel it as you approach: the sense of being counted, timed, logged, the way every vehicle here moves like it's part of a machine you can't see the rest of. A speaker somewhere near the gantry's base crackles out routine traffic advisories in a synthesized voice utterly indifferent to the rain, the noise, the six tongues of the crowd it's talking over.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Analyst Priya Devrath** — Meridian technician stationed at the gantry's ground-level relay hub; competent, bored, and quietly willing to sell traffic-pattern data to whoever asks the right way.

**Rook** — a wererraven data-courier who uses the gantry's dead zones and signal shadows as a rest stop between runs, and knows the tower's blind spots better than Meridian does.

**Supervisor Halkett** — Devrath's superior, a true corp believer who treats the gantry's uptime like a religious duty and reports anomalies zealously.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Meridian-controlled infrastructure island inside otherwise lawless territory; a genuine corp presence, small but real.

The gantry itself is lightly guarded — a couple of contracted Ironclad techs for physical security — because its real defense is surveillance and response time, not muscle. **Alert** here is fast and digital: tampering trips Meridian's own network before a human ever notices, summoning a rapid-response security drone within minutes.

Halkett escalates every anomaly by the book; Devrath escalates only what she can't quietly bury.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

This is the source of the local Wired signal for the whole district — every AR tag, every traffic light, every SIN-reader within blocks routes through this gantry. It is, quite literally, one of the most visible nodes on the map for anyone watching from up-tower.

A hacker skilled enough to breach the relay can reroute or blind traffic-net coverage across a significant stretch of the Interchange — an enormously valuable and enormously dangerous capability.

Rook has mapped every signal shadow the gantry casts and will trade that map, cautiously, to runners who prove they're not Meridian plants.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Devrath's Side Deal. Analyst Devrath has been selling convoy movement patterns to a chop-shop syndicate for a cut, and Supervisor Halkett is starting to notice discrepancies. The crew gets pulled into covering the leak, exposing it, or exploiting it themselves.

2. The Blind Spot. Someone wants the gantry's coverage blinded for exactly ninety seconds during a specific convoy's passage — long enough for an ambush the crew is hired to either stage or stop.

3. Rook's Warning. The wereraven courier has noticed Meridian quietly expanding the gantry's surveillance radius far beyond its stated traffic-management mandate — a sign, perhaps, of the war climbing the foundations reaching even this deep into the Flats.

## The Weigh Station

*Ironclad convoy checkpoint*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Floodlights turn the old weigh station into a pool of stark white in a district that otherwise runs on neon and shadow, and the effect is deliberate — Ironclad wants you seen the moment you're seen. Concrete barriers funnel traffic into a single lane beneath the station's scale-deck, and above it the ramps stack three levels high, chemical rain falling in curtains through the gaps between them, lit gold and white by the crossing beams of the floodlights and the running lights of the convoy rigs idling in the queue. There's no sky, only that dense concrete underside far above, but the vertical space is packed — a hovercraft escort holding position overhead, drones sweeping methodical grid patterns, ground-rigs waiting their turn in a line that moves with military patience.

Ironclad operators in matte-black combat-frame plating check manifests against cargo with scanners that hum audibly over the engine-noise, unhurried, unbothered by the queue building behind you. Every vehicle that passes through gets logged, weighed, cleared or flagged, and the whole process has the particular tension of a place where the rules are simple, enforced, and entirely someone else's to write.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Captain Rael Dumont** — Ironclad officer commanding the checkpoint; professional, unbending about protocol, but pragmatic enough to negotiate for the right price or the right favor.

**Tech-Sergeant Bahn** — runs the scanner deck; sharp-eyed, has caught more smuggled cargo than anyone else on the station's rotation, takes genuine pride in it.

**Faro** — a fixer who works the queue itself, offering "expedited processing" to nervous drivers for a fee that quietly reaches Dumont's off-books account.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — a genuine Ironclad-controlled enclave, one of the few hard-security points in the district.

This is real military-grade security: combat-frame operators, coordinated drone overwatch, and a hovercraft escort on immediate standby. **Alert** here means an actual armed response within seconds, not minutes — Ironclad does not bluff about enforcement, and a firefight at the Weigh Station draws reinforcements fast.

Corruption exists (Faro's arrangement with Dumont) but is carefully hidden behind the checkpoint's legitimate, visible rigor.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Weigh Station runs one of the most complete SIN-and-cargo verification systems in the district — every manifest cross-checked in real time against Sanctum insurance records and Ferrum shipping contracts.

A hacker who breaches the scanner deck's system can forge a clean manifest convincing enough to pass Bahn's scrutiny — a rare and valuable capability worth a great deal to the right smuggler.

Faro's "expedited processing" scheme leaves a data trail even he doesn't know exists, buried in the station's own audit logs — evidence that could end Dumont's career if surfaced.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Expedited Processing. Faro's bribery scheme is about to be discovered by an incoming Ironclad internal auditor. He hires the crew to help him and Dumont bury the evidence — or someone else hires the crew to make sure it isn't buried.

2. The Flagged Cargo. Tech-Sergeant Bahn flags a convoy shipment that doesn't match its manifest — the crew is offered a job either escorting that cargo safely past the flag or intercepting it before it clears the station, and both sides are willing to pay well.

3. A War's Logistics. Dumont receives sealed orders to prioritize a particular convoy route above all civilian traffic for the next several days — a sign that "violence is logistics," and that Ironclad is quietly staging for something bigger climbing up from the Sinks.

## Neon Mile

*Drive-thru simsense & vice strip*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Mile announces itself in light long before you reach it — a half-kilometer stretch of ramp-frontage lit so densely in pink and magenta that the chemical rain itself seems to glow, every drop catching color on the way down like a curtain of dissolving candy. There's no sky above the strip, just the underside of the next level, but you'd hardly notice; the Mile has replaced the sky with its own ceiling of light, holo-marquees stacked six deep advertising simsense lounges, drive-thru pleasure-booths, and tailored-dream dispensaries in overlapping come-ons that blur into a single continuous seduction.

Ground-rigs cruise the Mile slow, windows down despite the rain, drivers haggling prices with barkers who lean into open windows without breaking stride. Overhead, hovercabs queue for the strip's elevated pickup gantries while courier-drones dodge between marquee arms strung with dripping cable. The sound is a wall — six languages of pitch, thumping bass leaking from a dozen open doorways, the hiss of rain on hot neon housings — and under it, if you know to listen, the low electronic murmur of a hundred simsense feeds bleeding faintly into the air like a headache waiting to happen.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Madame Sable** — proprietor of the Mile's most prestigious drive-thru lounge, an Obsidian-licensed operation selling tailored dreams from armored booths; smooth, watchful, protective of her strip's reputation.

**Ticker** — a BTL dealer working the strip's shadow economy, selling bootleg simsense to those Obsidian won't serve or can't afford; constantly moving, constantly nervous.

**Deacon Vray** — runs a "wellness check" scam disguised as street ministry, preying on burnt-out simsense addicts drifting off the Mile; genuinely charismatic, genuinely predatory.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Obsidian-influenced but not Obsidian-controlled; the corp licenses the legitimate lounges and looks the other way at everything else in exchange for a cut.

Lounge security is private and discreet — bouncers who prefer de-escalation to violence, since scenes are bad for business. **Alert** typically means a customer overdosing or a BTL batch going bad publicly; response is fast but focused on containment and cleanup rather than punishment.

Ticker's BTL trade draws occasional Caduceus-adjacent health inspectors investigating bad batches, which is the closest thing to real law the Mile sees.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Mile's AR is aggressively, almost violently dense — every marquee layered with targeted advertising keyed to biometric scans from the drive-thru booths, reading mood and spending history before you've rolled down the window.

A hacker digging beneath the ad-layer finds Madame Sable's client ledger, an extremely valuable and extremely dangerous list of who among the district's powerful indulges in what.

Ticker's BTL network runs on a hidden dead-drop frequency broadcast from a modified drone that never lands in the same spot twice — a genuine challenge to trace, and worth plenty to whoever manages it.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Sable's Ledger. Someone has stolen a partial copy of Madame Sable's client ledger and is shopping it around as blackmail material. She wants it recovered quietly — and wants to know exactly who saw it first.

2. Bad Batch. Ticker's latest BTL shipment is causing violent seizures in users, and word is starting to spread. The crew can trace the batch back to its source before Caduceus inspectors — or angry customers — do it more permanently.

3. Deacon Vray's Congregation. A grieving family hires the crew to extract a relative from Vray's predatory "wellness" scheme — only to discover the Deacon has genuine Obsidian backing, making him more dangerous to cross than he looks.

## Dead Ramp

*Collapsed ramp; gang toll & drop*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Half the ramp is simply gone — sheared away decades ago in some collapse nobody bothered to document, leaving a jagged concrete stump forty meters up that juts into empty air like a broken bone. What's left curves down into a bottleneck choke point, one lane where there used to be four, and that bottleneck is exactly why it's valuable to somebody. There's no sky past the wound in the ramp above, just the raw dark gap between levels, chemical rain falling harder here where there's no upper ribbon to catch it first, hammering the broken concrete in a way that sounds almost like applause. Drones give the collapse a wide berth out of old habit; a hovercraft threads carefully past the exposed rebar, its running lights catching the wet edges in flickers of gold.

As you approach the chokepoint, you see the toll booth someone's built from scavenged shipping containers, chains looped across the lane, and the small crowd of enforcers lounging in the container's shadow with the bored, dangerous patience of people who do this every single day. A ground-rig ahead of you stops, pays, and rolls on without a word exchanged beyond the transaction — the arrangement here is old enough that nobody argues about it anymore, not out loud.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Cutter Voss** — leader of the gang running the Dead Ramp toll, a former demolition worker who half-suspects (correctly) that the original collapse wasn't entirely an accident; ruthless about the toll, oddly protective of the ramp's structural secrets.

**Nix** — Voss's second, a quiet fixer who uses the toll booth as a cover for a genuine dead-drop service, running messages and small packages for anyone willing to pay extra.

**Old Ferrum Survey Marker** — not a person, but a half-buried Ferrum inspection drone, dormant for years, that still occasionally reactivates and broadcasts confused structural-integrity warnings nobody has bothered to shut off.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — hard-claimed gang territory; the toll is the law here, full stop.

Voss's crew is armed and numerous enough to make the chokepoint genuinely dangerous to force — the collapsed geometry means there's nowhere to maneuver, only through. **Alert** here means Voss's enforcers calling in reinforcements from a nearby holdout; refusing the toll or resisting escalates to violence almost immediately, with the terrain favoring the defenders completely.

The old Ferrum survey drone's occasional reactivation sometimes triggers a genuine structural inspection request, which nobody has ever actually shown up to answer.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The collapse registers on Ferrum's infrastructure maps as "pending remediation," a status that has apparently been pending for over a decade — a bureaucratic ghost that conveniently keeps official attention away.

A hacker who reactivates or interrogates the buried survey drone can pull the original structural report from the collapse — and Voss's suspicion turns out correct: the damage pattern doesn't match a natural failure. Someone brought this ramp down on purpose.

Nix's dead-drop service runs through a tag on the toll booth's container wall, visible in AR only to those given the mark — used by several factions in the district as a neutral message point specifically because Voss's toll makes the location so reliably staffed.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Whose Collapse. The buried survey drone's data proves the Dead Ramp's collapse was deliberate sabotage — decades old, motive unclear, but the signature of the demolition charges matches a technique still used by a very specific, very old Ironclad-adjacent demolitions specialist who's still active in the district.

2. Nix's Overreach. Nix has been running dead-drop messages for a faction Voss would never knowingly help — and when Voss finds out, the toll gang's internal loyalty fractures right as a rival crew moves to take the chokepoint.

3. The Toll War. A rival gang wants the Dead Ramp's chokepoint for themselves and is willing to go to open war for it, threatening to turn the district's most reliable shortcut into a battlefield — and everyone who depends on that route, from wheelmen to Ironclad convoys, has a stake in how it resolves.
