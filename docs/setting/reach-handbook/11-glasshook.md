# Glasshook

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // The Fallen Arcade*

## The Fallen Atrium

*Central court; the arcade's throne*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The concourse ahead simply stops being a concourse — the floor buckles, the ceiling drops away, and you're standing at the lip of a wound a thousand feet across. This was the arcology's grand atrium once, nine stories of glass and gallery ringing a single vertical space so shoppers could look up from the sub-levels and see daylight. The daylight is long gone. What's left is a crater of collapsed floors slumped into each other like a fanned deck of cards, glass domes crushed to green gravel underfoot, and at the bottom, lit by a hundred stolen signs, a court that never stops being a court.

There is no sky here — there never was, this deep, and now there isn't even the pretense of one. The underside of Grid-level structure presses low overhead, a ceiling of pipe and girder sweating that warm chemical rain down through gaps where the old dome glass used to be, so the whole crater glitters wet under neon the way a wound glitters wet under a light. Above the throne-space, the traffic layers stack the way they do everywhere in the Flats — courier-drones weaving formation through the broken dome ribs, a boosted hovercab riding its cushion along what used to be the third-floor railing, ground-rigs grinding over rubble ramps someone graded smooth with a stolen paver-bot — and none of it slows down for the crater, because the crater is the one fixed point everyone in Glasshook orbits.

At the bottom, thrones built from register-desks and busted escalator treads face each other across bare concrete, and on the worst nights you can hear, under the rain-hiss and drone-whine and six tongues of shouting, the sub-bass thump of speakers wired straight into the tower's dying foundation-pumps — the arcade's throne, still holding court a thousand years after the last sale ever rang.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Corla** — self-styled "Queen of the Crater," an ex-Corran labor boss who took the Atrium by holding it longest, not hardest; runs a rough parliament of gang seconds and demands tribute in information more than scrip.

**Ledger** — Corla's accountant-enforcer, a wire-thin figure with a hand-terminal full of every debt owed in Glasshook; polite, exact, terrifying because he never raises his voice.

**Brennik Oss** — an Ironclad deserter turned freelance muscle who "keeps the peace" for a cut, meaning he decides whose fight gets to happen.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — gang-held neutral court under Mother Corla's uneasy parliament; no corp presence, no single gang controls it outright.

Violence here is a diplomatic incident, not a firefight — drawing steel in the crater without Corla's nod summons every gang second at once, and the "response" is the room deciding your fate by vote. Genuine armed intervention only comes from Brennik's crew, who arrive slow and leave no survivors on the side that started it.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The public AR over the crater is a wreck of dead ad-ghosts — a thousand looping storefront holograms still trying to sell products that stopped existing a century ago, glitching through the rain like drowned signage. Under that noise, Ledger runs a private debt-ledger layer only visible with a key he controls personally.

A skilled hacker can find the old arcology's structural-sensor net, still half-alive, quietly reporting load-stress on the collapsing floors above — worth knowing before you stand under one.

Dead-drop tags cluster on the old information-kiosk stumps ringing the crater floor, Corla's parliament using them as a public bulletin board for gang business.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Vote. A rival crew wants Corla gone and needs deniable muscle to make the "accident" look natural during a parliament session. Taking the job — or warning her — reshapes who holds Glasshook's throne.

2. Foundation Failure. The structural-sensor ghost-net is flagging total collapse risk on the crater's western tier. Nyx wants the crew to quietly confirm it before betting cargo routes on the district; Ferrum wants it buried so they don't have to pay for condemnation.

3. The Debt Auction. Ledger's terminal holds decades of favors and debts across every district gang. Someone up-tower wants a copy — and Ledger, cornered, offers the crew a worse deal: steal it for him, and vanish it before anyone else can.

## The Dead Mall

*Simsense & vice in old shopfronts*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The concourse narrows into a canyon of dead storefronts, and every single one of them is alive with something it was never built to sell. Steel shutters that once guarded shoes and sim-toys have been pried back and hung with beaded curtains, blacklight tarps, hand-painted signs promising things the original tenant never could. Obsidian's product moves through here wholesale-cheap and three grades rougher than the Spires ever see — dream-cable coiled in shopping baskets, BTL cartridges racked on what used to be a jewelry counter, a woman in a broken display window running a live feed no license anywhere admits exists.

No sky reaches this deep in the arcade's ribs — only the sagging underside of the level above, patched with tarps where the ceiling gave way, dripping its warm chemical rain straight down onto the crowd, who don't seem to notice anymore. The neon here is cheaper and closer than anywhere else in Glasshook: hand-strung LED ropes looping storefront to storefront at head height, so the whole canyon glows pink and sodium-gold at a distance no upscale district would tolerate. Above the shutter-line, in the gap where the second concourse used to run, courier-drones dart low and fast — this is a delivery route now, not a walkway — while a scavenged ground-rig idles at the canyon mouth, engine ticking, waiting on a fare too strung out to remember where they live.

The sound wall here is different from the rest of the district — less shouting, more murmur, a hundred half-conversations pitched low over simsense hums and the wet static of cheap dream-cable running warm. It's the sound of people trying very hard not to be noticed wanting what they want.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Vess Karo** — the Dead Mall's resident Obsidian franchise-holder, technically unlicensed, tolerated because he pays the right people and never lets product get too pure or too dangerous to be deniable.

**The Widow** — an ex-simsense actress running the live-feed booth, her rig older than her, selling access to a decade of recorded lives she can no longer legally claim as her own.

**Pinny** — a teenage runner who moves BTL cartridges between shopfronts in a gutted shopping cart; knows every hidden stash in the canyon and sells that knowledge too, if the price is right.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Obsidian-adjacent vice market, tolerated gang presence (Karo's crew), no corp enforcement.

Karo keeps two enforcers posted at the canyon's mouth who care about one thing: that nothing here draws corp Alert. Actual violence is common between customers and dealers but rarely escalates beyond the shopfront it started in — the crowd simply flows around it like water around a rock.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

AR tags bloom over every shutter — Obsidian's ghost-branding still legally "owns" some of these storefronts on paper, meaning the public overlay shows glossy simsense ads for products that were discontinued before the arcology fell. Underneath, a hacker finds a black-market tag-layer: real menus, real prices, hidden behind the corporate ghost-skin.

The Widow's booth broadcasts on a frequency that isn't supposed to exist anymore; anyone jacked in nearby gets bleed-through of old recorded lives at the edges of their vision, a known hazard the locals call "ghost-flash."

Sanctum's actuarial crawlers occasionally sweep the district for insurance-fraud patterns; their presence here is entirely digital and entirely unwelcome.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Bad Batch. A shipment of dream-cable has started putting users into comas instead of highs. Karo needs it traced back before Obsidian notices its brand is killing people in an "unlicensed" market they'll deny ever existed.

2. Ghost-Flash. The Widow's decades-old broadcast is bleeding something new into the feed — fragments that don't match any of her recorded lives. Someone, or something, may be riding her signal.

3. Pinny's Ledger. The kid knows too much about too many stash spots, and someone wants that knowledge erased along with her. The crew is hired to get to her first — for very different reasons, depending on who's paying.

## Ring Market

*Curved gallery bazaar*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The gallery curves away from you in both directions, a smooth arc of shopfronts that once formed one ring of the arcology's outer concourse, and somehow — against every odd in Glasshook — half of it still functions the way it was built to. Stalls have replaced the boutiques, tarps strung gallery-rail to gallery-rail, and the whole curved length is packed with the loudest, most functional commerce left in the district: printed circuit off-cuts, reclaimed water filters, food that's actually food, chrome that's actually sterile.

The dome above this stretch held longer than most, so the "sky" is at least a real ceiling instead of open wound — the underside of a cracked skylight dome, dark glass sheeted with condensation, letting the warm chemical rain seep through in threads rather than sheets, dripping onto tarps that vendors have long since angled to drain it away from the goods. Traffic moves in three distinct bands here: cargo-crawlers grinding along the old service corridor behind the stalls, hovercabs and small aircraft threading the gap where the dome cracked open to the level above, and on foot, the endless curved river of buyers moving with the ring's own gravity. Neon signage is mercantile here, not seductive — hand-lettered prices glowing under scavenged sodium bulbs, cyan barcodes flickering over every stall for the SIN-readers vendors installed themselves.

The sound is commerce distilled: haggling in six tongues layered over the sub-bass thrum from below, drone-whine overhead, rain hissing on tarp-plastic — and under it, if you know to listen, the click of dozens of illegal scanners reading your chrome before you've said a word.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Auntie Fen** — the Ring Market's informal mayor, a Corran guild-clan matriarch who mediates stall disputes and sets the unofficial toll every vendor pays for gallery space.

**Doc Ilsa** — not a licensed ripperdoc but close enough for the Flats; runs a stall selling grey-tech medical patches and off-brand anti-rejection meds, Caduceus's cast-off formulas repackaged and cut.

**Two-Bit Yarrow** — a fence who deals exclusively in information, buying and selling rumors as readily as goods; every gang in the district owes him a favor or fears one.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Corran-mediated commercial order under Auntie Fen; a working truce, not a gang's turf.

Fen's toll buys real protection: a rotating shift of vendor-hired muscle who intervene fast against theft or violence, because a fight here costs everyone business. Escalation beyond a beating draws Fen herself, and she carries more authority than firepower — enough that most listen.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Dense, vendor-run AR — every stall broadcasts its own price-tags and quality claims directly into the overlay, a chaotic but surprisingly honest marketplace of light layered over the physical one.

A hacker digging past the surface finds Two-Bit Yarrow's real product: an information exchange running as a shadow-market beneath the visible one, encrypted rumor-threads bought and sold like any other good.

Sanctum occasionally seeds fraud-detection ghost-listings here to bait counterfeiters; locals have learned to spot the tells, mostly.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Toll Dispute. A new gang wants a cut of Fen's toll and is threatening to burn stalls until they get it. The crew can be hired as muscle by either side — or by Fen, to make the threat disappear quietly.

2. Yarrow's Price. Two-Bit Yarrow has information on a Ferrum demolition order targeting Glasshook — and he's selling it to the highest bidder, which might be the crew, might be someone planning to profit from the district's destruction.

3. Bad Meds. Doc Ilsa's latest batch of anti-rejection patches is cut with something Caduceus never approved, and people are dying slow. Tracing the supply chain leads uncomfortably close to a Caduceus black-budget experiment.

## Skylight Shrine

*Faith-camp under the broken dome*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Where the arcology's central dome cracked wide enough to let real rain through instead of just condensation, someone built a shrine to catch it. Tents and salvaged tarps ring a shattered skylight forty feet across, and beneath it, candles and lumen-strips burn in careful rows around a collection point where the chemical rain gathers into a shallow pool — clean enough, filtered enough, that people line up for it like it's a sacrament, because down here, it basically is.

There is still no true sky, even at the break — just the ragged underside of three collapsed levels stacked above, girders exposed like ribs, and through the gaps, distant running lights of aircraft and hovercraft threading levels far above that will never come down this far. The rain that falls through here is the same warm chemical drizzle as everywhere else, but it falls harder, unfiltered by the usual grates, and the shrine's caretakers have learned to read its rhythms like weather. Drones occasionally dip low to investigate the heat signature of the candle-rows before veering off, uninterested; ground traffic gives the shrine camp a respectful, unspoken berth, cargo-crawlers detouring around it out of habit more than reverence.

The sound here drops, for once, below the district's usual wall — a murmur of prayer and low singing, the drip and patter of rain into collection barrels, the distant creak of settling structure overhead — and under all of it, the ever-present sub-bass heartbeat of the pumps, which the faithful have started calling the tower's own pulse, still beating after a thousand years.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Brother Kesh** — a street-priest of the Lamp on Ninth tradition, Corran-rooted, who maintains the shrine's soup line and rainwater filtration with patched-together Caduceus-cast filtration units; asks nothing of anyone who kneels.

**Sera Vahn** — a former Sanctum actuary who lost her SIN in a corp restructuring and found a second purpose running the shrine's rain-collection logistics; still thinks in risk tables even while she prays.

**The Weeper** — a masked figure who appears at the shrine only during the heaviest rains, leaves medical supplies, and vanishes; rumored to be Caduceus-connected, never confirmed.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — faith-camp neutral ground, unaffiliated with any gang; protected by consensus rather than force.

No one in Glasshook profits from burning the shrine, and every gang leader has family or history that's passed through its soup line at some point — an unwritten rule keeps violence out. The few who've tested it found the entire district turn on them fast; Brother Kesh's network of favors runs deeper than his robes suggest.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The shrine registers as dead air on most commercial overlays — no ads, no SIN-reader logging, a deliberate scrubbed zone Sera Vahn maintains using old Sanctum access she was never supposed to keep.

Underneath, a hacker finds a genuinely selfless network: an anonymized medical-need board where the desperate post what they lack and the shrine's network quietly tries to fill it, funded by unmarked donations from sources across every faction.

The rainwater collection system doubles as a physical dead-drop — messages and small packages left sealed in the filtration barrels, retrieved on a rotating schedule only Kesh fully knows.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Weeper's Debt. The masked benefactor has stopped coming, and the medical supplies have stopped with them — right as a fever spreads through the shrine camp. Finding out why means finding out who The Weeper really works for.

2. Sera's Ledger. Sanctum wants its old actuarial access back — the access Sera never returned — and is willing to threaten the shrine's dead-air status to get it, which would expose the entire camp to corp surveillance overnight.

3. Holy Ground. A Ferrum demolition survey has marked the shrine's dome-break for "structural remediation" — corp language for clearing it. Brother Kesh needs the crew to find leverage before the paperwork becomes an eviction with guns behind it.

## The Escalators

*Vertical gang-turf ladder*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Six dead escalators climb the district's spine in a broken zigzag, each landing claimed by a different crew, and you can read Glasshook's entire power structure just by looking up the stack — territory markers spray-glowing brighter the higher you go, because higher ground has always meant something even after the building that made "higher" meaningful stopped moving anyone anywhere.

The sky, such as it was, ended six stories up in a slab of collapsed retail floor that now serves as someone's roof and someone else's ceiling — there is no seeing past it, only the warm chemical rain finding its way down through cracks between landings, staining each escalator's dead treads a different rust-black shade. Between the levels, in the open shaft where the escalators used to run, the vertical traffic tells its own story: a courier-drone won't cross above the third landing without paying a toll broadcast on an open channel, hovercabs avoid the shaft entirely, and below, ground-rigs idle at the base ramp because no wheelman trusts the footing past that point. It is, unusually for Glasshook, a piece of the district where the ground and the air both defer to whoever holds the steps.

Climbing it means climbing through six distinct sound-fields — a different crew's music, different crew's argument, different crew's silence — each landing's neon a different gang-color bleeding into the rain, until at the top the wall of noise resolves into whatever the highest crew is currently celebrating or mourning.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Grieve** — leader of the Chromebiters, who hold the top three landings; a former Ironclad washout who runs the stack like a military chain of command and enforces the toll system with brutal, almost sentimental consistency.

**Little Ax** — a preteen lookout for the Rustline crew on the second landing, too young to be as good at this as she is, feeding information to whoever pays best behind her crew's back.

**Torvo Meng** — a Nyx-connected supplier who climbs the escalators once a week to restock every crew simultaneously, the one figure allowed to move through all six landings unmolested.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — hard gang-partitioned vertical turf; each landing is its own micro-territory, contested at the borders.

Every landing enforces its own rules; crossing without paying toll or announcing yourself draws immediate, physical response from whichever crew holds that step. Real Alert only comes from Grieve's Chromebiters at the top, whose response is disciplined and disproportionate — they consider the whole shaft theirs by right of altitude.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Each landing broadcasts its own competing AR territory-marker, a visual turf war layered directly over the physical one — six overlapping claims of ownership flickering across the same six stories.

A hacker can read the informal toll-economy in the data: courier payment logs, protection fees, the entire vertical shakedown system laid bare in transaction pings between landings.

Torvo Meng's supply runs leave a data trail that, properly read, maps Nyx's entire Glasshook distribution network — a valuable and dangerous thing to know.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Toll War. Grieve wants to extend Chromebiter toll rules to every landing, and the lower crews are refusing. The crew can broker peace, break it further, or profit from either outcome.

2. Little Ax's Price. The young lookout has information a rival gang would kill for — literally — and wants out of Glasshook entirely. Extracting her means navigating six landings' worth of enemies who'd rather she stayed silent, permanently.

3. Torvo's Route. Nyx's supply chain through the Escalators has been compromised — someone's skimming shipments between landings. Nyx wants the leak found quietly before it triggers a turf war that disrupts every route through the district.

## Broadcast Booth

*Pirate media; ex-Meridian kit*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Tucked into what used to be a mall information kiosk, a wall of scavenged Meridian-spec broadcast gear hums behind a cracked glass window, antenna cable snaking up through a hole punched in three dead floors above to a rooftop dish nobody up-tower has noticed yet — or has noticed and doesn't care to stop. This is Glasshook's own voice, illegal and unlicensed, cutting into corp channels for exactly as long as it takes someone to triangulate the signal and exactly as long as the booth's operator can move the dish afterward.

No sky shows through that antenna hole — just a narrow shaft of darker dark, rain sheeting down it in a concentrated column that the booth's operator has rigged a gutter to catch and route away from the equipment. Around the kiosk, the district's usual layered traffic gives this spot unusual attention: Meridian-liveried drones occasionally pass slow and low, sensor arrays visibly sweeping, while the pirate booth's own tiny courier-drone darts out on broadcast nights to physically relay footage to bootleg receivers too far to reach by signal alone, weaving between hovercabs that have learned to give this airspace a wide, wary berth.

The booth itself sounds like static given purpose — feedback squeal, the clatter of patched-together relay hardware, a looping test tone under six different pirated channels arguing for bandwidth — and over it, distorted through cheap speakers, whoever's broadcasting tonight, voice pitched to carry over rain and rot and the sub-bass thrum that never quite goes away.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Static Rell** — the booth's founder and chief engineer, an ex-Meridian signal technician who "liberated" a rack of relay gear on her way out the door during a corp downsizing, and has hated Meridian with focused precision ever since.

**Cutter Nomi** — Rell's on-air voice, a former street-performer who turns pirate news into something closer to theater, half-real half-legend, and Glasshook loves her for it.

**The Mole** — an anonymous Meridian insider who feeds Rell scoops on corp movements in the Flats; identity unknown even to Rell, contact only through dead-drop code bursts.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — independent pirate operation, loosely protected by district goodwill rather than any gang's muscle.

Meridian occasionally sends quiet enforcement to shut the booth down — not open war, but signal-jamming, equipment sabotage, sometimes a "misplaced" drone strike on the dish. Rell's real defense is redundancy: three backup dishes hidden across Glasshook's rooftops, so killing one broadcast never kills the signal for long.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The booth's broadcasts hijack corp channels in short, precise bursts, visible in the Wired as a flickering "signal intrusion" ghost that Meridian's automated systems flag and hunt but rarely catch in time.

A hacker can trace the booth's relay architecture back to genuine ex-Meridian schematics — professional-grade gear hiding in a kiosk that looks like scrap, worth more to the right buyer than anyone in Glasshook realizes.

The Mole's dead-drop code bursts are buried in innocuous public data — ad-clicks, weather-pings — a pattern only Rell (and now possibly the crew) knows how to read.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Burn the Mole. Meridian has narrowed its internal leak hunt to a handful of suspects, and The Mole needs help vanishing before discovery — or needs the crew to help identify a decoy to take the fall instead.

2. The Dish War. Meridian has finally located all three backup dishes and plans a coordinated night raid to end the pirate signal for good. Rell needs muscle, misdirection, or a miracle — and pays well for whichever the crew can provide.

3. Scoop of a Lifetime. The Mole's latest tip suggests Meridian is quietly mapping Glasshook for something bigger than routine surveillance — a prelude to a corp move on the district itself. Broadcasting it could ignite the whole Flats in the crew's favor, or paint a target on the booth.

## Loading Dock

*Fence & smuggler receiving bay*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The old arcology's freight entrance still works the way it was built to — barely — a wide concrete apron ringed by loading bays where delivery trucks once backed in by the dozen. Now it's Glasshook's actual front door, the place where everything that isn't sold openly in the Ring Market first arrives in the district, off the books, in the dark, under a corrugated overhang that's more rust than roof.

The overhang is the closest thing to sky this place offers, and it fails at the job constantly — the warm chemical rain finds every gap, pooling in oil-black puddles across the apron that catch neon in broken, shifting mirrors. Here, more than almost anywhere else in the district, the vertical traffic layers stack in plain, functional view: cargo-crawlers queue at the bay doors nose to tail, a hovercab-turned-smuggling-runner idles just outside the fence line pretending to wait for a fare, and above, courier-drones and the occasional low-flying unmarked aircraft make careful, deliberate passes — everyone here is a professional, and professionals don't linger where they're seen.

The sound is industrial rather than commercial — hydraulic whine of bay doors, the clank of pallets, low urgent negotiation over engine-idle, rain hissing on corrugated metal — and threaded under it, the sub-bass foundation thrum that makes the whole apron feel like it's breathing along with every truck that rolls through.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Bosun Callas** — the Loading Dock's fence-in-chief, a Nyx-chartered smuggler-broker who decides what comes through and what turns back at the fence line; runs the bay like a small, orderly kingdom of contraband.

**Riko "Wheelbase" Dunn** — a rigger who knows every ground-route in and out of Glasshook that doesn't show up on any Ferrum survey map; hired by anyone who needs cargo moved quietly.

**Inspector Vane** — a Sanctum risk-assessor who shows up unannounced to "audit" the dock's insurance compliance, which everyone understands is code for checking whose payoffs have lapsed.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — Nyx-chartered smuggling operation; orderly by criminal necessity, not law.

Callas keeps the dock quiet because chaos brings corp attention nobody wants; his own enforcers handle theft and disputes fast and privately. Genuine Alert only spikes if Sanctum's audits turn hostile or a shipment goes catastrophically wrong — then Nyx sends real muscle from outside the district to contain it.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The dock's public manifest overlay is a work of quiet fiction — cargo listed as textiles and recycled parts that is, in the Wired's official record, entirely legitimate. The real inventory exists only in Callas's private ledger.

A hacker who breaches that ledger finds the district's actual supply chain laid bare — every gang's weapons, every clinic's grey-tech, every simsense shipment's true origin.

Inspector Vane's audit visits leave behind Sanctum surveillance ghost-pings that linger for days; smart smugglers time their runs around the gaps.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Audit. Inspector Vane's next visit isn't routine — Sanctum has flagged a genuine anomaly in the dock's insurance data, and someone needs it fixed, faked, or the Inspector permanently delayed.

2. Wheelbase's Route. Riko has found a ground-route bypassing a Ferrum checkpoint entirely — valuable to every smuggler in the district, and dangerous enough that whoever controls it controls a piece of Glasshook's whole economy.

3. The Shipment That Wasn't. A crate arrives at the dock with no manifest, no sender, and something inside that's very much alive. Callas wants it gone before it draws attention; several factions want to know what it is first.

## The Food Court

*Grey-food & dream-dust dealers*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The old food court's ring of counter-stalls still glows the way it did a thousand years ago — the same curved layout, the same overhead menu-boards, half of them still flickering ghost-prices for meals no one alive remembers eating. What's cooked here now is grey-market protein and reclaimed starches, and what's sold under the counter at half the stalls is dream-dust, cut and recut until its origin is anyone's guess and its effects are somebody else's problem.

Three collapsed floors sit stacked above the court like a cracked layer cake, the nearest one close enough that the warm chemical rain drips through in a fine, near-constant mist rather than proper rain, beading on every greasy counter and hissing where it hits the hot-plates. The court sits at a junction of two collapsed concourses, which means it catches an unusual amount of crossing traffic: courier-drones dropping off both food orders and dust deliveries indistinguishably, a cargo-crawler squeezed absurdly into a space built for foot traffic, hovercabs discharging customers who eat standing at the rail because sitting down here means committing to whatever the dust does to your evening.

The sound is appetite and static together — sizzle of grease, hawkers overlapping in six tongues, a dozen menu-boards' dead jingles looping into corrupted noise, rain hissing on hot metal, and under it, that same tower-heartbeat sub-bass that makes the whole court feel like it's simmering along with the food.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Chef Odalys** — runs the court's only genuinely reputable stall, real protein sourced through a Corran guild-clan supply chain; feeds anyone who's hungry regardless of ability to pay, and is quietly beloved for it.

**Dustman Kreel** — the court's dominant dream-dust cutter, an Obsidian-adjacent operator who treats his product like a science and his customers like data points.

**Fingers Aoi** — a pickpocket-turned-informant who works the rail crowd, selling gossip about who's buying what to whoever's paying, gangs and Edgerunners alike.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — mixed-use commercial ground, loosely split between Odalys's food-stall alliance and Kreel's dust operation; an uneasy shared-space truce.

Fights here are common and rarely fatal — the crowd is too dense and too indifferent for anything but a beating to happen before it's broken up by sheer foot traffic. Serious violence draws Kreel's own enforcers fast, since a body in the court is bad for everyone's business.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The dead menu-boards still broadcast ghost-ads for a defunct restaurant chain, a corrupted loop that's become part of the court's ambient noise floor in both light and sound.

Underneath, Kreel runs an order-and-delivery layer disguised as food-delivery traffic — dust orders routed and paid for exactly like a meal, invisible to anyone not looking for the pattern.

Fingers Aoi's information trade leaves a light data-footprint that a sharp hacker could use to reconstruct half the court's client list — valuable, and likely to get someone killed if it leaked.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Cut Too Far. Kreel's latest dust batch is causing seizures, and Chef Odalys — furious that it's scaring off her customers too — wants the crew to find out why and make it stop, one way or another.

2. Fingers's Ledger. Aoi has been selling information to too many sides at once, and someone's finally decided she's more valuable dead than talking. The crew can save her, silence her, or buy her ledger before either happens.

3. The Supply Squeeze. Odalys's Corran supply chain has been cut off by a Ferrum checkpoint shakedown, and without real food, the court tips entirely into Kreel's dust economy. The crew's choice of who to help decides what the Food Court becomes.

## Fountain Court

*Water-tap & neutral-ground truce*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The fountain hasn't run on its own power in a century, but it still holds water — rigged now with filtration hoses and collection barrels feeding off a cracked main, so that the dry marble basin where shoppers once tossed coins for luck has become the single most valuable piece of infrastructure in Glasshook. Every gang, every faction, every desperate soul in the district needs this water, and that single fact has made the Fountain Court the one place in the arcade where nobody starts a fight.

The dome above the court is more intact than most, a scarred but unbroken shell that gathers the warm chemical rain into channels someone engineered decades ago to feed the fountain's original plumbing — a rare piece of the old arcology still doing, however crudely, the job it was built for. Around the court's edge, the district's layered traffic slows out of unspoken respect: courier-drones circle wide rather than crossing the open basin airspace, hovercabs land at a designated distance rather than pushing close, ground-rigs queue politely at water-collection hours instead of jostling for position — a strange, fragile pocket of order in a district defined by its lack of one.

The sound here is almost gentle by Glasshook's standards — running water, the creak of hand-pumps, quiet transactional murmurs, rain on the intact dome overhead — a hush that makes the ever-present sub-bass heartbeat feel, for once, less like a threat and more like a held breath.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Old Marrin** — the fountain's self-appointed keeper, ancient and Corran-rooted, who maintains the filtration rig and enforces the neutral-ground truce through sheer accumulated moral authority.

**Tessa Blackwell** — a Cinderhold representative who negotiates water-rights on behalf of the district's exiled and SIN-less population, ensuring the truce holds even for those with no gang to protect them.

**The Broker** — an unaffiliated fixer who uses the fountain's neutral ground to arrange meetings between rival factions too hostile to meet anywhere else in Glasshook.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — universally respected neutral ground; no single faction controls it, and all factions enforce its peace collectively.

The truce is self-policing: anyone who draws a weapon here loses access to water for their entire crew, permanently, by unanimous district consensus. That threat has held for longer than anyone can remember, and it works better than any armed guard could.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The court runs an unusually clean, cooperative AR layer — a shared, faction-neutral scheduling system for water access that multiple gangs maintain jointly, a rare piece of the Wired here used exactly as intended.

A hacker probing deeper finds The Broker's meeting-arrangement threads, encrypted negotiations between factions that would never admit to speaking otherwise.

Old Marrin keeps a private log of every faction's water usage down to the liter — quiet leverage that has, more than once, prevented a fight before it started.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Poisoned Well. Someone has tampered with the filtration rig, and the water's starting to make people sick. Old Marrin needs the crew to find the culprit before the truce collapses in a district-wide panic.

2. The Broker's Meeting. Two warring factions have agreed to negotiate at the fountain, and someone wants that meeting to fail violently — a perfect setup for a hired disruption, or for the crew to play unlikely peacekeepers.

3. Rights and Runoff. Ferrum has quietly asserted ownership of the fountain's original plumbing infrastructure and is threatening to shut it down unless the district starts paying for "their" water — a slow-motion crisis that could unite Glasshook's gangs against a common enemy for the first time.

## Lost & Found

*Missing-persons broker; body trade*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

A hand-painted sign reading LOST & FOUND hangs over what used to be a mall service counter, and the joke, if it ever was one, curdled long ago. This is where Glasshook's missing go looking for each other, and where the district's body trade quietly does its business under the same roof — photographs pinned three-deep across every wall, hand-scrawled descriptions of the vanished, and behind the counter, a woman who has learned to tell, just by the way a client asks, whether they want their missing person found alive.

The ceiling here sags lower than anywhere else in the district, a single collapsed floor pressing close enough that you could touch it standing on a chair, and the warm chemical rain seeps through in slow dark stains across the drywall rather than proper drips — this room feels buried in a way the rest of Glasshook, for all its ruin, doesn't quite. Outside the doorway, the usual layered traffic passes with a kind of unconscious avoidance: courier-drones rarely stop here, ground-rigs don't idle at the curb, as if the whole district has agreed, without saying so, to keep Lost & Found at arm's length.

Inside, the sound wall drops to something closer to a hospital waiting room than a marketplace — hushed questions, the shuffle of photographs, a distant drip, and underneath, so faint you might mistake it for imagination, the same tower-heartbeat sub-bass that runs beneath everything else in the Flats, patient as ever.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Maren Cole** — Lost & Found's proprietor, ex-Caduceus records clerk who lost her license in a scandal she's never explained; brokers missing-persons cases and, discreetly, the sale of unclaimed bodies and their harvestable chrome to the district's ripperdocs.

**The Cataloguer** — Cole's silent assistant, a heavily augmented figure of uncertain gender who catalogs every photograph and every body with the same clinical patience, rumored to have once worked Caduceus's official morgues.

**Whit Sarrow** — a grief-hardened Cinderhold father who's been searching Lost & Found's boards for his daughter for two years, and has become an unofficial fixture — and unofficial guard — of the place.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — independent brokerage, unaffiliated with district gangs, protected by universal discomfort rather than force.

Almost no one bothers Lost & Found; there's an unspoken understanding that everyone might need it someday. The few who've tried to rob Cole found the entire district's grief turned against them fast — Whit Sarrow alone has driven off more than one attempt with nothing but sheer refusal to back down.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Cole maintains a private missing-persons database that vastly outperforms any corp SIN-registry for the Flats' population — the Wired's official record simply stops caring about people down here, and her handwritten photographs fill that gap.

A hacker who accesses her files finds not just the missing but a shadow ledger of body-trade transactions — who sold, who bought, what chrome was harvested from whom, an ethically radioactive but valuable data-cache.

The Cataloguer maintains a second, hidden layer: cross-references to Caduceus's own deniable "acquisition" programs, connecting missing Flats residents to legitimate corp research in ways no one up-tower would want confirmed.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Whit's Daughter. After two years, a photograph matching Whit Sarrow's daughter finally surfaces — in the Cataloguer's hidden body-trade ledger, not the missing-persons wall. Whit hires the crew to find out what really happened, whatever the cost.

2. Caduceus's Ledger. The Cataloguer's hidden cross-reference file, if it got out, would prove Caduceus has been sourcing "volunteer" test subjects from the Flats' missing-persons pool. Someone wants that file, and someone else wants it destroyed forever.

3. Cole's Confession. Maren Cole is dying and wants to make something right before she goes — she offers the crew her entire archive, decades of secrets, in exchange for one impossible last favor: finding the person whose disappearance she caused, back when she still worked for Caduceus.
