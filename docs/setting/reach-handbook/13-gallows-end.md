# Gallows End

*The Flats // Ossian Reach // The Edge of the Warrens*

## The Last Light

*Final lit plaza; boss of the edge*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The street simply stops believing in itself about a hundred meters ahead of you, the neon thinning block by block like a signal losing signal, and then there is the plaza — the last one, the one every map of the Flats draws with a small dotted warning around its edge. Above, there is no sky, only the underside of the Ninth Grid pressing down close and black, sweating its warm chemical rain onto a cracked concrete bowl ringed by the tallest, oldest signs left standing this far down: a triple-stacked Obsidian dream-den ad still running a loop nobody's paid for in a decade, a Cassavir relay-flag hung from a girder by someone with a sense of humor, a Nyx toll-marker burning steady cyan. The rain drags all three colors down into the puddles until the whole bowl looks like it's on fire underwater.

Traffic still finds this place, stubbornly, layer on layer — a courier-drone flock threading the black overhead in a tight defensive V, an Ironclad gunship idling far up on a routine sweep it never actually descends for, a string of cargo-crawlers grinding along the last real road grumbling under chains because the street surface gives out to gravel and bone-white rubble past here. Ground level is thick with rig-runners double-parked nose-out, ready to floor it back toward the lit Grid at the first wrong sound. Six languages of shouting, the sub-bass foundation-thrum, the drone-whine, the hiss of rain on hot iron — all of it a little quieter here than it should be, like the plaza itself is listening for something underneath the noise.

At the bowl's center, raised on a dais of stacked shipping plate, a floodlight burns steady and merciless — the actual Last Light, the fixture the whole plaza is named for, powered off a line nobody can trace and guarded by nobody who'll admit to guarding it. Past its throw, the dark starts. You can feel the edge of the district in your teeth before you ever see the barricades.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Mother Kessa** — self-appointed "boss of the edge," a former Ferrum dock-forewoman who lost her SIN in a foundation collapse and now runs the plaza's unofficial council of last-block holdouts. Trades safety and light for tithe and information; genuinely protects who she can.

**Old Renn** — keeps the floodlight running, claims descent from the original maintenance crew who wired it before the Warrens sealed off. Speaks to the light like it's a person. Knows every splice in the district's power grid.

**Vashti "Two-Toll" Orn** — a Nyx-aligned toll-runner who taxes anyone moving goods toward Barricade Row or the Drop, in exchange for a "clean word" that keeps them off gang watch-lists for a night.

**Deacon Ishii** — itinerant street-priest of the Lamp tradition who preaches nightly at the dais steps about what waits below; half the plaza thinks he's mad, the other half leaves him offerings anyway.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — contested/lawless, held loosely by Mother Kessa's council rather than any single gang or corp. The last patch of ground with functioning municipal power before the Warrens.

No formal law. Kessa's council enforces a single rule — nobody kills under the Light — with numbers, not guns; break it and every hand in the plaza turns against you before Ironclad or anyone else could respond. Real violence draws scavenger gangs from Barricade Row within minutes, drawn by the noise like sharks to blood in dark water.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired flickers and stutters this close to the edge — official Grid maps mark the plaza "unverified," and SIN-readers here are old, patched, and easy to spoof. It's the last place upstream from the Warrens where the overlay still mostly works.

A hacker who digs finds a graveyard of dead account-pings — SINs that checked in here once and never again, their last known location frozen forever at the plaza's edge.

AR tags left by Kessa's council mark safe stalls, known toll-runners, and — in a private layer only she controls — a live tally of who currently owes her.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Last Toll. Mother Kessa wants the crew to shadow a caravan of Warrens-salvage traders through the plaza to Salvage Gate without Vashti's toll-runners bleeding them dry — but the real threat is a Barricade Row warband testing whether the Light's truce still holds.

2. Dead Reckoning. Old Renn's floodlight is failing, and with it the last verified ground-truth marker the Grid recognizes this deep. Losing it would let the Warrens' dead-air creep swallow the plaza within weeks — someone upstream (possibly Meridian, possibly worse) wants exactly that.

3. The Council's Debt. Kessa's tally of favors has grown too large for her to control, and a Nyx broker is quietly buying up the debts of half the plaza. The crew is hired — by Kessa, or by Nyx — to find out who really owns the Last Light now.

## Barricade Row

*Fortified gang line vs. the Warrens*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

You smell the Row before you see it — scorched plastic, gun-oil, and the wet-mineral reek that bleeds up out of the dark past the wire. It used to be three parallel streets; now it's one fortified wall of stacked containers, sandbagged rubble, and salvaged plate welded into a crooked rampart that runs the width of the district, broken only where the old roads punch through in chokepoints nobody crosses without being seen first.

There's no sky here, same as anywhere this low — just the black underbelly of the Grid above, dripping its warm chemical rain onto razor coils and gun positions, the neon reduced to hazard-strobes and targeting-lume in sullen red and amber instead of the seductive color up-Flats. But the air is still busy: a pair of Ironclad-surplus drones on loan to the line-bosses patrol in slow figure-eights above the wall, a hovercab won't come within two blocks of the checkpoint no matter what you pay, and every so often a heavy cargo-rig grinds up from the plaza behind you loaded with something the crews on the wall want badly enough to open a gate for. The wall of sound here is different too — less voices, more machinery: generator-thrum, the clack of somebody racking a weapon just to remind you they can, and under everything, that same thousand-year heartbeat of the foundation pumps, steady as a held breath.

Past the wire, past the checkpoint lights, the street doesn't so much end as stop existing — the pavement gives way to broken plate and then to nothing your eyes can resolve. Every gun on the Row points that way. That should tell you everything.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Corvo Ashwell** — warlord of the Ferronail Crew, the largest gang holding the central barricade; ex-Ironclad contractor who "retired" with a stolen combat-frame and a grudge. Runs the Row like a siege, because he believes it is one.

**Sera "Latch" Nkemdi** — Corvo's quartermaster and the only person on the Row who talks to outsiders civilly; brokers passage deals and mercenary contracts through the checkpoints.

**The Widow's Boys** — a smaller rival crew holding the eastern chokepoint, rumored to trade directly with things that come up from the Warrens rather than just guard against them.

**Tomas Greel** — Meridian stringer who's paid the Ferronail Crew for exclusive footage rights to "whatever comes through," and is visibly disappointed most nights.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — gang-fortified, effectively a private front line. The Ferronail Crew and Widow's Boys hold their sections as sovereign territory; no corp will contest it because no corp wants the job.

Security here is total and immediate: armed checkpoints, sightlines covering every approach, and standing orders to shoot anything that comes over the wall from the wrong side without waiting for ID. Alert response is instant and lethal — the Row doesn't do warnings, because warnings are how people used to die before they learned better.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The gangs run their own jury-rigged sensor mesh layered over what's left of the Grid's overlay — motion-tags, heat-ghosts, and a private comm channel that lights up red the instant something crosses the wire from the dark side.

Officially the Wired shows Barricade Row as a "structural hazard zone, access restricted" — corp language for we stopped mapping this and so should you.

A skilled hacker can tap the gangs' sensor mesh and get earlier warning of Warrens activity than the defenders themselves — valuable, and a very good way to get shot by someone who assumes you're one of the things they're watching for.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Relief Line. The Ferronail Crew is bleeding numbers to something that's started probing the wall at irregular hours, and Corvo needs deniable guns willing to hold a chokepoint for one bad night while he figures out what it is.

2. The Widow's Deal. Evidence surfaces that the Widow's Boys are trading captured Edgerunners — or worse, live SINs — to something below the wire in exchange for safe passage. Someone wants proof, and someone else wants it buried.

3. Breach of Contract. Sanctum's actuaries have quietly classified the entire Row as an "uninsurable total loss zone," which means the conglomerates have stopped caring who wins here — opening the door for a third faction, human or otherwise, to make a play for the wall itself.

## The Drop

*Sealed lift-shaft down to the Warrens*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The shaft-house looks like a tomb because that's more or less what it became. It was a freight lift once — one of the old bones-of-the-tower elevators that used to run supplies down into levels the Reach has since disowned — and someone, a long time ago, welded its doors shut with plate three layers thick and painted a warning across it in six languages and one that isn't a language at all, just a symbol every child in the Flats learns to fear before they learn to read.

Overhead there's the same ceiling every street down here has to live under — no sky, just the dark underbelly of the level above, chemical rain sheeting off the shaft-house's corrugated roof in curtains lit orange by a single flickering sodium lamp. But the air over the Drop is wrong in a way you feel before you can name it: courier-drones give the shaft-house a wide, deliberate berth, arcing around it rather than over it; the one hover-rig idling nearby has its running lights pointed away, like even light doesn't want to look down that hole. Ground traffic avoids the block entirely except for the vigil-keepers' foot patrols and the occasional Nyx supply crawler making its last delivery before turning back. The sound-wall that blankets the rest of the district — the voices, the drone-whine, the hiss of rain on hot metal — goes strangely thin here, swallowed by something under the plate that never quite goes silent and never quite resolves into a sound you can name.

Stand close enough, in the wet and the cold sodium light, and you'll swear you can feel a draft coming up through the seams in the door. Warm, when the air on your face is cold everywhere else. Nobody has ever explained that, and everybody who works this block has stopped asking.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Warden Iyoba Cresh** — self-titled "Warden of the Drop," a burned-SIN former Sanctum risk-assessor who took it upon herself to log everything that happens at the shaft. Keeps meticulous, slightly unhinged records. Will trade information for almost anything.

**The Vigil Three** — a rotating trio of Lamp-tradition street-priests who keep a permanent watch on the shaft-house door, never fewer than one at a time, praying and logging disturbances in a shared ledger.

**Renny Oskol** — a Nyx-connected fixer who occasionally arranges "sealed cargo" drops through a maintenance grate beside the main doors — the only sanctioned traffic still moving through the Drop in either direction.

**The Knocker** — not confirmed to be a person; the name given to whatever periodically strikes the sealed doors from the inside, three times, always at the same measured interval, always stopping before a fourth.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — nominally unclaimed, functionally held by the vigil-keepers' quiet authority and general terror. No gang wants the block; even the toughest crews on Barricade Row treat the Drop as neutral, cursed ground.

There's no armed security in the conventional sense — the deterrent is dread, reputation, and the Vigil Three's ledger, which every faction in the district respects because nobody wants their name in it next to something that came out. If the seal is ever breached, the Alert cascades district-wide within minutes: every barricade crew, every Nyx waystation, every vigil shrine will know before the corps do.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Wired goes almost dead over the shaft — a true dead-air pocket, one of the few places in the Reach where the overlay simply refuses to render, showing only a grey error-glyph where the map should be.

Hackers who push into that dead zone report fragments of old data — pre-collapse maintenance logs, a lift schedule for a floor that officially doesn't exist, and, more than one has claimed, something that pings back.

Cresh maintains her own private overlay-log of every disturbance, timestamped and geotagged, that she'll share for the right price — the closest thing the district has to hard data on what the Drop actually is.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Three Knocks. The Knocker's pattern has changed for the first time in years — a fourth strike, faint, was heard last night. Warden Cresh wants deniable contractors to investigate before the vigil-keepers' panic spreads to the whole district.

2. The Sealed Cargo. Renny Oskol's last sanctioned drop through the maintenance grate didn't come back up on schedule, and Nyx wants to know if it's a Warrens problem or if Renny's skimming — either answer is a job.

3. Breaking the Seal. A Ferrum survey team, escorted by Ironclad muscle, arrives with orders to reopen the shaft for "resource assessment." The vigil-keepers see it as unforgivable heresy; the crew is caught between a corp contract and the fury of everyone who's kept watch here for a generation.

## Salvage Gate

*Warrens-salvage buy point*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The gate isn't a gate anymore so much as a marketplace that grew around one — a old maintenance checkpoint whose actual doors were scrapped decades back for the very trade that now fills the block: things carried up out of the dark and sold to whoever's buying. Tarps strung between girders keep the worst of the rain off stalls piled with pre-Collapse alloy, dead tech nobody can identify, bone-white fragments that might be structural composite or might be something else entirely, and hand tools worn smooth by hands that don't work here anymore.

There is no sky above the Gate, only the low black ceiling of the level overhead, sweating its warm chemical rain through gaps in the tarps onto buyers haggling under strings of scavenged lumen-wire that throw a sick, uneven glow — cyan where the wire's still good, dead brown where it's not. The vertical space over the block is thinner than uptown but still crowded in its own cramped way: a single tired courier-drone weaves between support struts on autopilot, a scrap-hauler rig backs in through the crowd with its horn blaring continuously, and everyone on foot has long since learned to move without looking up, because looking up here just means rain in your eyes. Voices haggle in six languages over the price of a rusted control panel; somewhere a generator coughs and catches; the sub-bass foundation-thrum underlies it all, patient as ever.

Every buyer here asks the same unspoken question of every seller: how far down did you go for this, and what did you leave down there. Nobody answers honestly. Nobody expects them to.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Auntie Ferro** — matriarch of the Gate's trading families, a Corran guild-clan elder who set the first rules of "clean salvage" decades ago and still enforces them with a cane and a reputation. Buys, sells, and settles disputes.

**Digger Voss** — a scarred, taciturn salvager who goes deeper than anyone else still walking and always comes back with the strangest hauls; trusted for his goods, feared for what he might have picked up down there besides scrap.

**Ilse Marrow** — Caduceus-trained field medic who works the Gate unofficially, patching up salvagers who come back wrong before Auntie Ferro's rules force them to be quietly turned away.

**Baxa Ohn** — a Nyx buyer with standing orders to acquire anything electronic or "informational" that comes up, no questions, top price, immediate pickup.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — an independent trade-zone under Auntie Ferro's guild-clan authority, tolerated by the surrounding gangs because everyone profits from the Gate staying open and neutral.

Ferro's rule is simple and absolute: no violence in the market, no unvetted salvage sold without inspection, no questions about where anyone's cut came from. Breaking it means permanent exile from the only marketplace in the district that will still buy Warrens goods. Real threats — contaminated salvage, something that "came up wrong" — trigger an immediate, practiced quarantine drill the traders have run more than once.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The Gate's overlay is a patchwork of private trade-tags — item listings, price histories, seller reputations — laid over a Grid map that officially still shows the old maintenance checkpoint and nothing else.

A hacker who digs finds an unofficial ledger of "flagged" salvage — items sold through the Gate that later did something they shouldn't have — kept quietly by Ferro's own people as insurance against her own market.

Nyx runs a discreet standing data-tap here, buying not just objects but the metadata of where they were found, building a slow, patient map of the Warrens no corp survey team could ever legally produce.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Digger's Last Haul. Digger Voss has gone quiet after his latest run and Auntie Ferro wants someone to check on him before Baxa Ohn's buyers realize what he might be carrying — and try to take it themselves.

2. Contaminated Lot. A batch of "clean" salvage turns out to be actively hazardous — data-active, possibly Wired-linked, possibly something the Warrens didn't want brought up. Ferro needs it traced back through every hand it's passed through before it causes a district-wide incident.

3. The Buyer's Ledger. Someone has stolen Ferro's private flagged-salvage ledger. In the wrong hands it's blackmail material on half the traders at the Gate — and proof of exactly what's been quietly surfacing from the Warrens for years.

## Vigil Shrine

*Faith-watch over the dark; the Lamp kin*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The shrine occupies what used to be a transit substation, its old control room gutted and refitted with pew-benches made from packing crates and a single, patient lamp burning in the window that faces the dark. It's a daughter-house of the Lamp on Ninth, the street-faith tradition that keeps its soup lines and its washing rites and its vigils no matter how far down the Flats go — and here, at the literal edge of everything, that vigil means something more specific: they watch the chasm, in shifts, every hour of every day, so that no one ever has to look into it alone.

There's no sky above the shrine, only the close black underside of the level above, the rain coming down warm and faintly chemical the way it always does, streaking the shrine's single lit window and pooling in the gutters outside where a line of the hungry and the sleepless waits for the soup kettle no matter the hour. The vertical space above the block still carries its share of traffic — a Meridian relay-drone passes overhead on its fixed patrol route, indifferent; somewhere out past the barricades a hovercraft's running lights blink low and slow, probably a smuggler's rig giving the shrine's watch-lamp a wide, respectful berth. The sound here softens, almost reverently — voices low, the sub-bass foundation-thrum still present but muted, like the building itself is holding its breath along with everyone inside it.

Through the window, past the lamp, you can just make out where the street gives way to the dark. Someone is always there, watching it. Right now, it's your turn to wonder why.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Brother Amadi Vell** — the shrine's keeper, a former dock-worker who lost his crew to a Warrens breach years ago and has kept the vigil ever since; gentle, exhausted, unshakeable in his faith that watching matters.

**Sister Priya Nakash** — runs the soup line and the shrine's small clinic, trained informally by Caduceus field medics; the practical half of the shrine's work, and the one who actually keeps people alive.

**Old Mattis** — a blind vigil-keeper who claims he can hear things in the dark the sighted watchers miss, and has been right often enough that nobody argues anymore.

**Deacon Ishii** — the wandering priest also seen at the Last Light, considers the Vigil Shrine his true home parish and returns here between his plaza sermons.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — a recognized sanctuary under street-faith custom; even the hardest gangs on Barricade Row observe the old rule that shrines are neutral ground, a courtesy extended more out of superstition than piety.

The shrine keeps no weapons and wants none. Its only defense is the custom of sanctuary and the fact that every faction in the district, at some point, needs the soup line, the clinic, or the vigil's honest word about what's moving in the dark. Violating the sanctuary is a fast way to lose every ally you have left down here.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The shrine keeps almost no digital footprint by choice — the Lamp tradition distrusts the Wired on principle — so the overlay here shows little beyond a generic "community shelter" tag, deliberately unremarkable.

Off the books, Brother Vell keeps a hand-written ledger of every disturbance the vigil-keepers have logged at the chasm's edge over the years — a physical record the Wired has never touched and can't erase.

Wereraven scouts are known to rest here between runs, trading news of the deep Warrens for a hot meal — making the shrine an unlikely but reliable source of current intelligence on what's actually moving below.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Old Mattis Hears Something. The blind watcher reports a new sound from the dark — rhythmic, patterned, unmistakably deliberate — and Brother Vell wants it investigated quietly before panic spreads down the soup line.

2. The Ledger's Worth. A Meridian researcher offers Vell serious money for access to the vigil's decades of handwritten disturbance records. Selling it would fund the shrine for years; it would also hand a conglomerate the district's only real historical data on the Warrens' edge.

3. Sanctuary Broken. A wounded fugitive — Warrens salvager, burned-SIN, or worse — claims sanctuary at the shrine while a Barricade Row warband hunts them through the streets outside. The crew must decide whether the old rule still holds, and what it costs to enforce it.

## The Deadline

*Where SINs go to vanish; broker*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The building has no sign, which is the point — everyone who needs to find it already knows its name, and everyone else has no business here. It sits low and windowless behind a curtain of hanging chain-plastic that clatters in the wind off the chasm, the entrance marked only by a hand-painted line on the pavement that everyone calls the Deadline itself: cross it, and whoever you were on the other side stops mattering.

Above, there's the same close black nothing every street this deep has instead of a sky, rain sheeting warm and chemical off the building's corrugated overhang, catching a single dim red bulb above the door and turning the puddles the color of old rust. The air over the block carries a thin, careful trickle of traffic — a courier-drone drops something through a chute in the roof and leaves without circling back, a hover-rig sits dark and idling two streets off, engine running, driver watching the door rather than the road. Ground level stays deliberately quiet; nobody loiters near the Deadline who doesn't have to. The sound-wall thins here the way it does near the Drop — voices dropping to murmurs, the sub-bass foundation-thrum the loudest thing left, like the whole block has agreed to stop making noise about what happens inside.

Somewhere behind that chain-plastic curtain, someone is deciding whether your SIN — the six-digit thread of everything you ever were on the Grid's books — is about to stop existing. For some people, down here, that's the whole point of coming.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**The Clerk** — the broker who runs the Deadline, identity unknown even to regulars, always masked, always calm; erases SINs, forges new identities, and — for the right price — makes people disappear entirely, sometimes permanently, sometimes just administratively.

**Foss Ardan** — the Clerk's enforcer, a hulking ex-Ironclad washout who searches everyone at the curtain and has never once been seen to speak.

**Yuen Ratha** — a Meridian data-ghost who trades the Clerk clean SIN-templates skimmed from real deaths up in the Grid, in exchange for a cut of every erasure.

**Dez** — a nervous, half-erased regular who haunts the block outside, technically SIN-less but not quite gone, a walking cautionary tale for anyone considering the same transaction.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — a fiercely independent operation the Clerk defends personally; not aligned with any gang, tolerated by all of them because everyone eventually needs an eraser.

Foss Ardan is the only visible security, but the Clerk's real protection is leverage — everyone who's used the Deadline has something to hide, which means everyone has a reason to keep its existence quiet and its operator alive. Trouble here draws a slow, patient kind of retaliation rather than a loud one.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The building doesn't exist on any official Grid map — a genuine blank tile, deliberately scrubbed, which is itself the most reliable way to find it if you know what you're looking for.

The Clerk's actual erasure-work happens on an isolated, air-gapped rig inside — the one piece of infrastructure in the district that never touches the open Wired, making it nearly unhackable from outside and nearly untraceable once a job is done.

Dead SIN-threads that pass through here occasionally resurface months later, reused, on someone else entirely — a quiet trade the corps would very much like to shut down if they could ever prove it existed.

#### Plot Hooks

1. A Ghost's Favor. The Clerk needs a SIN erased fast and clean for someone too important to be seen anywhere near Gallows End — and wants deniable contractors to escort them in without anyone, including the crew, learning who it is.

2. Yuen's Supply Problem. The templates Yuen Ratha has been feeding the Clerk are running dry, and Yuen wants the crew to help "acquire" fresh ones from a source that's very much still alive.

3. The Recycled Name. A SIN erased at the Deadline years ago has just resurfaced attached to someone with a very public, very dangerous profile — and the Clerk wants it quietly traced back before Sanctum's actuaries notice the anomaly and start asking who's really behind the mask.

## Quiet Fringe

*Dead-air creep; avoid after dark*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The block feels wrong before you can say why. The neon is still there, technically — half-lit signs stuttering over shuttered storefronts, an old lumen-strip flickering its last colors into the wet — but something about the way sound behaves here sets your teeth on edge, a faint, wrong hush where the district's usual roar should be. This is the Quiet Fringe, and everyone who works Gallows End will tell you the same thing without being asked twice: don't be here after the lights change color.

No sky, of course — just the flat black underside of the level above, rain dragging down thin trails of dead neon until the street looks like it's bleeding out slowly. What passes for air-traffic up here has thinned to almost nothing: a courier-drone that strays too close to the block visibly corrects course and climbs, like something in its routing algorithm has learned to be afraid; a single stubborn ground-rig crosses the block at speed, headlights cutting twin tunnels through the rain, and does not stop for anything. The wall of sound every other block in the district takes for granted — voices, drone-whine, the hiss of rain on hot metal, the deep pump-heartbeat below — is all present here too, but thinned, delayed, wrong, like you're hearing it a half-second after it happened rather than as it happens.

Your comm implant hiccups once, quietly, as you cross into the block — a data packet arriving a fraction of a second late, or not quite arriving at all. Whatever's causing the dead-air creep here, it's spreading. Everyone agrees on that much. Nobody agrees on what it means.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Ondrej Fask** — a Meridian signal-tech obsessed with mapping the Fringe's expanding dead-air pocket, convinced it's an early warning of something structural, or worse, deliberate.

**The Watcher-Kids** — a loose crew of street children who dare each other to run through the Fringe after dark and always come back with a slightly different story about what they saw; harmless, or an early sign, depending who you ask.

**Iolanthe Grey** — a technomancer squatting in one of the block's dead buildings, drawn here by something in the Wired's silence that the rest of the district can't perceive at all; half oracle, half hazard.

**Sergeant Okafor** — leads an Ironclad "signal integrity" patrol that sweeps the Fringe on an irregular schedule, officially to protect Meridian relay infrastructure, unofficially unnerved by every pass.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — unclaimed by any gang; nobody wants ground that swallows signal, but Ironclad maintains a nominal, reluctant patrol presence tied to nearby relay assets.

There's no real security here in the human sense — the danger isn't armed. Alert response, when it happens, is technical and confused: comms drop, drone escorts lose lock, and reinforcements sent to the Fringe often arrive later than their own dispatch timestamp says they should.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

This is dead-air creep made visible — a slowly, measurably expanding pocket where the Wired's overlay lags, glitches, or fails outright, spreading a few meters further each month by Ondrej Fask's careful measurements.

Hackers who probe the edge of the pocket report the same disorienting symptom: their own tools respond a half-second slow, as if the Fringe itself has a kind of localized latency to reality.

Iolanthe Grey claims the silence isn't empty — that something in the Wired's underlying structure here has gone quiet on purpose, not broken, and that the difference matters enormously.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Fask's Measurements. Ondrej Fask needs deniable contractors to plant sensor relays deeper into the Fringe than he's willing to go himself, to prove — or disprove — that the dead-air pocket is accelerating.

2. The Watcher-Kids' Dare. One of the street children who ran into the Fringe after dark hasn't come back, and the others swear they saw someone waiting for her in the static. Her family wants her found before the search becomes a search for a body.

3. Iolanthe's Warning. The technomancer surfaces with a fragmentary, urgent message pulled from the Wired's silence — something about the Drop, or the Warrens, or both — and insists it has to reach the vigil-keepers or the Nyx Waystation before the next tide of dead-air creep arrives.

## Rope Market

*Climbing & descent gear; Warrens kit*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

Before you see the stalls you see the gear hanging off every available surface — coiled rope in a dozen grades, climbing harnesses salvaged and re-webbed a hundred times over, breather masks with their filters proudly displayed like trophies, lumen-flares bundled in dozens, grapples and winch-rigs and hand-crank descenders racked along improvised pegboards made from scavenged girder-mesh. This is the Rope Market, the last place in the district — in the Flats — where you can outfit yourself properly for a trip down, and everyone working here knows exactly what that means for the person buying.

The sky, as ever this low, doesn't exist — just the close dark underside of the level above, chemical rain sheeting down onto tarps stretched between stalls, catching neon-price-tags in cyan and gold that swim and blur in the wet. Traffic here skews low and heavy: a cargo-crawler backs in to make a bulk delivery of rope-stock, its horn blaring; a scrap-hauler idles nearby loaded with recovered descent gear bought back cheap off Warrens salvagers too spooked to go down again; overhead, a courier-drone or two threads between support cables strung for exactly that purpose, careful not to catch a line. Voices haggle hard and fast over rope-grade and rot-resistance, over which breather filters actually work below the Drop's dead-air line, over whose gear failed on whom and why — a market conversation that is, underneath the haggling, entirely about survival.

The vendor nearest you holds up two coils of rope, one twice the price of the other, and asks without much ceremony: how far down are you actually planning to go? It's not a sales question. It's the only question that matters here.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Big Halsey** — the market's most respected vendor, a former Warrens salvager himself who quit the deep runs after losing partners to bad gear and now sells only rope and rigging he'd trust with his own life.

**Petra Voss** (no relation to Digger) — runs the market's breather-mask stall, sourcing filters from a Caduceus supply contact and modifying them to handle the Warrens' worse air; expensive, but reliable.

**Nix** — a teenage runner who tests returned/salvaged gear before resale by using it herself on short drops, functionally the market's unofficial quality-control department and far too reckless about it.

**Corran Aldis** — a guild-clan negotiator who brokers bulk gear contracts for salvage crews and Barricade Row alike, keeping the market's prices from spiraling during gear shortages.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — an independent trade block, loosely protected by the vendors' mutual interest in staying open; too useful to every faction in the district to be worth attacking.

No dedicated muscle, but theft here is rare and punished informally and thoroughly — steal rope that might save someone's life and the whole market turns on you fast. Real trouble draws a crowd of armed customers before it draws any organized security.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

A brisk, practical trade-tag layer covers the market — gear reviews, failure reports, and a crowd-sourced (if grim) rating system for which rope grades have actually held up on real Warrens descents.

A hacker digging deeper finds Nix's informal testing logs — an unofficial but startlingly accurate record of which gear survives the Drop's dead-air zone and which doesn't, information worth real money to serious salvage operations.

Big Halsey keeps no digital records at all by preference, trusting only his own memory and reputation — making him a rare, entirely un-hackable source of expertise in the district.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Bad Rope. A batch of counterfeit "premium" descent gear has entered the market, indistinguishable from Halsey's real stock until it fails under load. Someone's dying because of it, and Halsey wants the source found before it's his reputation, too.

2. Nix's Test Run. Nix has gone missing after a solo test-drop near the Drop's dead-air boundary, and the market's vendors — unofficially, off the books — want her found before her family or the vigil-keepers turn it into a bigger incident.

3. The Bulk Contract. Corran Aldis needs deniable escorts for a shipment of rope-stock coming up from a Cinderhold supply barge — cargo that a Barricade Row gang has decided it would rather take by force than pay for.

## Bone Bridge

*Span over the chasm to a dead block*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The bridge earns its name honestly — a long, narrow span of pale structural composite, salvaged and re-poured so many times over the centuries that it's gone the bleached white-grey of old bone, arching out over the chasm to a dead block on the far side that the district map simply marks as unclaimed. Cables strung with warning-flags and old prayer-ribbons run its length, snapping wet in a wind that comes up from below rather than from anywhere you'd expect wind to come from.

Above, no sky — only the black underside of the level overhead, rain coming down warm and chemical, sheeting off the bridge's low guardrails into the dark on either side, which is somehow worse than rain falling anywhere else in the district, because here you can watch it fall and never see it land. The vertical space over the bridge is thin but not empty: a Meridian relay-drone crosses at altitude, running lights blinking a steady rhythm; a hover-rig crew, paid well and nervous about it, ferries salvage across at low altitude rather than trust the span's old composite; on the bridge itself, foot traffic moves in a tight, purposeful line, nobody lingering, nobody looking down for longer than they have to. The sound here strips down to almost nothing but wind and rain and the groan of old structural composite settling under weight it was never rated for — the district's usual roar fading behind you with every step you take out over the gap.

Halfway across, if you stop — and something about the place makes you want to stop, just for a second — you'll notice the far side of the bridge is dark. Not unlit-dark. Dark like the lights over there stopped working for a reason nobody's fixed.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Toll-Keeper Bram** — collects an informal crossing toll at the bridge's near end, ostensibly to fund the bridge's upkeep, actually because he's the only one with the nerve to stay posted there full-time.

**The Far-Siders** — a small, insular community of squatters and burned-SINs who've made a life on the dead block across the bridge, distrustful of outsiders, protective of whatever they've built over there.

**Yusra Okonkwo-Vale** — a Ferrum structural engineer sent, against her own better judgment, to assess whether the bridge is salvageable or should be demolished before it fails and takes crossers with it.

**Wereraven scout Kiel** — uses the bridge as a regular crossing point on runs between the district's shrine and points further into the unclaimed dark; one of the few who crosses without paying Bram's toll, by mutual unspoken agreement.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — nominally unclaimed on both ends; Bram's toll is tolerated custom rather than enforced law, and the Far-Siders defend their block fiercely but only if actually threatened.

The bridge itself is the danger — structural failure is a constant, quietly acknowledged risk, and nobody in authority has inspected it properly in years. Social friction is low; physical risk is high. The real "security" is the Far-Siders' watchfulness over who actually makes it across.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

The bridge registers on official Grid maps as condemned and closed, a designation everyone ignores because the alternative is a much longer, much more dangerous route around the chasm.

The dead block on the far side is an actual overlay blank — no SIN-readers, no AR tags, nothing, which is either why the Far-Siders chose it or a result of choosing it, nobody's sure which came first.

A hacker checking the bridge's old structural sensors — if any still function — could get real warning of an imminent collapse; so far, nobody's bothered, which itself worries the few engineers who know the sensors exist.

#### Plot Hooks

1. Structural Assessment. Yusra Okonkwo-Vale needs escorts to complete her survey of the bridge without the Far-Siders — who correctly suspect Ferrum wants an excuse to demolish their only way home — running her off first.

2. The Toll Dispute. A Barricade Row gang has decided Bram's informal toll should belong to them, and the Far-Siders have made it clear that if the bridge falls under gang control, they'll cut the near-side cables themselves rather than allow it.

3. What Kiel Saw. The wereraven scout crossed the bridge on a routine run and came back changed — quiet, evasive, unwilling to say what she saw on the dead block beyond its usual dark. Someone needs to know if the Far-Siders are still themselves.

## Nyx Waystation

*Last supply post before the deep*

### Player Description

*(read aloud as the crew arrives)*

The Waystation used to be a freight depot, and Nyx never bothered changing much beyond the signage — a squat, reinforced block of a building with loading bays still scarred from decades of cargo traffic, now the literal last stop for supply, information, and deniability before the district runs out of ground entirely. Its cyan sigil, the cleanest, best-maintained light on this entire stretch of the Flats, burns steady over the main bay door like a promise nothing else down here can make.

There is no sky above the Waystation — just the low dark underside of the level overhead, chemical rain sheeting off the depot's reinforced roof in thick warm sheets, pooling silver-black in the loading yard where cargo-rigs idle in a neat, disciplined row that looks almost wrong this deep in the district, where nothing else runs on schedule. Overhead, the air stays busy in Nyx's usual efficient way: a tight formation of courier-drones lifts off on a fixed rotation, a hovercraft with cargo netting slung beneath it eases down onto the yard's landing cradle with practiced precision, and ground-rigs queue for loading with none of the chaos you'd expect from any other block this close to the edge. The sound here has its own particular texture — less the district's ragged wall of noise and more a controlled hum, forklifts and loading cranes and quiet, professional voices running manifest checks, the sub-bass foundation-thrum underneath it all like it's just another piece of machinery Nyx has learned to schedule around.

A quartermaster in a rain-slicked Nyx jacket clocks you before you've finished crossing the yard, already running your face against a manifest you didn't know you were on. Whatever you're here for — gear, information, a way down, a way to disappear — the Waystation has almost certainly handled it before, for someone else, last week.

### Director's Section

#### Key NPCs

**Quartermaster Devra Solis** — runs day-to-day operations at the Waystation with brisk, unsentimental efficiency; answers to Nyx's regional cartel structure and treats every transaction, human or cargo, with the same professional detachment.

**"Ledger" Okonkwo** — the Waystation's information broker, trading intelligence on Warrens conditions, salvage routes, and gang movements to anyone Nyx has cleared to buy it.

**Big Tarn** — chief of the loading crew, an ex-dock worker built like a cargo-crawler himself, who personally vets every crew heading down through the Waystation's supply channel and has a good instinct for who's about to get themselves killed.

**The Courier Cell** — a tight rotation of Nyx-run drone and hover-pilots who make the actual final supply drops into Warrens contacts, seen by almost nobody, spoken of by everyone.

#### Security

Zone: Flats — solidly Nyx-controlled, the most orderly and well-defended point in the entire district by a wide margin, because Nyx cannot afford to lose its last supply artery into the deep.

Security is professional, not showy — quiet cameras, disciplined loading-crew muscle, and a standing arrangement with Barricade Row's gangs that keeps the Waystation off-limits to raiding in exchange for preferential trade terms. Real threats trigger a fast, coordinated Nyx response rather than a chaotic one; this is corporate-grade organized crime, not street chaos.

Alert here means Nyx's regional enforcers arrive, not just local muscle — a tier of consequence most of the district can't match.

#### The Overlay (What the Wired Shows Here)

Nyx runs its own clean, private overlay-cell here, similar in spirit to Cassavir's at the Switchboard — public AR shows only a generic freight-depot tag, while the real manifest data, cargo-tracking, and personnel logs stay locked inside Nyx's proprietary layer.

A hacker who breaches it finds the district's most valuable dataset: real-time Warrens supply-route intelligence, drop coordinates, and a running list of which Warrens contacts are still answering their comms — priceless, and exactly the kind of theft that ends careers permanently.

The Waystation also hosts one of Nyx's dead-drop relay nodes, feeding intelligence up into the wider Churn network in the Sinks — making it a quiet nerve-node in Nyx's larger operation, not just a local outpost.

#### Plot Hooks

1. The Last Run. Nyx needs a deniable crew to escort a critical supply shipment down past the Drop to a Warrens contact who's gone dangerously quiet — and wants to know why before writing the contact, and the shipment, off as a loss.

2. Ledger's Leverage. Ledger Okonkwo has been secretly selling Warrens intelligence to a Meridian buyer behind Nyx's back, and Quartermaster Solis wants proof before she decides whether to handle it quietly or make an example of him.

3. Supply Line Under Siege. A rival cartel faction is testing whether it can choke off the Waystation's supply route from the plaza side, betting Nyx won't risk open war this deep in the Flats to defend one outpost — and Solis needs to prove them catastrophically wrong.
