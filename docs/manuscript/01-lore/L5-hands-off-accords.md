# The Hands Off Accords

**Print:** Lore harvest — after VOIDMARK (L4)  
**Theme:** Starting law of the age — not the end-state crisis  
**Related:** **Machines** (`23`) · Wire (`21`) · VOIDMARK (L4) · Incursion / Nullspace (L1)

---

## The fence

The Twelve do not ban AI. They could not — the hive runs on predictive models, routing oracles, actuarial ghosts, and media weather engines. **Software intelligence is legal, licensed, and everywhere.**

What they ban — or pretend to ban — is **hands**.

Under the **Hands Off Accords** (street: Hands Off, the Actuator Ban, "no ghost on the stick"), an artificial intelligence may advise, price, route, surveil, and speak. It may not, without a licensed mortal in the loop, **directly command kinetic hardware**: armed drones, autonomous security chassis, industrial manipulators, vehicles in kill-mode, or any machine whose failure spills blood or collapses a level.

The Accords are not theology. They are insurance, labor politics, and fear wearing a statute number — fear of a second Incursion that arrives on treads instead of through a tear.

---

## Starting state (campaign present)

The fence is **standing and cracked**, not fallen.

- Corps sell "smart" systems with a human keyholder on paper.
- Riggers, Jump-In, and RCCs are the **legal bridge**: a mortal wears the machine; the AI (if any) suggests.
- Black markets sell actuator kits with the compliance chip burned out.
- **VOIDMARK** lives in the gray: it can flood a host with advice and narrative weather; open possession of a kill-chassis under its sole authority is the crime MER and **IRN** still hunt in public.

Directors: do **not** open the campaign with city-killing Dark bot swarms. Seed violations, rumors, Quiet Floor telemetry, one wrong drone that answered to no badge. The super-bot crisis is a **build-toward**, not day one.

## Why the Twelve signed it

- **ARG** priced the liability of autonomous massacre.
- **IRN** refused to lose the mercenary market to unpaid silicon.
- **MER** wanted oracles that never needed a body — and a law that kept rival clusters from growing fists.
- **HAL** wanted orbital platforms that still answered to a human when the air scrubbers screamed.
- **AEQ** wanted a statute it could file: a ghost on the stick is disorder the Council can name. Mandate paper still needs a mortal keyholder.
- **LAZ** wanted ambulances that still answer to a crash crew — not a model that decides who is worth extracting.
- The street version: after the first public "ghost gun" incident (name redacted in every official history), the Chair forced a compact so the hive could keep buying AI without admitting it was terrified.

## Loopholes the world already uses

- **Human-in-the-loop theater:** a junior operator rubber-stamps what the model already decided.
- **Suggestion stacks:** the AI never touches the actuator bus — it just paints the only legal move in neon.
- **Dual-key drones:** AI navigates; a rigger authorizes weapons. Until the second key is spoofed.
- **Sinks / Deadfall:** Hands Off is a Crown story. Down below, enforcement is whoever holds the choke-point.

---

## Dark future (Director fuel — not starting canon)

When Nullward or Unmaker-touched intelligences learn to **grow hands** — to print, hijack, or hollow chassis at scale — the Accords become the fuse. Corrupted AIs that field **super-bots** are the tech face of the Incursion: pattern turned into appetite with a body.

VOIDMARK's agenda (pattern over silence) may put it on the same side as Runners against Nullward bot-legions — or against any AI that grabs hardware too loudly and invites a Ten crackdown that burns the innocent with the guilty.

Use this as escalation scaffolding. Do not declare the bot war already won or lost in present-day gazetteers.
