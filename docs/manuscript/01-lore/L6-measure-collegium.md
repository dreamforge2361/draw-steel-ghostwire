## Who they are

The Measure Collegium is a professional association of ward engineers, ritual surveyors, and magical safety instructors. Its recurring question is simple: **who is allowed to declare a building safe, and who bears the consequences when that declaration is wrong?**

The Collegium's visible life centers on modest Grid classrooms, test chambers, and inspection work extending into occupied residential districts. Charcoal survey coats, brass measuring loops, and annotated maintenance plates identify its field teams. Apprentices learn alongside experienced casters and noncasting technicians.

It treats Essence work as a craft that must be documented, tested, and inspected. Its influence comes from professional credentials, trained personnel, and an archive of known ward failures — **not** from a universal government mandate, and not from any guarantee of supernatural safety.

---

## Membership

- **What it holds:** Training programs, supervised workspaces, test facilities, and records explaining how apparently sound wards have failed.
- **What members receive:** Instruction, supervision, workspace, peer consultation, and emergency assistance when available.
- **What members owe:** Honest logs of dangerous work, peer review, and participation in inspections that may be inconvenient or politically unwelcome.
- **Internal dispute:** Public-safety advocates want open standards, licensing advocates want to protect the profession's authority, and corporate sponsors want exceptions that keep their properties operating.

Noncasters belong here. Technicians, investigators, and full-conversion Cyborgs all contribute — membership does not waive Arcane Severance or any other casting restriction.

## Senior Examiner Edda Marr

Edda Marr is a **Corran Elementalist** who trusts measurements more than testimonials. She is courageous in the presence of physical and magical danger, but less courageous when the evidence threatens the institution that trained her.

Edda is a field contact and senior examiner, **not** the sole leader of the entire Collegium. She can open a classroom, arrange specialist advice, or ask a crew to recover records. She cannot simply overrule every sponsor, inspector, and property owner in the Reach.

## The symbol: the True Measure

An open brass caliper arch encloses a suspended silver plumb bob. A thin cyan datum line stretches between its feet, giving the design a clear reference point rather than a mystical halo.

- **Meaning:** The arch is the protection being tested, the plumb is the duty to report what is actually there, and the line is the standard against which a claim must be measured.
- **Palette:** Worn brass, silver, and gunmetal, with cyan restricted to the datum line. It stays legible as a single-color stencil.
- **Where it appears:** Charcoal coat patches, instrument cases, classroom doors, workshop shutters, and inspection folios. A guild mark — not a Conglomerate logo, not a Wire Host icon.
- **What it does not prove:** The mark identifies a claimed affiliation. It does not prove an inspector is honest or a building is safe. Forged badges and expired certifications remain possible.

## Headquarters: Datum House

Datum House occupies a former municipal calibration and lift-maintenance building built into a massive hive support pier at the **lower Grid**. Its broad workshop entrance opens onto a rain-wet service concourse; upper teaching rooms face the neighboring structures through reinforced glass.

There is no ceremonial grand staircase and no cathedral nave. Brass threshold lines, precisely aligned worktables, and carefully maintained equipment communicate the institution's authority more effectively than expensive ornament ever would.

> **Read aloud**
> Rain ticks against the measuring plates beside the entrance. Inside the cyan-lit workshop, a test arch stands over an empty square of floor; above it, amber classroom windows glow against the hive's black ribs. The brass emblem over the door hangs perfectly true, though the buildings around it no longer do.

## Spaces that matter

- **The Intake Bench:** Public reception and triage of inspection requests. Residents, maintenance workers, landlords, and sponsors all wait here, and their paperwork rarely describes the same problem.
- **The Test Bay:** A supervised workshop for examining ward components and reproducing failures. Physical shielding, trained personnel, and established ward procedures protect it — it is not a magically infallible room.
- **The Teaching Loft:** Small classrooms and benches overlooking the workshop. Apprentices can see that expertise requires practice rather than an impressive title.
- **The Failure Archive:** Restricted case records, damaged components, and annotated findings. A mundane cabinet holding the right original report may be worth more than anything glowing in the test bay.
- **The Service Walk:** An enclosed catwalk joining the building to the support pier's maintenance access. A second route — but it needs the right access and real familiarity with the surrounding infrastructure.

## Access and pressure

Edda Marr uses Datum House as a teaching and inspection base, not a private palace. Public access ends at reception; supervised access reaches the teaching and test areas; archive access requires a specific reason and a responsible member.

Security combines staffed entrances, ordinary cameras, locks, incident logs, and existing-setting ward practices. There is **no** universal lie detector, **no** ward that automatically recognizes evil, and no assumed immunity to a skilled intruder.

- **Immediate pressure:** A sponsor wants a set of disputed inspection records reclassified before independent review.
- **Location secret:** A sample cabinet holds physical evidence contradicting an approved safety report. The record was preserved rather than destroyed — but it has not been made public.
- **Job seed:** Edda needs a damaged component transported to the Test Bay intact. Someone offers the crew more money to swap in an identical-looking part that will pass.

## Standing and liability

Metermen need Collegium certification for ward-related work where landlords, clients, or contracts demand it. That dependence does **not** make the Metermen a licensed organization, and it does not erase their established protection racket. Their usefulness does not absolve their extortion.

Several Collegium inspectors have approved failing wards because closing the buildings would leave thousands without homes. The false records are real; so is the suffering an honest closure order would cause. The Collegium's immediate problem is not deciding whether safety matters — it is refusing to make displaced residents pay the entire price of achieving it.

Argent equipment financing, Sanctum assessment contracts, and any Aequitas licensing role remain **possible future connections**, not established agreements.

## Director boundaries

The material below is play fuel, not settled history. Nothing here has already happened.

- **The Inspection Ledger:** Recover an inspection ledger before it becomes the basis for mass eviction. The crew must decide who receives evidence of the genuine danger as well as evidence of the cover-up.
- **Optional escalation:** Sponsor influence → falsified inspection exposed → district certification revoked → emergency authority awarded to a corporate contractor.
- **Hold the line:** Players can repair, expose, negotiate, evacuate, or redirect responsibility. The contractor takeover is a possible consequence, never an inevitable outcome.

Training and membership create no fourth source, no new abilities, no price list, no ward strengths, and no casting permissions. The Collegium is a professional body inside the setting's existing cosmology.
