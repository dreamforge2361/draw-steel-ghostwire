## What this chapter is for

Ossian Reach is the **default home hive** unless the table names another city. This chapter tells the Director how to **run** that hive from the materials already in the book and the module — how to pick a board, open a job, climb or drop a stratum, and cut to the next session without reprinting the Handbook.

Players do not need this chapter to make a runner. Directors need it when the question is “where are we, who calls, and which book do I open?”

If a rule and a gazetteer sentence disagree, **RAW wins** (`docs/raw/`). Gazetteers win for place names, fixtures, and street color.

## The session loop on this hive

Use the three modes in How to Play (`01`). The Reach only names the rooms.

1. **Hook.** Someone who already exists hands the crew a reason: Mama Cassavir at Switchboard, a Grid-warden selling a blind eye, a Nyx cut-out on the water, a Greenline voice out of Cinderhold, a choir that wants a body walked home. If you have no face, the Run Generator’s patron list is the same roster — pick one and stop inventing a twelfth fixer.
2. **Legwork.** Stay on **one stratum** until the plan requires a climb or a drop. Ask: which district is the meet, which district is the target, which grey shaft or licensed spine they will use (`L3` / Handbook Ch. 2). Shop and Availability stay in `08`. Wired recon is Overlay first unless the job is a node (`21`).
3. **The run.** Declare the **primary board** (street, Wire, or both) before the first Power Roll that can raise heat (`04`, `25`). If both boards are live, cut in short beats so the Jacked In runner is not a second session.
4. **Aftermath.** Pay the crew (`08` payout bands). Burn Lifestyle (`26`). Leave leftover Trace Alert, Wanted flags, and burned SINs as **next-hook fuel**, not a lecture. Do not auto-clear Wire heat because the fight ended (`04`, `21`).

One job may span several sessions. The hive does not reset between them.

## Pick the stratum, then the district

**Altitude is class.** You do not “go to the city”; you go to a **rung**. Name the rung out loud so the table knows which doors, cameras, and Lifestyle fiction apply.

| Stratum (Reach names) | Lifestyle feel | Default job temperature |
|---|---|---|
| **Halo / Crown** | Elite doors, orbital light | Corp chess, Congress heat, deniable black ops |
| **Spires** | High | Tower work, licensed violence, pretty lies |
| **Grid** | Middle | Wage-body crime, Metermen corridors, SIN-scans |
| **Flats** | **Low** (Lifestyle 4) | Default start. Watched enough to hurt, dark enough to work |
| **Warrens** | Squatter / Low seam | Grey transit, written-off blocks, Ferryman crossings |
| **Sinks / Deadfall country** | Squatter, Signal-thin | Descent jobs, Quiet Floor pressure, Churn supply |
| **Cinderhold / wastes gate** | Frontier, unowned | Way out of the box; Kestrel “Clean Route” pressure |

**Procedure:**

1. Choose the **stratum** the score touches (one primary, one spill if it goes wrong).
2. Choose **one district or fixture** on that rung (a Flats district, a Grid corridor, Cinderhold’s gate — not “the whole Reach”).
3. Open that Handbook page (and the matching Wired Flats master node if anyone jacks in).
4. Place opposition with Ch 26’s budget + threat shape, then **dress** it with that page’s gangs and corp smell. Do not rebuild the Actor math.

Starting tables live on the **Flats** until a climb is the point of the job. Climbing is the hard direction: SIN-scans, posture-checks, and chrome that reads proud downstairs reads criminal upstairs. Descending is easier and more dangerous — cameras die, local power fills the gap.

## Vertical first, horizontal second

Movement between rungs is a **scene**, not flavor.

- **Licensed spines** — fast, lit, fare on the ledger, checkpointed. Time for exposure.
- **Grey transit** — unlicensed lifts, smuggler shafts, dead conveyors. Quiet for risk (structure, turf, someone else’s lean).
- **Water lanes** (Flats / Slackwater) — barges and skiffs as a highway the lift-stack never sees. Violence often arrives by water.
- **Sinkmouth / down-shafts** — last controlled passage toward the Sinks. Treat as a checkpoint with a person attached (Ferryman, Nyx Warden), not a Hacking puzzle you spoof from the couch.

You do not need new ¥ ladders for this. Fake or burner SINs, route fees, and Cassavir selling a shaft are **services** under `08` / `26`. If a number is not printed, quote it like a Medic restock: once, in ¥ or in a favor, and move.

**Documents and chrome discipline** are part of the climb, not optional color. A Flats-Low SIN does not open a Spire gate. Visible street chrome is a posture-check. The specialist at the door is a Face, a Hacker, or a Scout — a crew with no answer turns around.

## The job board (who pays)

Do not invent a shadow economy. Use the faces the setting already named:

| Patron type | Typical meet | Pays in | Watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mama Cassavir** (Switchboard) | Upper Flats, dead transit exchange | Introductions, clean/dirty cut-outs, grey routes | She sells connections, not people. Burn her trust and you get disconnected |
| **Street gang / level-boss** | Their turf | Cash, passage, a war if you cross the lean | Ninth Ward Kings, Rust Saints, Glass Vipers, Metermen, Skinjobs — L3 sketches + Handbook |
| **Corp cut-out** | Grid or Spire lobby, or a deniable Flats drop | ¥ on the high band, scrip, Restricted windows | You are deniable; Trace Alert and badges are the receipt |
| **Nyx / Churn** | Water, Sinks edge | Contraband routes, barge access, ugly pay | The schedule of the flood is a weapon |
| **Cinderhold / Greenline / Marrow** | Outer gate | Frontier work, ammo, the right to come back | Kestrel’s Clean Route wants the gate civilized |

**Payouts** stay on the provisional bands in `08` (street / district / corp / black-ops). Heat and Trace Alert should skew the band up; a quiet job skews down. Lifestyle burn happens **after** payout (`26`). Do not pay character power in nuyen.

## Opposition, events, and when to leave

Build the fight in Ch 26: budget → threat shape → dress the spine → boards and clocks. Then:

- Match opposition **level** to the crew’s echelon (`24`). Flats street crews are Echelon 1 weather; Crown response is not.
- Convert leftover **T5–T1** Handbook language with the tables in `24` (foes, gear, nodes). Never say “tier” to players.
- Draw a **Reach Event** when you want the city to breathe. Most rows are flavor or RP. Combat rows are rare on purpose.
- **Escape is a win condition.** Extraction clocks belong on Reach jobs. The hive always has another squad if you linger for the last minion.

Named fixtures that punish lingering (Deadfall Nine / the Quiet Floor, a climbing dead-air seal, a flood siren) are **clocks**, not random encounters. If you put the Nine on the table, decide what “a little larger” means this session — one more quiet block, one more dead comm — and stop.

## Downtime has an address

Lifestyle bands are the same vertical you just ran (`26`). A Flats crash is **Low** unless the fiction is a condemned nest (Squatter) or a hardened loft the crew is overpaying for. Paying Middle while living in the Flats is a **safehouse story**, not a math exploit — the Director can charge heat or a lean for a flat that looks too clean.

Medic restock, §Craft, chrome surgery, and Ritual Workings resolve in their own chapters. This chapter only asks: **where** did they sleep, and who can find that door?

## First three nights (optional)

If the table has no campaign yet, do not write a novel. Run this skeleton:

1. **Night one — Flats job.** Cassavir (or the generator) hands a district score on the Flats. One board primary. Pay street or district. Burn Low Lifestyle. Leave one unpaid lean.
2. **Night two — climb or drop.** The same lean forces a Grid SIN-scan **or** a Warrens crossing. One checkpoint scene. One fight or one Wire scrape, not both unless the table is fast.
3. **Night three — named fixture.** Touch one fixture that cannot be a random alley: Switchboard politics, a water-lane problem, Cinderhold’s gate, or a Quiet Floor leak. Escalate heat; do not resolve the Chair or the Incursion.

After that, the crew has turf, a patron, and a reason to care which rung they sleep on. That is enough Reach to print.
