## The core distinction — implants are not Cyborgs

This is the load-bearing line of the whole chapter.

- **Cyber-augmented hero** = a living person (human, elf, ogre, revenant, etc.) whose implants **overlay** their natural chassis. They remain their species, keep their soul and their capacity for magic (though chrome erodes it), and are healed by ordinary means for the flesh parts. **Uses this chapter.**
- **Full-Conversion Cyborg** (*Machina sapiens*, the species) = a **machine-first being** on an artificial chassis that has **replaced** the person — permanently Arcane-Severed, tech-only healing, capped humanity. Uses this chapter with Body Integrity **25** and living Chrome; Frame Modules = later retag stub.
- Pushing Body Integrity to zero does **not** turn a hero into a Cyborg — they flatline/die. Becoming a Cyborg is a character-creation species choice, never a chrome outcome. The two systems rhyme deliberately (both use slots, both trade humanity for power) but never merge.

## Design pillars

1. **Chrome is bought with wealth and paid for in flesh — never in character-power budget.** Implants cost **nuyen** (to purchase and install) and **Body Integrity** (a permanent flesh-limit pool). They cost **no XP or character power.** The Economy chapter's character-power firewall holds absolutely: money and flesh buy chrome; chrome never buys attributes, skills, or class power directly. Balance lives in the Integrity cap and the magic-erosion cost, not in a character-power cost.
2. **Every implant gives a real, situational benefit — and stacking is deliberately throttled.** Chrome should be worth it: a genuine capability (an edge, a new sense, a concealed weapon, a reaction boost). But raw numeric stacking is the enemy of balance, so three throttles apply at once: the **Body Integrity cap**, **body-location slot limits**, and a **no-double-dip rule**. A hero can be impressively chromed; never infinitely chromed.
3. **Flesh is finite and the trade is permanent.** Body Integrity does not regenerate by resting. Once spent on an installed implant it is gone until that implant is surgically removed — a downtime project that returns most, not all, of its Integrity (scar tissue is real). Every implant is a genuine, weighty choice.
4. **Chrome and magic are at war inside the body.** The same soul-stuff that channels the Veil is what chrome carves away. Every point of Body Integrity spent proportionally erodes a magic-user's casting capacity. Cheap, crude chrome cripples a mage; only rare, very expensive soft/bioware-grade implants at high tiers barely touch the soul — so a mage *can* run a little chrome, but only late and only at ruinous cost. This is the living-hero echo of the Cyborg's absolute Arcane Severance.
5. **Chrome can fail you.** Implants are devices in a body in a world full of EMP, hostile deckers, ambient magic, and gunfire. They can be **Suppressed, Damaged, or Destroyed** — reusing the conditions system and Wired biofeedback, not a bespoke ladder. A hero who has replaced their eyes with cyber-optics has a new, exploitable vulnerability.

## Track 1 — Body Integrity (the flesh limit)

**Body Integrity** is a permanent pool representing how much intact, unmodified living flesh-and-soul a hero has. It is the master throttle on chrome.

- **Starting Integrity:** every non-Cyborg character begins with a **full Body Integrity pool of 20** — enough for roughly 3–5 mid-grade implants. Species and Background/Career may nudge the starting value slightly.
- **Spending Integrity:** each installed implant has a Body Integrity cost by grade and location. Installing it permanently reduces available Integrity by that amount. Insufficient remaining Integrity means the implant cannot be installed — the body rejects it.
- **Recovering Integrity:** only by removing an implant (a downtime surgical project). Removal returns **75% of spent Integrity** (round down); the remaining 25% is permanently lost to residual scarring. Body Integrity is never restored by rest, magic, or respites.
- **Integrity 0 (flatline):** if a hero's remaining Body Integrity would drop to zero or below through installation, the surgery fails and can kill or cripple them — a hero simply cannot install past their flesh. A hero reduced toward zero by battle-damage forced replacement flirts with this edge — the more of you gets shot off and replaced, the closer you drift to the Cyborg's abyss without ever crossing into the species.

## Track 2 — Grades: used, standard, and soft (the Integrity/nuyen trade)

Every implant comes in **grades** that trade nuyen against Body Integrity — the central economic choice of chrome, simplified to three clean grades.

| Grade | Nuyen cost | Body Integrity cost | Flavor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Salvage / Used** | Cheapest | Highest Integrity cost (×1.5 of Standard) | Second-hand, black-clinic, ex-corpse chrome. Cheap to buy, brutal on the flesh. The desperate hero's entry point. |
| **Standard / Clinic** | Baseline | Baseline Integrity cost | Factory-grade, properly installed. The default. |
| **Soft / Bioware** | Most expensive (often ruinously so) | Lowest Integrity cost (×0.4 of Standard, round up) | Grown, not bolted — vat-cultured, soul-friendly augmentation. Sips Integrity. The only grade a magic-user can realistically tolerate, and only at high tiers. |

Grade is the knob that makes chrome a money-vs-flesh decision every time, and it is the mechanism behind the mage exception in Pillar 4: soft/bioware's tiny Integrity footprint is what lets a wealthy high-tier caster run a little chrome without gutting their magic.

**Representative per-implant Standard Integrity costs:** Datajack 1 · Cyber-Eyes 2 · Cyber-Ears 2 · Wired Reflexes 6 · Muscle/Bone Lacing 5 · Dermal Plating 4 · Cyberlimb 5/limb · Implant Weapon 1 · Running Gear 3 · Skillwires/Encephalon 4 · Reaction Enhancer 3 · Internal Air/Filtration 2 · Vocal Modulator 1.

## Track 3 — Body-location slots (where the chrome goes)

Implants occupy **body locations**, each with limited capacity — the anti-stacking spine and the hook for called-shots and location damage.

| Location | Typical implants | Slot cap | Slot note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Head / Neural** | Datajacks, skill-wires, encephalon/processors, reflex governors, memory | 3 | Most contested location; neural implants are the most powerful and the most magic-eroding |
| **Eyes / Optics** | Cyber-eyes, optical suites (low-light/thermal/magnification/recording), targeting | 1 | Strong sensory edges, but a discrete destroyable target |
| **Ears / Aural** | Cyber-ears, audio suites (amplification, spatial, dampers, translators) | 1 | Sensory; pairs with optics for perception builds |
| **Torso / Core** | Bone/muscle lacing, dermal plating, internal air/filtration, secondary hearts | 3 | Survivability and raw-body chrome; heaviest Integrity draws |
| **Arms / Hands** | Cyberlimbs, integral weapons, tool-hands, cyber-implant weapons | 2 per arm | Limb replacement; called-shot vulnerable |
| **Legs / Locomotion** | Cyberlegs, hydraulic jacks, running gear | 2 per leg | Movement chrome; limb-damage exposed |
| **Nervous system (wired reflexes)** | Reaction enhancers, wired reflexes, combat governors | 1 | The hardest cap in the game, to prevent reaction-boost stacking |

- Slots are a **hard ceiling independent of Body Integrity** — even a hero with Integrity to spare cannot cram three sets of wired reflexes into one nervous system.
- Some large implants (a full cyberlimb) consume the whole location; small ones (a datajack) share.
- Body-location tracking is what lets combat called-shots and future spells target specific chrome ("shoot out his cyber-eyes").

## Track 4 — Chrome vs. magic (the proportional erosion)

This is the rule that keeps the mage-versus-street-sam identity meaningful.

- Every point of Body Integrity spent on implants proportionally reduces a magic-user's casting capacity (their magic resource pool / effective magic rating).
- **Magic-erosion formula (shared by every caster — Elementalist, Street Priest, Technomancer; no class-specific variants):** casting-resource cap reduced by **1 point per 2 Body Integrity spent** on Standard-grade chrome (round down); **1 per 3** for Soft/Bioware; **1 per 1** for Salvage.
- **Grade is the mage's only escape valve.** Because soft/bioware costs the least Integrity, a wealthy caster can install a small amount with only a minor magic hit. Crude salvage/standard chrome guts a mage almost immediately.
- **Tier gate:** genuinely low-erosion soft implants are high-tier, high-Availability, very expensive items — a mage running chrome is a late-game, rich-character archetype by design, never a starting build.
- This is the living echo of Arcane Severance: the Cyborg has spent all their Integrity (pure chrome) and has zero magic, permanently. A cyber-augmented mage walks the first steps of that same road and pays the same currency — soul for chrome — just never to the bottom.

## Track 5 — Damaging, suppressing, and destroying chrome

Cyber disruption reuses the existing conditions system and Wired biofeedback rather than a bespoke status ladder.

- **Suppressed (temporary offline):** EMP bursts, a decker's hostile action across the Wired, a targeted spell, or a system-disruption effect can knock an implant offline for a duration — the hero loses that implant's benefit and may suffer a **biofeedback jolt** (reusing the Wired biofeedback → Stamina/condition rules). Recovers when the effect ends or the implant reboots.
- **Damaged (degraded):** combat location damage (a called shot to the cyber-eyes), a powerful disruptive spell, or sustained EMP can leave an implant Damaged — it works at a penalty (a bane on its rolls, reduced benefit) until repaired in downtime.
- **Destroyed:** enough damage destroys an implant outright — its benefit is gone and its Body Integrity is **locked out** (the flesh is wrecked around the dead chrome) until a downtime repair-or-replace project restores it. Destroyed neural/nervous chrome can inflict a serious biofeedback wound on destruction.
- Combat's called-shot rules, specific anti-cyber spells ("System Rot," "Chrome Sunder"), and Wired attack programs will name their exact effects in their own passes; this chapter guarantees chrome is a legitimate, targetable vulnerability, not an untouchable stat bonus.

## Track 6 — Acquisition, installation, and removal (the downtime loop)

All chrome work is downtime work, plugging into the Downtime and Economy chapters:

- **Acquire.** Buy the implant with nuyen, gated by Availability (Economy chapter) — high-grade soft/bioware is high-Availability and may itself require an acquisition project through a fixer.
- **Install.** A downtime surgical project at an appropriately equipped safehouse (a chop-shop-grade lifestyle/facility). Installation spends the Body Integrity and occupies the location slots. A botched install (project failure) can cost extra Integrity or inflict a lasting condition — chrome surgery has teeth.
- **Battle-damage replacement.** When a hero loses a limb or eye to 0-Stamina consequences, cyber replacement is the natural fix — restoring function but spending Integrity. The game's built-in "forced chrome" horror pressure.
- **Remove.** A downtime surgical project that frees the location slots and returns most of the spent Body Integrity (residual scarring keeps some — see Track 1: 75% returned, 25% lost).
- **Repair.** Damaged/Destroyed chrome is mended by downtime repair projects — the **Wrench**, **Medic/street-doc**, and **Technomancer** are the natural installers/menders.

## Chrome Packages — off-the-rack loadouts (the fast path)

For players who don't want to itemize. A **Chrome Package** is a pre-vetted, factory-bundled suite of implants sold as one product — legal-by-construction (it always complies with the Body Integrity, slot, and grade limits), installed in a single surgery, and bought with one nuyen payment. Packages are the fast on-ramp to being chromed; itemizing remains the min-maxer's path.

### How packages work

- **One product, one surgery, one payment.** A package bundles several implants into a single downtime install project at a single bundled nuyen price and a single, published total Body Integrity cost and slot footprint.
- **Legal-by-construction.** Every package is pre-designed to fit within a normal hero's Integrity budget and the location slot caps — buying one can never accidentally break the limits.
- **Grade applies to the whole package.** Like any implant, a package comes in Salvage/Standard/Soft grade — the same nuyen-vs-Integrity trade, applied to the bundle.
- **Convenience premium, capability parity (the balance rule).** A package costs slightly more nuyen than buying its contents piecemeal — paying for pre-tuning, guaranteed compatibility, and one clean surgery — but grants no more raw capability than the equivalent itemized build. Casuals buy the bundle; optimizers still itemize for the marginal nuyen savings and finer control.
- **Upgrade path.** You can itemize on top of a package (add individual implants) if Integrity and slots remain, or swap out a package for a better-grade version as a downtime remove-and-replace project (paying the removal scar residual). A package installs and removes as a unit — no partial ownership.

### The non-stacking rule (the anti-loophole)

- **One package per role.** Each package occupies a defined role (Mobility, Sensory/Comms, Durability, Recovery, etc.) and claims that role's typical locations. No two packages of the same role, and packages that overlap the same locations cannot both be worn.
- **Same caps.** A package's Integrity and slots draw from the same pools as hand-picked implants — you can't buy three packages and blow past the limits.
- **No same-effect double-dip.** Two sources of the same benefit (e.g., a Mobility package's speed boost and a standalone Running Gear implant) do not stack — take the better, not the sum.

## Class hooks

- **Operator / Scout** — the natural heavy-chrome archetypes: wired reflexes, muscle/bone lacing, integral weapons, cyber-optics. Class features should let them run chrome efficiently (a small Integrity discount, suppression resistance, or exclusive combat implants).
- **Wrench / Medic / Technomancer** — the installers/menders. Their features should grant edges on install/repair project rolls, reduce botch risk, or unlock rare implants. Medic/street-doc = flesh surgeon, Wrench = hardware mechanic, Technomancer = neural/soft-code specialist.
- **Hacker** — less about having chrome, more about attacking others' chrome across the Wired (suppress/damage hooks).
- **Commander / Face** — lightest chrome footprint (soft social chrome — tailored vocal implants, empathy processors — but wary of the Persona-eroding reputation of visible metal).
- **Casters (Elementalist / Street-Priest / Necromancer)** — strongly discouraged by Track 4. Every caster uses the shared magic-erosion formula; Soft grade only, late and expensive — anything heavier collapses their magic. The deliberate street-sam-vs-mage identity line.
- **Cyborg** — does not use this chapter at all (see core distinction).
