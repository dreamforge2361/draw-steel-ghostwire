# GHOSTWIRE: Society Symbols and Headquarters

Visual and location development • 22 September 2026

Three original symbols and three headquarters illustrations for the Measure Collegium, Wickkeepers, and Ash Survey. Their designs extend the approved faction identities into recognizable places and physical-world marks, keeping GHOSTWIRE's wet charcoal, restrained cyan, worn metal, and amber light.

**Status:** The three societies and their established contacts are canon. The symbol names, headquarters names, layouts, and new location details in this document are newly created designs for review, not yet additional locked canon. The artwork is concept art, not a measured battle map, and does not establish mechanics or supersede Rulebook 0.4.0.

## Measure Collegium

### Symbol: The True Measure

An open brass caliper arch encloses a suspended silver plumb bob. A thin cyan datum line stretches between its feet, giving the design a clear reference point rather than a mystical halo.

- **Meaning:** The arch is the protection being tested, the plumb is the duty to report what is actually there, and the line is the standard against which a claim must be measured.
- **Palette:** Worn brass, silver, and gunmetal, with cyan restricted to the datum line. The design should remain legible as a single-color stencil.
- **Physical uses:** Charcoal coat patches, instrument cases, classroom doors, workshop shutters, and inspection folios. It is a guild mark, not a Conglomerate logo or Wire Host icon.
- **Authentication limit:** The mark identifies a claimed affiliation; it does not prove that an inspector is honest or that a building is safe. Forged badges and expired certifications remain possible.

### Headquarters: Datum House

Datum House occupies a former municipal calibration and lift-maintenance building built into a massive hive support pier at the lower Grid. Its broad workshop entrance opens onto a rain-wet service concourse; upper teaching rooms face the neighboring structures through reinforced glass.

There is no ceremonial grand staircase or cathedral nave. Brass threshold lines, precisely aligned worktables, and carefully maintained equipment communicate the institution's authority more effectively than expensive ornament.

**Read aloud:** Rain ticks against the measuring plates beside the entrance. Inside the cyan-lit workshop, a test arch stands over an empty square of floor; above it, amber classroom windows glow against the hive's black ribs. The brass emblem over the door hangs perfectly true, though the buildings around it no longer do.

### Spaces that matter

- **The Intake Bench:** Public reception and triage of inspection requests. Residents, maintenance workers, landlords, and sponsors all wait here, and their paperwork rarely describes the same problem.
- **The Test Bay:** A supervised workshop for examining ward components and reproducing failures. Physical shielding, trained personnel, and established ward procedures provide protection; it is not a magically infallible room.
- **The Teaching Loft:** Small classrooms and benches overlooking the workshop. Apprentices can see that expertise requires practice rather than simply possessing an impressive title.
- **The Failure Archive:** Restricted case records, damaged components, and annotated findings. A mundane cabinet containing the right original report may be more valuable than anything glowing in the test bay.
- **The Service Walk:** An enclosed catwalk joining the building to the support pier's maintenance access. It provides a second route but requires appropriate access and familiarity with the surrounding infrastructure.

### Access, people, and pressure

Senior Examiner Edda Marr uses Datum House as a teaching and inspection base, not a private palace. Public access ends at reception; supervised access reaches the teaching and test areas, while archive access requires a specific reason and a responsible member.

Security combines staffed entrances, ordinary cameras, locks, incident logs, and existing-setting ward practices. There is no universal lie detector, no ward that automatically recognizes evil, and no assumed immunity to a skilled intruder.

- **Immediate pressure:** A sponsor wants a set of disputed inspection records reclassified before independent review.
- **Location-specific secret:** A sample cabinet contains physical evidence that contradicts an approved safety report. The record has been preserved rather than destroyed, but it has not been made public.
- **Job seed:** Edda needs a damaged component transported to the Test Bay intact. Someone offers the crew more money to replace it with an identical-looking part that will pass.
- **Connection to the wider city:** Metermen crews arrive seeking inspections that will keep their corridors operating. Their usefulness does not absolve their extortion.

## Wickkeepers

### Symbol: The Sheltered Wick

A small amber flame sits above a dark wick and chipped lamp bowl, protected by two separated hood-like strokes. The roughened bowl and imperfect symmetry make it feel maintained by people rather than issued by a corporate identity office.

- **Meaning:** Shelter does not create the flame, own it, or promise that it will never go out. It simply keeps the weather off long enough for someone to make it through the night.
- **Palette:** Aged amber brass and charcoal. No dazzling white aura, holy halo, corporate cyan rim, or mandatory magical effect.
- **Physical uses:** A small mark inside a door jamb, an apron patch, the reverse of a pantry tag, or a palm-sized plate visible only after approaching a trusted entrance.
- **Safety rule:** No public citywide shelter directory and no large emblem identifying every refuge. The symbol alone is never enough to establish trust; recognition should be personal and revocable.

### Local headquarters: The Last Kettle

The Last Kettle is a converted laundromat and night kitchen beneath an old lift concourse in the Flats. It is Tavi Sorn's local coordination hub and one useful home for the network, not the Wickkeepers' global headquarters, central church, or authority over other cells.

Its public work is ordinary and visible: hot food, washing, dry clothes, and a place to sit briefly out of the rain. Private assistance is arranged through people rather than displayed on a front-door menu.

**Read aloud:** Amber steam clouds the kitchen window while old washing drums turn behind the next pane. A dry bench fits beneath a patched awning, and someone has set clean cups beside a kettle too battered to belong in a licensed café. The little lamp mark is inside the doorway, where you would only notice it after deciding to step in.

### Spaces that matter

- **The Night Window:** A public serving counter and dry waiting bench. No testimony, conversion, or confession is required to receive a meal.
- **The Wash Room:** Laundry machines, drying racks, repair supplies, and the everyday excuse for coming and going. It is practical cover, not proof that every customer knows about the network.
- **The Back Table:** A small room for private conversations and short-term coordination. It holds current needs, not a master list of every Wickkeeper in the city.
- **The Upstairs Rooms:** A limited number of temporary beds behind plain curtains and ordinary doors. Capacity is finite, and someone must decide what happens when every bed is occupied.
- **The Service Passage:** A rear delivery route leading into the local maintenance circulation. It offers another way out but is neither secret to everyone nor guaranteed clear.

### Access, people, and pressure

Tavi Sorn coordinates this site through persuasion, work, and local trust. She cannot order distant groups to accept a guest, and the Last Kettle does not replace the scattered faithful or Choirmother's established community.

Safety comes from watchful volunteers, discreet introductions, shutters, ordinary locks, limited records, and contingency plans. The building is not a fortress, and safety never depends on pretending that a chalk symbol can protect everyone.

- **Immediate pressure:** A growing queue competes with shrinking fuel and food stocks. Help offered on generous terms arrives with invasive conditions.
- **Location-specific secret:** A restricted aid agreement grants more access to delivery and recipient records than the local volunteers understood. If used, this makes the Last Kettle the shelter implicated in the society's established supply compromise; do not duplicate that plot automatically across every refuge.
- **Job seed:** Bring back a missing delivery without exposing the people waiting for it. The missing driver is hiding because the cargo paperwork would identify a protected witness.
- **Connection to the wider city:** Fixers may refer people here through trusted contacts. Mama's ownership, Brother Ash's membership, and any permanent gang agreement are not implied by the location.

## Ash Survey

### Symbol: The Held Fault

A straight survey stake cuts down through fractured gray ground bands, ending in an amber point. A small cyan instrument light at its head makes the design legible as observation and measurement rather than a weapon or occult execution mark.

- **Meaning:** The break is real, the observer has not looked away, and the record must remain straight even when the ground does not.
- **Palette:** Slate, ash-silver, charcoal, oxidized amber, and a single cyan witness light. Do not substitute real-world radiation, biohazard, or emergency-service insignia.
- **Physical uses:** Sample cases, weather shells, field carriers, garage shutters, and portable observation stations.
- **Safety rule:** The society's symbol does not mean “decontaminated” or “safe to enter.” Hazard status requires separate, current information; the mark merely identifies who recorded or handled something.

### Field headquarters: The Cinder Yard

The Cinder Yard is a repurposed freight-transfer garage at the industrial Flats/Sinks threshold. Retaining walls, drainage grates, a gantry crane, and a narrow overlook make it feel like working infrastructure that has been adapted to a more dangerous profession.

Its location supports expeditions into damaged hive levels and onward routes without placing a comfortable research campus inside an active breach. The name does not make it part of Cinderhold; the exact atlas site remains to be selected.

**Read aloud:** Amber lamps catch rain running through the yard's grates. A carrier waits beneath the gantry while a narrow wash-down bay hisses beside it; beyond the rail, abandoned freight levels fall away into darkness. On the shutter, a straight instrument stake crosses fractured ground and ends in one stubborn amber point.

### Spaces that matter

- **The Dirty Return:** A separate arrival lane for exposed teams and equipment. Check-in, isolation, and wash-down precede movement into other spaces, with established-setting precautions rather than a miraculous cleansing arch.
- **The Sample Lock:** Controlled temporary storage and documented transfers. The main research archive is deliberately kept separate from unassessed material.
- **The Clean Bench:** Analysis, equipment calibration, report preparation, and repair after clearance. Technicians can identify uncertainty without pretending every instrument can explain a supernatural event.
- **The Gantry Bay:** Maintenance and loading for utility carriers, drones, winches, and field gear. This is not an armory for a private army.
- **The Dispatch Loft:** Route planning, radio work, and extraction coordination with a clear view over the yard. It preserves field testimony as well as practical operating records.

### Access, people, and pressure

Daska Venn coordinates departures and returns from here. She remains a full-conversion Cyborg who cannot cast, using organization, machines, and extraction discipline to protect colleagues who can.

Dirty and clean routes are deliberately separated, and visitors do not simply stroll into sample storage. Mechanical segregation, controlled access, trained handling, and relevant ward procedures reduce risks without granting automatic resistance, curing contamination, or bypassing RAW.

- **Immediate pressure:** A sponsor wants an original sample returned before the Survey finishes a comparison test.
- **Location-specific secret:** The Dispatch Loft holds evidence that the best emergency exit crosses a site never fully cleared. This operationalizes the society's existing corridor dilemma without determining its exact route.
- **Job seed:** A returning team's carrier is approaching with conflicting passenger counts and an intact sample seal. The crew must establish what happened without assuming the discrepancy proves possession.
- **Connection to the wider city:** Rust Saints can provide heavy labor at the yard under negotiated terms. The site does not establish new standing contracts with Verdant, Kestrel, or Lazarus.

## Using all three in one session

A disputed inspection begins at Datum House, displaced residents seek help at the Last Kettle, and recovered evidence goes to the Cinder Yard. The crew moves among three recognizable places while the same incident changes meaning at each stop.

The Collegium wants a defensible report; the Wickkeepers need people fed and housed tonight; the Survey wants the original evidence preserved before anyone declares the site clean. Players can build trust with all three without being required to agree with every demand.
