# GHOSTWIRE: Magical Societies

Canon supplement to Rulebook 0.4.0 • Approved 22 September 2026

The Measure Collegium, Wickkeepers, and Ash Survey are established GHOSTWIRE organizations. They occupy the space between corporate power and individual survival: institutions that teach, protect, investigate, compromise, and give runners people to answer to besides their employers.

**Status: LOCKED.** Michael approved these three societies by name on 22 September 2026. Their profiles and named contacts below are canon; the separately labeled adventure hooks and escalation tracks are options for future play, not events that have already occurred.

## Shared canon boundaries

These organizations can disagree about doctrine, evidence, policy, and responsibility without introducing a new supernatural source. Their members remain people living within GHOSTWIRE's established cosmology, not exceptions to it.

- **One source:** Essence, Conviction, and Resonance remain different approaches to the Word. Training and membership do not create a fourth source or a third cosmic power.
- **Faith is personal:** No society can certify holiness, sell Conviction, or appoint an official hierarchy of the true Light. The Wickkeepers provide practical support, not a replacement church.
- **Noncasters belong:** Technicians, investigators, medics, organizers, and full-conversion Cyborgs can contribute. Membership does not waive Arcane Severance or any other casting restriction.
- **Costs remain real:** Inspection, instruction, research, and charitable aid do not remove the Veil's Price or guarantee that a dangerous site can be cleansed.
- **Membership can be worthwhile:** These three organizations are not secretly unified cult fronts. Their conflicts arise from sincere duties, scarcity, institutional self-interest, mistakes, and outside pressure.

## The Measure Collegium

### Identity and place in the Reach

The Measure Collegium is a professional association of ward engineers, ritual surveyors, and magical safety instructors. Its recurring question is simple: who is allowed to declare a building safe, and who bears the consequences when that declaration is wrong?

The Collegium's visible life centers on modest Grid classrooms, test chambers, and inspection work extending into occupied residential districts. Charcoal survey coats, brass measuring loops, and annotated maintenance plates identify its field teams. Apprentices learn alongside experienced casters and noncasting technicians.

### Work, membership, and authority

The Collegium treats Essence work as a craft that must be documented, tested, and inspected. Its influence comes from professional credentials, trained personnel, and an archive of known ward failures; its accreditation is not a universal government mandate or a guarantee of supernatural safety.

- **What it holds:** Training programs, supervised workspaces, test facilities, and records explaining how apparently sound wards have failed.
- **What members receive:** Instruction, supervision, workspace, peer consultation, and emergency assistance when available.
- **What members owe:** Honest logs of dangerous work, peer review, and participation in inspections that may be inconvenient or politically unwelcome.
- **Internal dispute:** Public-safety advocates want open standards, licensing advocates want to protect the profession's authority, and corporate sponsors want exceptions that keep their properties operating.

### Senior Examiner Edda Marr

Edda Marr is a Corran Elementalist who trusts measurements more than testimonials. She is courageous in the presence of physical and magical danger, but less courageous when the evidence threatens the institution that trained her.

Edda is a field contact and senior examiner, not the sole leader of the entire Collegium. She can open a classroom, arrange specialist advice, or ask a crew to recover records, but she cannot simply overrule every sponsor, inspector, and property owner in the Reach.

### Relationships and current liability

Metermen need Collegium certification for ward-related work where landlords, clients, or contracts demand it. That dependence does not turn the Metermen into a licensed organization or erase their established protection racket.

Several Collegium inspectors have approved failing wards because closing the buildings would leave thousands without homes. The false records are real; so is the suffering an honest closure order could cause. The organization's immediate problem is not deciding whether safety matters, but refusing to make displaced residents pay the entire price of achieving it.

Argent equipment financing, Sanctum assessment contracts, and any Aequitas licensing role remain possible future connections rather than newly established agreements.

### Adventure material, not predetermined history

- **The Inspection Ledger:** Recover an inspection ledger before it becomes the basis for mass eviction. The crew must decide who receives evidence of the genuine danger as well as evidence of the cover-up.
- **Optional escalation:** Sponsor influence → falsified inspection exposed → district certification revoked → emergency authority awarded to a corporate contractor.
- **Director boundary:** Players can repair, expose, negotiate, evacuate, or redirect responsibility. The contractor takeover is a possible consequence, not an inevitable canonical outcome.

## The Wickkeepers

### Identity and place in the Reach

The Wickkeepers are a decentralized mutual-aid network providing safe rooms, meals, transport, and witness routes for people sanctioned institutions refuse or endanger. Their promise is practical: “Keep someone else here until morning.”

A chipped amber lamp behind a counter can mark a local point of contact. Shelter information is kept offline, contacts are personal, and neighbors do most of the work. Casters are a minority, not a ruling caste within the network.

### Aid without a new church

The Wickkeepers support genuine faithful people without claiming to speak for the Light. No central authority can declare a member holy, grant Conviction, or command every local group.

- **What they hold:** Trusted doors, small stores of supplies, local knowledge, and people willing to take an inconvenient shift.
- **What members receive:** Requests for shelter, introductions, food, transport, or help moving vulnerable people are answered as resources allow.
- **What members owe:** Time, discretion, practical help, and a willingness to aid people who cannot repay them.
- **Internal dispute:** Some local groups want public legitimacy to protect their shelters; others fear registration would become a list of future disappearances.
- **Religious boundary:** The network is not identical to the hidden remnant and does not represent all Street-Priests. Cooperation is personal and voluntary.

### Tavi Sorn

Tavi Sorn is a Pure Human kitchen organizer with no casting ability. Her strengths are remembering names, organizing scarce resources, and recognizing when official help will make a frightened person's situation worse.

Tavi coordinates local work rather than commanding the whole network. She can ask for volunteers and negotiate access, but a request from her does not obligate every Wickkeeper cell in the Reach.

### Relationships and current liability

The Wickkeepers' work puts them in the same neighborhoods as fixers, territorial gangs, unlicensed healers, and the Concord's charitable operations. They must negotiate practical access without surrendering the people they shelter.

One shelter accepted Concord supplies during a shortage. Its volunteers do not know how much information the aid agreement exposes. The shelter's compromise does not make the whole network a Concord front.

Choirmother remains a separate established character with her existing story, not the Wickkeepers' founder or secret commander. Referrals through Mama and personal connections to Brother Ash remain available campaign connections, not automatic membership or new biography.

### Adventure material, not predetermined history

- **Before Protective Relocation:** Move an unlicensed healer and several patients before officials collect them for “protective relocation.” The healer's work is genuine, but an embarrassing lie complicates public defense of them.
- **Optional escalation:** Resource shortage → conditional charity accepted → shelter names exposed → residents targeted for removal.
- **Director boundary:** Aid can be genuinely helpful without concealing a trap. Do not make every safe room another false refuge.

## The Ash Survey

### Identity and place in the Reach

The Ash Survey is a field society that maps thin places, investigates contamination, and checks whether promised remediation actually occurred. Its people work where corporate reassurance, local necessity, and physical evidence fail to agree.

Teams wear patched weather gear and carry dull mineral sample cases, portable instruments, and practical extraction equipment. Casters work beside medics, riggers, witnesses, and demolition crews. Their operating ground includes damaged hive sites, the Flats/Sinks boundary, and hazardous routes into the frontier.

### Evidence and responsibility

“A clean report is not a clean site” is the Survey's governing maxim. Its authority comes from field experience and independently preserved findings, not from permission to override all civic or corporate law.

- **What it holds:** An independent incident archive, samples, observations, and practical knowledge of failed containment.
- **What members receive:** Reconnaissance support, equipment, extraction planning, and colleagues who understand the risks.
- **What members owe:** Uncensored findings and willingness to take difficult assignments, sometimes returning to sites they barely escaped.
- **Internal dispute:** Quarantine advocates demand immediate closures. Neighborhood advocates insist that sealing a site without moving its residents is abandonment.
- **Limits:** Survey methods do not guarantee that every threat can be detected or cleansed, and reports need not contain a complete explanation of the Incursion.

### Field Coordinator Daska Venn

Daska Venn is a full-conversion Cyborg field coordinator. She cannot cast; she runs logistics and extraction, protects the team's living members, and refuses to let sponsors rewrite the casualties.

Daska is a coordinator, not proof of a magical exception for Cyborgs. Her membership demonstrates that expertise, courage, and responsibility matter to the Survey even when someone cannot wield magic.

### Relationships and current liability

The Rust Saints provide heavy labor for Survey work. Their established kinship loyalties and hard terms still apply: cooperation on a job is not institutional ownership or unconditional allegiance.

The Survey's best escape corridor crosses a site that was never fully cleared. Closing the route protects future travelers but strands people who currently depend on it. Leaving it open without warning makes the Survey complicit in the kind of concealment it condemns.

Verdant's frontier interests and Kestrel's expanding routes can bring either corporation into contact with Survey findings, but no new standing corporate partnership is established here. A Lazarus extraction contract remains a future option, not an existing agreement.

### Adventure material, not predetermined history

- **The Original Samples:** Escort a team into a supposedly remediated building and recover the original samples. The employer paid for testing, not necessarily for the answer.
- **Optional escalation:** Sponsor suppresses sample → residents report symptoms → site declared inaccessible → contaminated property quietly changes hands.
- **Director boundary:** Preserve choices between warning, evacuation, repair, disclosure, and negotiation. A contaminated property is not automatically a portal to the campaign's final confrontation.

## Using the three together

These societies have complementary skills and competing duties. The Collegium asks whether the protection meets a defensible standard, the Wickkeepers ask where the residents will sleep tonight, and the Ash Survey asks whether the danger has actually been understood.

They can all be right about part of the same crisis. No fixed alliance, enmity, shared headquarters, or single chain of command is required for that tension to produce play.

### Optional opening situation: The Price of a Safe Night

An occupied Flats block has failing wards and residents who cannot afford to leave. A missing technician's records may explain why the installed protection does not match the certified work.

Use the three organizations as competing sources of help, evidence, and pressure. The crew may save the building, arrange an evacuation, expose fraud, or force a responsible party to fund repairs. Do not assume an unapproved faction such as the Closed Hand is behind the failure.
